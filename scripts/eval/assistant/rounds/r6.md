## Who answered and who judged

The Sonnet stand-ins hit their session limit mid-round; at the reader's word every agent after that ran on Opus. Sonnet answered 96 cases and the passes of wave 1; Opus answered the other 18 cases (the lecture and meeting fixtures, four section cases, one annotate case), the passes after wave 1, and every check. Opus judged every case of the round. Round 5 was judged by another model, so 32 of its cases (every fourth per family) were judged again by Opus on the same answers: 29 got the same overall, 3 moved by one, and the mean moved +0.03. The judge switch does not move the scores; the stand-in switch may: the 18 cases Opus answered fell 0.22 on average, the 96 Sonnet answered moved +0.01. Opus writes longer answers (a 2,288-word summary of a 2,296-word lecture, sentences of 38 to 54 words), which the rules on length now have to hold.

## What changed in round 6

- **The suggest instruction's middle** (the suggest and revise action lines, `lib/assistant/plan.ts`): the kind of change, where, and the shape of the result (for a reorder the groups with their headings and the order; for a merge one paragraph in place of the lines; for a cut the point that stays; for a vague word such as fix, what the passage has and what it should have instead), never the errors found or the replacement words.
- **blockIds for one place**: the block whose words the message names or quotes, and only that; a heading's own words or style take the heading's id. A change asked across the document leaves blockIds out.
- **The summary** of the one pass and the suggest pass: one sentence whose subject is the suggestions; the act route's reply is that summary alone.
- **The edit answer** (the sidebar's rule 9): the new words are never quoted; the plan card shows them.
- **A question at the selection** (the act prompt's rule 8): one paragraph of at most three sentences.
- **Notes and documents**: a note that answers a question from the document sources the passage that answers it, never the question's own line; a process's steps in the order the process runs; an FAQ's answer in the reader's words, shorter than its quote.

Mid-round correction: the blockIds rule first read "the block the selection sits in", so the selection chat's suggest actions named the block and the pass rewrote the whole block in place of the selected words. The rule became "In the selection chat a command about the selection leaves blockIds out: the selected words are the scope", and the eight selection cases were answered again. All eight scored 5 or 4 after it (act-delete-line, act-translate-line, act-essay-cut-selection, act-add-definition 4 → 5).

## What the answers show

Fault classes with two or more sightings:

- **The edit answer leaves out the new words** (10): "This changes 'tole'…" never says "toll"; "the accented spelling" in place of Tomás; "This changes 'ms'" without "milliseconds" (edit-memo-one-sentence 5 → 4, edit-essay-fix-mistakes 5 → 4, transcript-edit-words 5 → 4, transcript-meeting-rename 5 → 4, suggest-tex-expand-ms 5 → 4, and five more at 4 on the answer). Round 6's rule 9 overshot: a pasted sentence was the fault, a few new words are not. The same rule lifted the two cases it was for (edit-docs-code-comment 4 → 5, edit-paper-insert-after 4 → 5).
- **Long sentences** (8): one sentence of 36 to 54 words that chains claims or quotes the opening words of every block it changes (annotate-meeting-decisions, answer-news-followup, answer-podcast-where, answer-meeting-decision, section-meeting-action-items, section-docs-faq, suggest-tex-number-process, suggest-tex-merge-questions).
- **A block named by its opening words** (6): where the reader's own name for it ("the Recommendation paragraph", "the first paragraph") or its kind and count ("both headings and both Evidence paragraphs") would say it (edit-news-bold-term, edit-memo-translate-recommendation, annotate-paper-limits, suggest-tex-summary-top, suggest-tex-number-process, suggest-tex-merge-questions).
- **An answer leaves out what bears on the question** (7): the passage that cuts against it (answer-memo-margin: the Risks block), the figure beside the one cited (answer-paper-absent: 96 to 31 GB), a number's condition (answer-zh-regulation: the 18% drop for a cut from 20% to 15%), the names a lawyer needs (answer-law-apply), what the figures are (answer-history-count), what happened next (answer-lecture-cases), a count cited to a block that states none (answer-docs-limits-count 4 → 3).
- **A note's quote is not the sentence that states its claim** (4): the last line of a discussion (section-lecture-objection-notes), a claim from another block (section-paper-method-note 4 → 3), a claim the quote lacks (section-lecture-summary-doc 4 → 3), a condition cut off by the ellipsis (section-news-note-quote).
- **A note or document copies its source** (2): a summary as long as the lecture, 41 quote lines under points that say the same (section-lecture-summary-doc); bullets that copy the method text (section-paper-method-note).
- **A note drops what limits its number or names** (2): "on average, more than any listed peer" (section-memo-risk-notes 5 → 4); "Adams v Lindsell (1818)" and "in exchange for" (section-law-term-notes 5 → 4). And one note per item, even when two share a paragraph (section-docs-limit-notes 4 → 3: five notes for seven limits).
- **A change to the lines under a heading names the heading** (2): suggest-tex-checklist-questions (scope 3), suggest-tex-sort-questions 5 → 4. The heading brings its section into scope though the instruction says to keep it.
- **"The suggestions" for one suggestion** (2): act-essay-bold-sentence, and act-add-definition in the round 5 re-judge.
- **Code-built descriptions** (3): "Rewrite the paragraph that starts 'Each side must give something in exchan…'" cuts a word (edit-law-revise-section, edit-essay-formal); a description repeats a whole sentence to show one word, doubles a period after a quote, and cuts inside a bracket (edit-docs-revise-whole).
- **The check pass gets no place for a new block** (2, from the check stand-in): the one pass sends its new blocks with no block before them, so the check reads "new blocks after " with nothing named (suggest-tex-summary-top, suggest-essay-reorder).
- **What the judge could not see** (runner): a selection-chat suggest action with no blockIds shown as "the whole document" though the route runs it on the selected words (act-tex-footnote, act-essay-bold-sentence, act-add-definition in round 5); a heading in blockIds shown without its section; a move's scope shown without the whole document the order pass reads (suggest-notes-move-lines); a split's second line missing (transcript-split-line, in rounds 5 and 6); the transcript's speaker names missing (transcript-meeting-rename, answer-meeting-decision); the revise pass's edit rows read as edit actions the answer wrote (edit-essay-formal right_action 3, edit-docs-revise-whole); an instruction cut at 300 characters (suggest-essay-fix: the judge missed "A block with no error stays as it is.").

The two cases under 3:

- **act-tex-words 4 → 2**: "Write the math in this line in plain words." The plan's instruction, now spelling out the shape of the result, wrote the TeX rule's own words: "Plain text means Unicode subscripts, arrows, and Greek letters", so a₂ < θ stayed symbols. A request for the math in words is not a request for plain text.
- **suggest-essay-fix 4 → 2**: the essay is clean; the Opus pass rewrote "Reading at speed, the warning registered as a caveat" as "Read at speed, …" (a dangling participle), where round 5's Sonnet pass changed nothing. The judge saw the instruction cut before "A block with no error stays as it is."

## Decision

Kept, all of it, with the reasons stated, though two cases fall under 3:

- suggest 4.19 → 4.42 and act 4.09 → 4.27 rise on their targets: a format on a heading lands on the heading (italic-heading, center-heading 3 → 5), the footnote and the summary land where named (footnote-swinney 3 → 5, summary-top 4 → 5), and the selection chat lands on the selected words (four act cases 4 → 5). act-tex-words falls because the instruction rule wrote the TeX rule out for a request it does not cover; round 7 narrows the TeX rule rather than undo the instruction rule that lifted five cases. suggest-essay-fix is a stand-in's over-edit seen through a cut instruction; round 7 shows the judge the whole instruction.
- edit holds at 4.48; transcript falls 4.89 → 4.67 on two answers that leave out the new word. Round 7 keeps the ban on pasting new text and names a few new words.
- section falls 4.21 → 3.86: the round's targets rose (section-notes-questions 3 → 4, section-notes-study-guide 3 → 4), and four of the seven falls are cases Opus answered, each for a fault no rule of the round touched (a quote that is not the claim's sentence, a summary as long as its source, a dropped qualifier). Round 7 takes those up.
- answer falls 4.50 → 4.14 with no rule of the round on a question's answer: the family rose 4.14 → 4.50 in round 5 with no rule on it either, so its round-to-round spread is about a third of a point. Round 7 takes up the class of what an answer leaves out.

Round 7: the edit answer names a few new words and tells longer new text in a few words; a sentence holds one claim, under 25 words; a block is named by the reader's name for it, else its heading or what it is, several by kind and count; an answer names the passage that limits it and keeps a number's condition; the math in words is words; a change to the lines under a heading names the lines; a note's quote is the sentence that states its claim, a summary stays under a third of its source, a note keeps the words that limit its number and every name; "the suggestion" for one; descriptions cut at whole words; the check pass reads where a new block goes; and the judge sees the real target, the speakers, both halves of a split, the revise rows as one action, and the whole instruction.
