## What changed in round 3

One change per family, each for a fault two or more cases showed in rounds 1 and 2:

- **transcript**: a timed block's tag reads `(TRANSCRIPT 1:05–1:42)`, the times the reader sees on the line, in place of seconds with a decimal (`lib/derive/context.ts` renderBlockLines; the digest's sources and the Ask, Find, and Formalize prompts say the same).
- **scope**: when the document cannot be changed, the answer still gives the new words the message asked for, or offers a highlight or a comment on the passage without proposing it unasked (synthesis rule 8, act rule 10). A new case, scope-shared-mark, asks for bold on a shared document.
- **section**: add_note's content is the passage's point in plain words, its quote the sentence the content restates, every note of a plan the same shape (the add_note action line); one note per X the document names reads every block that states an X, under any heading (synthesis rule 11, act rule 13). A new case, section-docs-limit-notes, asks for a note per limit the rate-limiting page sets.
- **answer**: rule 6 adds no context the question did not ask for, no point made twice, no account of how the answer was found, a lookup in one paragraph.
- **edit**: a description names its block by its opening words (rule 5); the answer names a changed block once, in words the reader knows, with its tag beside them (rule 9); the one pass and the suggest pass change the words that carry a register change and keep the rest of the sentence as printed (their rule 3).

Mid-round correction: rule 9 first said to name a block by its tag alone. A `[block <id>]` tag renders as a ¶ chip that shows none of the block's words (`components/markdown.tsx`), so that sentence hid which paragraph changes; the judges of the first edit batches said so. The rule was rewritten, the judge prompt now says what a tag renders as, and every change case was answered again under the corrected rule. The answer-family and scope cases kept their first answers (the sentence governs change answers only).

## What the answers show

Every mechanical check passes. Fault classes with two or more sightings, by family:

- **suggest**: the suggest instruction for a document-wide fix lists the errors the assistant found and the words that replace them, so the pass changes those two and nothing else (suggest-essay-fix 2: "Change 'Reading' to 'Read' in both"), and the answer of a proofread carries the corrected words (suggest-draft-proofread; round 2's proofread narrowed its blockIds the same way). Third sighting of the class: the instruction names the kind of change, never the errors found or the replacement words, and the answer never pastes the new words.
- **section**: a note per X puts two Xs in one note (section-docs-limit-notes 3: five notes for seven limits); a summary of one part skips a block of it (section-paper-method-note 3: the method's cost paragraph has no bullet); a note that says the document does not answer a question omits the lines that bear on it (section-notes-questions 3); the lecturer is "He" though the lecture gives no gender (section-lecture-objection-notes, section-lecture-summary-doc). The add_note rule did what it said (section-memo-risk-notes 3 → 5, every note the same shape), and the content faults are what is left.
- **answer**: the answer stops before the document's own forward figure or the block that would undo the result (answer-memo-margin, answer-paper-absent), does not rule out the near-matches of a count (answer-history-count), or tags a computed count with a block that does not state it (answer-docs-limits-count). All 4s; "complete" is the worst criterion in three of them.
- **edit**: the answer says nothing of what the new words say (edit-paper-insert-after), nothing of the block a move leaves pointing at nothing (edit-news-move-dissent), nothing of grammar on a spelling-and-grammar ask (edit-essay-fix-mistakes), or names one thing twice (edit-docs-heading-level: "level one" and "h1"). The plans are exact in every one. A rewrite drops a qualifier ("in the quarter", "a year earlier": edit-memo-shorten-two) or loosens a definition it glosses (edit-law-revise-section).
- **editDescription**: a one-word span reads "Change “…I…” to “…this essay…”." twice, and an edit of three spans names one (edit-essay-formal 3). The change window from round 2 quotes too little when the span is short.

## Decision

Kept: the timed tags (transcript 4.67 → 5.00, every case 5), the shared-document answer (scope 3.50 → 4.67), the edit rules (4.24 → 4.41, no case under 3 but edit-essay-formal, whose fault is the change window's), the add_note shape (section-memo-risk-notes 3 → 5; the family's mean holds at 3.91 with a new case that scores 3).

Answer rule 6: stays. The family's mean is 4.29, above round 1's 4.21 and round 2's 4.14, with every case at 4 or 5 (answer-lecture-revenue and answer-meeting-actions 5). The worst criterion of the 4s is now "complete" (a figure the question needed, left out) in most of them, and "fit" or "style" in none: the sharper rule stopped the asides without cutting the answers short.

Round 4 adds the act family (the selection chat on a rich-text document: a command's suggestions land at once) and the TeX cases from the reader's screenshot, with the JSON escape repair and the review box; it changes no answer rule, so its scores read as a second measurement of round 3's rules. Round 5 takes the suggest instruction rule (three sightings), the lecturer's gender (two), and the change window's short spans.
