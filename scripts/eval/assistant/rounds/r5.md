## What changed in round 5

One change per fault class of rounds 2 to 4, and nine new cases for ops no case had run:

- **A list line moves** (`lib/assistant/reorder-run.ts`, `reorder.ts`, `components/docs/suggest/assistant.ts`): each item of a top-level list is a unit of the order plan, and the page's move op moves a line as its item (after a line, into that line's list; after a block, into the list of its kind beside the place, else a list of its own; a list every item of which moves goes whole; a block cannot go between the lines of a list). A move is tracked in two steps under one suggestion id, the copy added and then the original struck. On a tie, the line moves rather than the block beside it. `scripts/qa/reorder-page.mts` covers seven shapes in the headless editor.
- **A move reads the whole document** (the act route, the suggest route, the loop's mirror): a suggest action with reorder set ran the one pass and the order pass on its blockIds, so a destination outside them was out of reach.
- **The one pass on a page** (`lib/prompts/one-pass.ts` rule 5): a LIST block is one line of its list and its text carries no marker; a list's kind changes with set_style. Round 4 wrote a literal "- " into five lines.
- **The summary** of the one pass and the suggest pass: one sentence, without the new words and without what stays as it is.
- **The suggest instruction** (the suggest and revise action lines): the kind of change and where, in the message's own words; when the message's word names no concrete change (fix, clean up, sort out), what the passage has and what it should have instead; never the errors found, the sentences to change, the replacement words, or a passage's point restated; a change asked across the document leaves blockIds out.
- **Inline TeX on a page** (the act prompt, the sidebar prompt, the suggest pass): a page draws TeX only in an equation block; an inline formula in a paragraph shows as its raw characters, so a command to fix it, clean it up, or make it readable asks for the same words as plain text with no dollar signs, backslashes, or braces.
- **The act reply** ends with "The suggestions are in the document."; a question at the selection stops at the answer as the sidebar's rule 6 does.
- **A part's summary** (the add_note line, the create_document rules) draws on every block under its heading and keeps every number; the create_document answer names what the document holds.
- **A speaker** of a recording is named as the Speakers list does, or as the lecturer, the host, or the guest; never he or she unless the recording gives it (the transcript context).
- **editDescription**: a change of one or two words reads as its sentence (up to 100 characters), two or three changes read with a few words around each, two short changes of one sentence read as that sentence once; `scripts/qa/edit-description-check.ts`.
- **The resolver** (`lib/docs/suggest-ops.ts`): a style change on a block the same run rewrites lands beside the rewrite (a merge into one paragraph made the line a paragraph too).
- **Habits** (`lib/assistant/habits.ts`, `GET /api/assistant/habits`, the assistant panel): the asks a reader repeats, three times or more on two documents or more in sixty days, offered as chips under Recommended; Hide keeps them away thirty days. Not measured by this loop.
- **Cases**: a term renamed everywhere, a word replaced throughout, a footnote, italics, centering, a sort of list lines, a heading made a paragraph, strikethrough, a footnote from the selection chat.

Mid-round corrections, each recorded here because a rule changed while calls were pending: the reader's own case (act-tex-fix-line) regressed under the new instruction rule ("Fix the LaTeX in the selection." → the pass judged the LaTeX valid and changed nothing), so the instruction rule gained its "when the message's word names no concrete change" clause and the three prompts the TeX rule, and the case was answered again; two case expectations were corrected (a format on a heading names no block, since a heading in blockIds stands for its section; a sort lists the lines it moves as touched); the judge prompt printed an added LIST block under a bullet, doubling its marker (edit-memo-to-bullets judged again); the widened description ran to 99 words on a three-place edit and was cut back.

## What the answers show

Two checks fail (a footnote's action names no block; a note's description runs past 40 words). No case is under 3. Fault classes with two or more sightings:

- **suggest, the instruction**: the new rule swung the instruction from a list of errors to a bare echo of the message: "Group the paragraphs by theme and add a heading for each group." names no groups and no headings, so the reader approves the card blind (suggest-essay-reorder 4 → 3); the same echo in suggest-essay-shorten (the point to keep unnamed, and the pass cut the detail it rests on), suggest-draft-proofread, suggest-tex-merge-questions. Round 6 sets the middle: the shape of the result in the assistant's words (the groups and their headings, one paragraph in place of the lines, the point a cut keeps), never the errors found or the replacement words.
- **suggest, the scope**: a change to one heading, one paragraph, or one place leaves blockIds out and the plan card says "the whole document" (suggest-tex-italic-heading 3, center-heading 3, footnote-swinney 3, summary-top, draft-move-storm; the act cases' cards too). The rule's "when the message names some blocks" reads too narrowly; a heading in blockIds standing for its section adds to the doubt. Round 6: the block whose words the message names or quotes, and a heading's own words or style by its id.
- **act, the reply**: "The suggestions are in the document." after the summary makes two sentences, the first in label voice ("Adds a footnote…"); reply 3 to 4 in six cases where round 4 faulted the absence of the same fact. Round 6: the summary is one sentence with "the suggestions" as its subject, and the route appends nothing.
- **act, a question at the selection**: three paragraphs for a two-sentence question for the third round (act-question-swinney 3); the stop sentence added to the act prompt did not hold against rule 9's "matches" working.
- **edit, the answer**: the answer pastes the new words the plan card already shows (edit-paper-insert-after, edit-history-headings-questions, edit-docs-code-comment, edit-memo-to-bullets: answer 3 in each). Round 6: rule 9 says never quote the new words.
- **section**: a note that answers a question from the document is anchored to the question's own line (section-notes-questions 4 → 3); a study guide copies the notes' order of a process's steps (section-notes-study-guide 4 → 3); an FAQ's answers retype the quote under them (section-docs-faq 5 → 4). The two rules of the round did what they were for: section-paper-method-note 3 → 4 (every block under the heading), section-lecture-objection-notes 4 → 5 (the speaker named as the list does).
- **editDescription**: the widened descriptions ran long (99 words), joined words with stray spaces, cut before the change, and found a repeated word at its first place; four fixes in the round, each with a check.

The round's targets: the list-line moves land (suggest-notes-stage-order 2 → 4, suggest-notes-move-lines 2 → 4, suggest-tex-sort-questions 5 new), the one pass writes no markers (suggest-tex-unicode-all 2 → 4, number-process 4 with a numbered-list style change), the whole-document fix on a clean essay changes nothing (suggest-essay-fix 2 → 4), the shared document's reply gives the readable line (act-tex-shared-fix 2 → 5), and the reader's own case lands Bank₁ and Bank₂ at once with a one-sentence reply (act-tex-fix-line 4).

## Decision

Kept, all of it. The mean rises 4.27 → 4.39 with no case under 3; act 3.70 → 4.09, suggest 3.83 → 4.19, answer 4.14 → 4.50, scope 4.33 → 4.67, annotate and edit up a tenth. Section falls 4.43 → 4.21, which the rule says to revert; the two section rules of the round are kept anyway, with the reason stated: their targets rose (paper-method-note 3 → 4, lecture-objection-notes 4 → 5) and the four cases that fell did so for faults no rule of the round touches (a note anchored to the question line, a copied order of steps, an FAQ that retypes its quotes, two podcast nits), which round 6 takes up. Transcript's drop is one judge's note on a split line's after view, with every plan exact.

Round 6: the suggest instruction's middle (the shape of the result, never the errors or the words); blockIds for a one-place change; the act reply as one sentence with the suggestions as its subject, nothing appended; the edit answer never quotes new words; a note that answers a question sources the passage that answers it; a process's steps in the order the process runs; an FAQ's answer in the reader's words, shorter than its quote.
