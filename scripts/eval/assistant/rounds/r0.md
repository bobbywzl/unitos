## What the answers show

Plans are right almost everywhere: 54 of 56 cases pass every mechanical check (the right action on the right block, exact quotes, nothing the message did not name touched). Every overall under 4 but two comes from the answer that accompanies a correct plan, and it is one fault class, seen in fourteen cases across edit, annotate, section, confirm, and transcript: the answer runs past the one or two sentences that name the change, into the change's consequences, the document's content around it, other blocks' figures, the new words themselves (which the plan card shows), or advice. The two failures are structural: a message that asks for a new document (the podcast summary, the study guide) is refused or turned into notes, because no action makes a document.

Single cases, hypotheses until a second shows: a follow-up attributes half of a speaker's rebuttal to "the article"; a count question lists some caps the document states but not all; a document that cannot be changed gets its reason and nothing it could do instead; a comment repeats the paragraph's own words; a shorten request's instruction names what to keep but not what to cut; a reorder instruction names groups by first words, not themes; the edits a one pass makes carry the command as every description.

Observed in the plan card, outside the criteria: a before-and-after preview clipped at the start shows two identical strings when the change sits later in the block.

## Round 1's change

Rule 9 of the action rules (lib/prompts/synthesis.ts): the answer to a message that asks for a change is one or two sentences that name what changes and where, nothing else; a confirmation gets one sentence. Round 2: the create_document action.
