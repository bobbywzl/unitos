# Northwind Logistics: Q2 Review Memo

This memo reviews Northwind Logistics' second quarter for the investment committee and answers one question: is the margin recovery real, or is it a one-quarter effect of fuel prices?

## The numbers

Revenue was 1.84 billion dollars, up 6 percent from a year earlier. Operating margin rose to 9.1 percent from 6.3 percent. Free cash flow was 142 million dollars against a loss of 18 million a year earlier. Net debt fell to 2.1 times EBITDA from 2.9 times.

Management attributes the margin gain to three things: a 14 percent cut in the trucking fleet, the closing of four regional depots, and lower diesel prices. Diesel averaged 3.41 dollars a gallon in the quarter, down from 4.02 a year earlier.

## What is fuel and what is not

Fuel is 11 percent of Northwind's cost base. A 15 percent fall in diesel therefore lowers costs by about 1.7 percent of revenue, all else equal. The margin gain was 2.8 percentage points. So roughly 1.1 points of the gain came from something other than fuel. The fleet cut and the depot closures explain most of that: management put the annualized savings at 88 million dollars, which is 1.2 percent of revenue.

The fuel part will not repeat. Diesel has already risen 9 percent since quarter end. If it holds at current prices, next quarter's margin loses about 1 point from fuel alone.

## Risks

Volume is the risk the numbers hide. Shipments fell 3 percent while revenue rose 6 percent; the difference is price. Northwind raised rates 9 percent on average, more than any listed peer, and two of its five largest customers said on their own earnings calls that they are moving volume to cheaper carriers. Losing either would cost about 4 percent of revenue.

The fleet cut also removes capacity. If volume returns, Northwind will lease trucks at spot rates, which management estimates at 30 percent above the owned-fleet cost per mile.

## Recommendation

The structural part of the margin gain, about 1.1 points, is real and should hold. The fuel part, about 1.7 points, is already reversing. We model next quarter's operating margin at 7.6 percent. At the current share price the stock discounts a margin of 9 percent or more, so we hold and do not add. We would buy below 38 dollars a share, where the price discounts the structural margin alone.
