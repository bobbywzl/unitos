# Alphabet first-quarter 2016 earnings call: the questions on Search ads

This is an excerpt of the question-and-answer part of Alphabet's earnings call for the first quarter of 2016, held on 21 April 2016, put in plain words for study. Ruth Porat is the chief financial officer of Alphabet. Sundar Pichai is the chief executive of Google.

## Prepared remarks on Search

Ruth Porat: Revenue from Google's own sites was 14.3 billion dollars in the quarter, up 20 percent from a year earlier. Growth was led by mobile search, with strong help from YouTube and desktop search. Paid clicks on Google's own sites rose 38 percent from a year earlier. Cost per click on those sites fell 12 percent, mostly because of the mix: YouTube ads and mobile clicks earn less per click than desktop search clicks.

Sundar Pichai: More than half of all searches now come from mobile devices. That is why we changed the desktop results page in February: text ads no longer appear on the right side of the page, and up to four ads can appear above the results for queries that are highly commercial. The page now looks the same on a desktop as on a phone.

## Question on ad coverage

Justin Post, Bank of America Merrill Lynch: With the right-side ads gone and a fourth ad slot at the top, how do you think about ad coverage from here? Is there room to show ads on more searches, or more ads per search, on desktop and on mobile?

Sundar Pichai: We do not have a target for ad coverage. We start from what is useful to the user, and for many commercial queries an ad is a very good answer. There are many changes we can make to the ad experience, and we approach each of them in a disciplined way, as people use mobile more and their behavior shifts. The desktop change came after a long period of testing, and the fourth ad shows only on a small share of queries.

Ruth Porat: I would add that we look at these changes over a long horizon. We are not managing to a number of ads per page in a quarter.

## Question on mobile pricing

Mark Mahaney, RBC Capital Markets: Cost per click keeps falling while paid clicks grow fast. When does the price of a mobile click close the gap with desktop?

Sundar Pichai: Mobile ads work better each quarter as formats and measurement improve, and advertisers bid more as they see the results. We do not forecast when the gap closes. We care more that the ad is useful on the device the person is holding.

## Question on the effect of the desktop change

Heather Bellini, Goldman Sachs: Did removing the right-side ads change revenue in the quarter?

Ruth Porat: The effect was small in the quarter and within what we expected from the tests. Fewer ads show on a desktop page than before, and the ads that remain get more attention.
