# Central bank holds rates, signals one cut by year end

The central bank kept its policy rate at 5.25 percent on Thursday, the fourth hold in a row, and said one cut before the end of the year is likely if inflation keeps slowing.

Inflation fell to 3.1 percent in August from 3.4 percent in July. The bank's target is 2 percent. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, stayed at 3.6 percent for the third month.

"We are not there yet," the governor said at the press conference. "Core prices are still rising faster than we can accept. But the direction is right, and if the next two readings confirm it, a cut in December is on the table."

Markets had priced a 40 percent chance of a cut on Thursday. After the statement, futures priced a 78 percent chance of a cut in December and a full percentage point of cuts over the next twelve months.

Two of the nine committee members voted to cut on Thursday. Their dissent, published with the decision, argued that the labor market has already turned: unemployment rose to 4.4 percent from 3.9 percent a year ago, and job openings are down 22 percent from their peak. "Waiting for core inflation to reach 2 percent before easing means easing too late," the dissent said.

The governor rejected that reading. Wage growth of 4.8 percent a year, she said, is not consistent with 2 percent inflation, and services prices, which are driven by wages, rose 5.1 percent over the year.

The decision affects mortgage rates directly. The average 30-year fixed rate was 6.9 percent this week. Lenders said they expect it to fall toward 6.5 percent by December if the bank cuts.

Analysts were divided on the timing. One bank economist said the committee would cut in December and again in March. Another said a December cut depends on the September inflation reading, due on October 12, coming in at 3.0 percent or lower.
