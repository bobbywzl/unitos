## Big picture

- 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2
- happens in chloroplasts, mostly in mesophyll cells of the leaf
- 2 stages, the light-dependent reactions first and then the Calvin cycle
- CO2 comes in through the stomata, water comes up from the roots
- looks like respiration backwards but different enzymes and a different place

## Light-dependent reactions

- where: thylakoid membranes, inside the grana stacks
- chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light, reflects green (that is why leaves look green)
- light excites electrons in photosystem II (PSII)
- PSII absorbs best at 680 nm and PSI at 700 nm, so they are called P680 and P700
- electrons run down the electron transport chain to photosystem I and lose energy on the way
- that energy pumps H+ from the stroma into the thylakoid space, so a gradient builds up
- the H+ gradient is stored energy, like water behind a dam
- PSI re-excites the electrons, then NADP+ picks them up w/ H+ to make NADPH
- cyclic = PSI only, makes ATP but no NADPH ??
- products ATP + NADPH go to the Calvin cycle

## Calvin cycle

- where: stroma
- no light needed directly, but it stops soon after dark b/c the ATP and NADPH run out
- rubisco is the key enzyme, slow, so the leaf needs loads of it
- ATP is made by ATP synthase as H+ flows back out of the thylakoid
- the O2 comes from water, not from CO2
- G3P (3C) is the sugar that comes out, 2 G3P join up to make glucose (6C)
- 6 turns = 1 glucose, uses 18 ATP + 12 NADPH

3 stages in each turn of the cycle:

- regeneration: 5 of every 6 G3P are used w/ ATP to remake RuBP so the cycle can go again
- carbon fixation: rubisco joins CO2 to RuBP (5C), the unstable 6C product splits into 2 x 3PG (3C)
- reduction: ATP and NADPH turn 3PG into G3P

## Factors affecting rate

- 3 main ones: light intensity, CO2 concentration, temperature
- limiting factor = whichever one is in shortest supply, the rate cannot rise until that one does
- light: rate rises w/ intensity, then levels off b/c CO2 or temperature becomes limiting
- colour of light matters: chlorophyll absorbs red and blue, reflects green
- CO2: more CO2 = faster rate up to a plateau, air is only about 0.04% CO2 so it often limits
- temperature: optimum is about 25 degrees, colder than that and the enzymes are slow
- above 35 degrees the enzymes denature and the rate crashes (does not come back)
- does temperature even touch the light stage?? ask Mr K
- water: rarely limiting by itself, but dry leaves close their stomata and less CO2 gets in
- graph shapes: light and CO2 curve up then flatten, temperature rises to the optimum then falls fast

## Practical: pondweed

- count O2 bubbles from pondweed per minute at different lamp distances
- intensity goes as 1 / distance squared, so halving the distance gives 4 x the light
- keep temperature steady w/ a water bath or the lamp heats the water and ruins the test
- bubble counting is rough b/c bubbles vary in size, a gas syringe is better
- repeat 3 times and take the mean
- results table: lamp distance, bubbles per minute, mean

## C3 vs C4 plants

- C3: normal route, rubisco fixes CO2 straight away in the mesophyll cells, first product is 3PG (3C)
- C3 examples: wheat, rice, soybean, most trees
- problem: rubisco also grabs O2 (photorespiration), wastes energy, worse when hot and dry b/c stomata close
- C4: CO2 is fixed first in mesophyll cells by PEP carboxylase, makes a 4C acid
- the 4C acid moves into the bundle sheath cells and releases CO2 there, the Calvin cycle runs in the bundle sheath
- so CO2 stays high around rubisco and there is very little photorespiration
- C4 examples: maize, sugarcane, sorghum
- C4 pays extra ATP to run the pump, so in cool shade C3 does better
- CAM plants (cacti) open their stomata at night, is that on our syllabus??

## Key terms

- stroma = the fluid inside the chloroplast, Calvin cycle happens here
- thylakoid = flat sac, its membrane holds the photosystems
- grana = stacks of thylakoids
- photosystem = chlorophyll + proteins that capture light
- NADPH = electron carrier, made in the light stage
- RuBP = the 5C sugar that CO2 attaches to
- stomata = pores in the leaf where CO2 gets in and water gets out
- bundle sheath = ring of cells around the veins in C4 leaves
- electron transport chain = line of carriers that pass electrons along and pump H+
