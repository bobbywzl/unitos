# Dissect — Unified Notes App for Deep Reading

A notes-centric web app for completely dissecting complex documents (research papers, financial reports, due diligence, consulting reports) with an AI assistant. Notes are the substrate; documents are inputs that attach to notebooks. Every AI feature is one operation: **Anchor → Derivation → Destination**.

---

## 1. Product Principles

1. **Notes outlive documents.** A note can cite many documents; a document can feed many notebooks. The unit of long-term value is the note, not the annotation.
2. **One primitive, many features.** Simplify, salience highlighting, analysis, and extraction-to-notes are all the same pipeline (anchor → LLM derivation → destination) with different prompt templates and destinations. Never build them as separate subsystems. Explain is one template of this pipeline. Questions about a selection go to the assistant (§7), which gathers the passages across the document that match the selection and answers from them; there is no separate Match-it tool.
3. **User approves everything.** All AI output that writes into notes lands as `pending` and requires one-keystroke accept/reject. Nothing enters notes silently.
4. **Provenance is non-negotiable.** Every note line must click back to its source anchor in the original document.
5. **The retrieval test.** A feature only writes to notes if its output is something the user will read again. Comprehension aids (laymanization, explanation) render in the reader and persist as annotations — not as notes in sections.
6. **Context conditions everything.** The reader's background, purpose, and intended application (the background in Settings; stored as `ReaderProfile`) are injected into every prompt, not scoped to one feature. Context is optional and never blocks reading or upload.

## 2. Tech Stack

- **Framework:** Next.js 14+ (App Router, TypeScript, server components where possible)
- **DB:** PostgreSQL + Prisma
- **AI:** GLM 5.3 (Z.ai, `glm-5.3`) behind the reader's tools, the assistant, Stitch's answer pass, Merge with AI, and the contents, and GLM 5.3 Flash (`glm-5.3-flash`) behind every reading but the parse and Extract — SALIENCE, EXTRACT, FIND, the glossary, the skeleton, the gist, the recommended-links scan, Stitch's route and select passes — and Kimi K3 (`kimi-k3`) behind Extract (DISTILL: a question against the whole document, and the quotes copied exactly), all via the Vercel AI SDK's OpenAI-compatible client (`lib/kimi.ts`, the `@ai-sdk/moonshotai` provider), streaming responses, and all through the AI gateway (below): GLM has no direct client, and without the gateway a GLM id resolves to Kimi K3 (`resolveModelId`, `lib/models.ts`). Kimi K3 (Moonshot AI, `kimi-k3`) keeps what GLM 5.3 cannot do: it reads images — a call that carries one (Circle & ask on a figure, a page image, a picture in the assistant, a video frame) goes to `VISION_MODEL` whatever the feature's model. The assistant with Web on runs on GLM 5.3 too (`WEB_SEARCH_MODEL`), searching with Z.ai's Web Search API; the search follows the model that answers, so without the gateway, where a GLM id resolves to Kimi K3, it is Moonshot's web-search formula (§7). The model stays a per-derivation-type config constant, with a reasoning effort beside it (`DERIVATION_EFFORT` in `lib/derive/config.ts`: `high` for the reader's tools, `max` for ANALYZE; Kimi K3 and GLM 5.3 take the same three levels). The parse passes — the URL core, structure, and layout passes — run on Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic, `claude-opus-5-5`) at max effort (`PARSE_MODEL` and `PARSE_EFFORT` in `lib/derive/config.ts`): what the parse gets wrong, every later tool inherits, so the parse gets the strongest model at its most thorough, not the cheapest; "max" is the slowest effort, and the layout pass reads the page's whole HTML against the request's time budget, so a pass that outruns it is skipped and the mechanical parse stands. The passes answer with ops by block index, a reading, and the figure rules are the code's, so the parse keeps its figures under any model. The passes call whichever client their model id belongs to (`lib/parse/model.ts`: a `claude-` id through `lib/claude.ts`, any other through `lib/kimi.ts`) with the same prompts, so the model is one constant; `scripts/parse-compare.ts` parses the same pages under two models and prints what differs. The handwritten passes — Import PDF's judgment and conversion, which read page images — run on Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic, `claude-opus-5-5`, `@ai-sdk/anthropic` provider; `lib/claude.ts` is the client) at high effort (`HANDWRITTEN_MODEL`, `HANDWRITTEN_EFFORT`): what the parse gets wrong, every later tool inherits. An SVG chart — a figure whose media is inline SVG — is read by Claude Opus 5.5 wherever a model reads one, Analyze on it and the assistant acting on it (`SVG_CHART_MODEL`, `SVG_CHART_EFFORT` high, `lib/derive/svg-chart.ts`): the whole source, up to 200,000 characters, goes to the model, where GLM 5.3 read the first 12,000 as XML. Visualize (§20, Unitos Ultra) runs on Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic, `claude-opus-5-5`; `VISUALIZE_MODEL`, `VISUALIZE_EFFORT` `max`): a visualization is vector graphics written as code, which Opus 5.5 leads, and the picture has to be faithful or refused. The voice command (§6) runs on Claude Sonnet 5 (`claude-sonnet-5`; `VOICE_MODEL`, `VOICE_EFFORT`): it follows a multi-part spoken instruction and copies quotes exactly at a fifth of Opus 5.5's price, and the document prefix is cached between commands. The keys are `MOONSHOT_API_KEY` and `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; `MOONSHOT_BASE_URL` and `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` override the endpoints. **The bimonthly model update:** the seven constants (`GLM_5_3`, `GLM_5_3_FLASH`, `KIMI_K3`, `CLAUDE_FABLE_5_1`, `CLAUDE_OPUS_5_5`, `CLAUDE_SONNET_5`, `GEMINI_FLASH` in `lib/derive/config.ts`) are the defaults of seven roles (`lib/models.ts`: glm, glmFlash, kimi, claude, opus, sonnet, gemini — the three Anthropic roles follow their own families, so the import follows Fable, Visualize follows Opus, and the voice command follows Sonnet; Z.ai publishes no model list, so the two GLM roles move only when the constant does, and the run says so on the row). On the 1st of every second month `/api/cron/models` (vercel.json, `CRON_SECRET`; the admin page's Check now runs it any time) reads each provider's published model list (`GET /v1/models` at Moonshot and Anthropic, the Gemini models list at Google), finds the newest version of the role's family at the same shape as its current id (`claude-<name>-<version>`, `kimi-k<version>`, `gemini-<version>-flash`; a -thinking, -lite, -preview, or dated variant is another product and never taken), makes one probe call on it, and writes the id to the role's `ModelChoice` row; the clients (`lib/kimi.ts`, `lib/claude.ts`, `lib/video/gemini.ts`) resolve a default id to its role's row on every call, so nothing else changes. Every outcome — no newer version, a probe that failed, a key not set — lands on the row for the admin page (`lib/model-update.ts`). **The model per feature:** every model call names its feature (`lib/feature-models.ts`: explain, simplify, salience, extract, distill, summarize, compare, analyze, visualize, voice, find, ask, formalize, assistant, act, web, vision, svg-chart, stitch, stitch-select, merge, gist, log, glossary, contents, skeleton, connect, parse, classify, convert — the usage record's names), and the feature's model is its constant in `lib/derive/config.ts` unless the admin set another in the gateway page's Model per function section (`/admin/gateway`, shown with or without the gateway; `FeatureModel`, one row per feature that leaves its default; `/api/admin/feature-models`). A pick is one of the chat roles' default ids — which follows the role through the model update — or any other id, called as written; the id is probed with one call before the row is written, and a pick whose key is not set, or that does not answer, is refused and the feature keeps its model. The web feature takes a GLM or Kimi id alone: the search is the model's provider's. A claude- id runs through `lib/claude.ts`, any other through `lib/kimi.ts` (`modelCall`, `lib/model-call.ts`), with the feature's effort translated to the client's levels, so a feature's model is one id whichever provider it belongs to; a call that carries an image still goes to the vision feature's model, and an SVG chart to the svg-chart feature's. The rows are read on every call and again after five minutes, like the roles' rows.
- **The AI gateway:** LiteLLM, one host in front of every AI provider (`litellm/`: `config.yaml` is the whole configuration, `Dockerfile` the image, `README.md` the deploy steps; `lib/gateway.ts` is the app's side). With `LITELLM_BASE_URL` and `LITELLM_API_KEY` set, every AI call goes to the gateway with the app key and the app reads no provider key: GLM's and Kimi's chat calls to the OpenAI-compatible route as `zai/<id>` and `moonshot/<id>` (the Z.ai entries route through LiteLLM's openai provider at Z.ai's base URL, the one route that forwards `reasoning_effort` as written, with Z.ai's prices set on the entry); Claude's to the Anthropic pass-through (`/anthropic/v1`), the raw forward, so every Anthropic field survives — the effort and the server-side refusal fallback, which the gateway's own Anthropic-format route drops; Gemini's to the Gemini pass-through (`/gemini`), the file store included (the resumable upload's second request goes to the URL Google answers, so the gateway never carries the media); Groq and OpenAI Whisper to the transcription route as `groq/<id>` and `openai/<id>`; OpenAI TTS to the speech route; DeepL, Moonshot's API root (the web-search formula, the model list), and Z.ai's API root (the Web Search API) to custom pass-throughs (`/deepl/v2/translate`, `/moonshot`, `/zai`), each taking the app key and adding the provider's own. Deepgram is the one direct call under the gateway (`DEEPGRAM_API_KEY` stays on the app's host): its request body is the media bytes, which a custom pass-through re-encodes as JSON, and the gateway's own Deepgram route answers text and word times without the speakers. The other provider keys live in the gateway host's environment. The gateway holds the model list as the two GLM entries, wildcards per provider (`moonshot/*`, `anthropic/*`, `gemini/*`), and the audio models, so the bimonthly model update needs no gateway change: it lists through the pass-throughs and probes a candidate as `moonshot/<id>`. The gateway applies the app key's limits — requests and tokens per minute, a budget and its reset period, set from the admin gateway page and held in the gateway's database — runs the router's fallbacks (`router_settings.fallbacks`: a GLM call that fails runs again on Kimi K3, a Kimi call on Claude Opus 5.5 through the gateway's translation, with `drop_params` so one provider's fields never fail the next), retries, and records spend per call. The Anthropic pass-through strips the request's `fallbacks` field — the name is one of the gateway's own router parameters — so under the gateway Anthropic's server-side refusal fallback never reaches Anthropic; `callForJson` (`lib/derive/json-call.ts`) does the same work on the app's side: a Claude answer that stops on a refusal runs once more on `CLAUDE_REFUSAL_FALLBACK` (`lib/claude.ts`), and the usage record names the model that answered. Every call carries `x-litellm-tags` (`feature:<name>`, `user:<id>`) and `x-litellm-end-user-id` (`gatewayHeaders`), so the gateway's spend logs split the way the app's usage telemetry does. `LITELLM_ADMIN_KEY` is the master key, read by the admin console alone (`lib/gateway-admin.ts`): the gateway page (`/admin/gateway`) shows readiness and version, the models with their limits and the gateway's prices, the fallbacks, the app key's spend and limits (editable), spend over 30 days by day, model, provider, function, and account (the gateway's daily activity and tag activity endpoints), and Check models, one probe per model; with no app key set it issues one (`/key/generate`, shown once). Without the gateway variables, every client calls its provider directly with its own key, as before; the two modes are one code path with the base URL, key, and model id resolved per call (`gatewayUrl`, `keyFor`, `gatewayModelId`). The usage telemetry (§7) keeps recording on the app's side in both modes: the app's page prices at list, the gateway's page at the gateway's list, and the two are checked against each other.
- **Prompt caching:** The full parsed document is the prompt prefix (Moonshot's automatic context caching: a byte-identical prefix past 256 tokens is served from cache at a tenth of the input price, with no cache markers in the request). Every selection-level derivation must reuse the cached prefix.
- **Parsing:** PDF → blocks server-side. Use `unpdf` or `pdf-parse` for text extraction; preserve reading order. Markdown file (`.md`, `.markdown`, `.txt`) → blocks through the URL walk (`lib/parse/markdown-document.ts`): the file becomes one HTML page (remark with GFM: headings with GitHub-style ids, paragraphs, lists and task lists, tables, code fences, blockquotes, images as figures with the title or alt as caption, footnotes, raw HTML kept, front matter dropped and its title kept) and the walk reads it like a web page, no model pass; math is set aside before the markdown parse so its underscores and stars are not emphasis — `$$…$$` and `\[…\]` become EQUATION blocks, `$…$` and `\(…\)` stay as written in the text; an image with an absolute http(s) URL keeps it, an image with a relative path is its caption, a relative link is its text; the bytes are kept (`Document.fileData`) and dedupe by hash like a PDF, and a re-parse reads them again (a stored file that does not start with `%PDF-` is a Markdown file). URL ingestion: full-DOM structural parse via `jsdom` — equations keep their TeX (KaTeX/MathJax annotations, rendered with KaTeX in the reader), charts keep their inline SVG, figures keep their images and videos, lists/tables/separators keep their shape — and figures keep the page's look: the page's stylesheets load at parse (`lib/parse/figure-style.ts`), a chart svg carries the page's colors, fonts, and backdrop as inline style on its elements, and an image carries the backdrop the page drew behind it, so a dark site's white-line diagram renders on its own black instead of the reader's paper; and figures keep the page's layout: the same bake reads the page as a 1280×900 desktop browser lays it out and writes what it finds into the DOM before the walk — what that browser hides (a page's mobile tree beside its desktop tree) is pruned, centered captions stay centered, bold, italic, and monospace text keeps its style, headings keep their size, and every media element and every column of a figure row carries its width as a share of the text column, so two charts set side by side stay side by side at their size instead of stacking at full width (the layout walk: a px width sets, a percentage multiplies, padding subtracts, a grid divides by its tracks, a flex row shares the rest among its growing children; the text column is the most common width among the page's prose). The text column's width travels with the document (`Document.columnWidth`, 400–1100 px) and is the reader's article column for it, so the text runs as wide as it did on the page — the pane still caps the column, so it contracts when the notes tray opens; a figure the page sets wider than its text column (a chart at 844 px beside 680 px prose) carries the width past 100% on its `<figure>` and the reader draws it centered on the column and past its edges, as wide as the pane allows. A figure whose media is a video keeps the column: drawn past it, a video runs past its own pixels on a high-density screen and goes soft, where a chart or a photo holds up. A figure row — a flex or grid parent whose columns each hold media and a caption — is one FIGURE with one nested figure per column, its text the captions joined line by line; a single captioned figure is one figure with its caption. After the walk, a "Figure N" caption left with no figure beside it is rebuilt from the media nearest it in the page's DOM, and a caption beside a figure that has none folds into it (`lib/parse/figures.ts`). An image, video, or chart set inside a paragraph of text is a figure between the text's parts, never dropped with the paragraph's tags (an image between two runs of a `<p>`); an inline icon — narrower than a tenth of the text column — stays out. Then the media check (`checkMedia` in `lib/parse/figures.ts`) reads every image, video, iframe, and chart of the page's content against the blocks: a media element no block carries is built into a figure where the page set it — after the block holding its container's text (a list item's, a table cell's), else after the block holding the text nearest before it — and what cannot be built (a video from a host the sanitizer refuses) is named in the check; the counts travel with the document (`ParsedDocument.mediaCheck`). After the model passes, a figure the passes dropped between two blocks that survived is restored (`restoreFigures`): the passes reference blocks by index and may drop a figure by mistake; a figure dropped with the chrome around it stays out. A page whose charts its scripts draw (an svg with a viewBox and nothing inside, a canvas) first renders in a real browser where one is configured (`lib/parse/render-page.ts`; `BROWSER_WS_ENDPOINT` or `CHROMIUM_PATH`, the browser §11 uses for transcripts, shared through `lib/browser.ts`): 1280×900, media, images, and fonts blocked, scrolled through one viewport at a time so scroll-revealed charts draw, 150 s at most, and the rendered DOM parses in the static page's place. A chart the page's scripts animate settles before the parse (`lib/parse/capture-animation.ts`): the page's clock is paused and stepped — the page samples its own chart's shape every step of its clock, so a batch of steps costs two round trips to the browser, not two per step — a chart that changes nothing in 6 s is still (longer than a loop's pause, so sampling that starts inside one does not mistake the loop for stillness), a one-shot animation is drawn to its end, and a chart that loops is recorded frame by frame over one loop, encoded as a looping GIF (`lib/parse/gif.ts`, 10 frames a second, 64 colors, only the pixels that change per frame), stored as an `ImageAsset` of the document (`ImageAsset.documentId`; a re-parse or a delete removes it), and takes the svg's place as an `<img>` of the reader's own `/api/images/<id>` path Without a browser, or when the render fails, the static page stands and the figure audit (§15) reports the caption without its figure — followed by two AI passes that reference blocks by index and never write text: the core pass returns the block ranges that are the article (site navigation, footer link lists, newsletter, social, and legal chrome fall outside the ranges and are dropped; a figure with media — an image, a video, an embed, an SVG, `hasMedia` in figure-audit.ts — between the first and the last kept block is kept whatever the ranges say, and no pass's `drop` ever removes one: the figure rules are the code's, not the model's, so the parse keeps its figures under any model), then the layout pass (`lib/parse/layout.ts`) reads the page's own HTML beside what survives — as a browser lays it out: class names, inline styles, and the layout facts the bake wrote in — and says what each block is on the page: the kicker over the title, the metadata line, the contents list and its label, a heading and its level, a pull quote, a quote, a caption, the figures that share one row, the chrome to drop, a wrong block type, a fragment split mid-sentence, and the page's font; a join concatenates label-and-value blocks with one separator, a merge joins a fragment to the block above it with a space, a figure row wraps figure blocks in one figure, and nothing else changes a block's words. The layout pass does the structure pass's work (drop, retype, merge) on the page's HTML; the structure pass alone runs for a PDF with upload instructions. The layout pass's decisions land as the same layout tokens the walk writes, so the reader renders both alike. Each pass runs against the request's time budget (`modelPassDeadline` in `lib/parse/ingest.ts`: the route's limit less a margin; `/api/documents` and `/api/documents/[documentId]/reparse` allow 300 s): a pass that cannot finish in time aborts or is skipped and the mechanical parse stands, never a failed add. Every ingest stream sends an empty heartbeat line every 5 s while a pass reasons in silence (`lib/ndjson.ts`), so the connection outlives the call. `@mozilla/readability` is the fallback for pages the structural walk cannot read. Ingest streams stage progress (fetch → extract → select → structure → layout → save) to the client. The browser render is the longest stretch of the fetch stage, so it reports each phase it enters as the stage's detail line — rendering the page, opening it, scrolling through it, settling chart n of m — and the progress card keeps moving instead of standing on one line.. Every document is stamped with the parser version that produced it; a URL document stamped with an older version re-parses automatically — on open, and when its URL is added again — and can be re-parsed manually from the document menu. **The vision check** (`lib/parse/vision-check.ts`) is the last pass of a URL import, after the layout pass: where a browser is configured, the vision model has its key, and the blocks hold a figure, the page is pictured in the browser as a reader scrolls it — one picture per screen, `VISION_CHECK_TILES` at most — and the blocks are drawn the way the reader draws them (`proofHtml`: the reader's column, figure, figure-row, and role rules) and pictured the same way; the vision model (`VISION_CHECK_MODEL`, the model that reads images) reads both sets side by side with the block listing and answers with ops by block index, never writing text: `figure_row` for figures side by side on the page that the reader stacks, `unrow` for a row the page stacks, `drop` for a figure that appears in no picture of the page (under a ceiling of a third of the figures), `caption` for a text block that is a figure's caption or label on the page. Ops that break the rules are skipped one by one; on any failure, or past its budget (`CHECK_BUDGET_MS`, or what the import has left), the blocks stand. The progress card shows the stage (`check`), the save stage reports it (`visionCheck`), and `VISION_CHECK=off` turns it off.
- **Anchoring:** W3C Web Annotation selectors via `apache-annotator` (`@apache-annotator/dom`, `@apache-annotator/selector`).
- **Digest (Phase 6):** the assistant's stored context — one `NotebookDigest` row per corpus per user, rebuilt on read when a content fingerprint moves (§7). No embeddings: the assistant reads the corpus whole.
- **Styling:** Tailwind. Split-pane layout via CSS grid, not a heavy library.
- **Auth:** dual mode (Scalae pattern). With `SESSION_SECRET` plus Google (`GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`, `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`), Apple (`APPLE_CLIENT_ID`, `APPLE_TEAM_ID`, `APPLE_KEY_ID`, `APPLE_PRIVATE_KEY`), or email (`RESEND_API_KEY`, `EMAIL_FROM`) credentials set, sign-in (hand-rolled OIDC code flows, database sessions in an httpOnly cookie, 30 days; accounts key on the email, so every provider lands in one account; Apple's callback is a cross-site form_post with a SameSite=None state cookie and a self-signed ES256 client secret; email sign-in stores a hashed single-use token per `EmailConfirmation` row (`purpose` "signup" | "reset"), 30-minute expiry, and creates the account only when the link is clicked; the link lands on the dashboard, with no password step and no card: the card comes after 30 minutes of active time, the billing ask (§24); a password is optional (scrypt, `User.passwordHash` "s1$salt$hash", "" = none): returning users sign in with the same email link, or with email + password at `/signin?mode=in` once Forgot password has set one — it emails a reset link to `/reset`, which sets the new password and signs every other session out) gates the app at `/signin`; corpora, profiles, and digests belong to accounts, and the first account to sign in adopts the local reader's data. The block list (`BlockedEmail`, one row per email; `lib/block.ts`): a blocked email is refused at every sign-in door (Google, Apple, email confirmation, password login, the email sign-in form, Forgot password, the test door), and blocking deletes the account's sessions and pending email links, so it is signed out at once; the row keys on the email, so an email with no account yet can be blocked, and a reset account stays blocked. The operator blocks and unblocks from the admin accounts page. Unset, the app runs as the single local reader (`user-1`), nothing gated. `/admin` keeps its own `ADMIN_PASSWORD` gate, decoupled from reader sign-in; the admin never has access to an account — no session, no impersonation, no edits — and sends notifications into accounts (§18) and nothing else. Corpus routes verify membership (owner or collaborator, §12); object routes resolve their object to its corpus or document and check the same roles. `/api/auth/test-login` is a QA door, sealed unless `TEST_LOGIN_TOKEN` is set. With sign-in on, `/signin` opens a beta notice once per tab (sessionStorage `unitos-beta-notice`): Unitos is in beta, thanks to every beta user, and every beta account gets Unitos free and unlimited for now — the notebook and the Kimi, Claude, Gemini, and Groq tokens it uses — signed by the Unitos team; Continue, Escape, or a click outside closes it. ## 3. Data Model (Prisma)

```prisma
model User {
  id         String    @id @default(cuid())
  email      String    @unique
  name       String
  picture    String    @default("")
  createdAt  DateTime  @default(now())
  lastSeenAt DateTime  @default(now())
  sessions   Session[]
}

model Session {
  token     String   @id
  userId    String
  user      User     @relation(fields: [userId], references: [id], onDelete: Cascade)
  createdAt DateTime @default(now())
  expiresAt DateTime
}

model Notebook {
  id        String    @id @default(cuid())
  userId    String    @default("user-1") // owner account; "user-1" = the local reader
  title     String
  profile   Json?     // ReaderProfile override for this notebook
  sections  Section[]
  documents NotebookDocument[]
  digest    NotebookDigest?
  createdAt DateTime  @default(now())
  updatedAt DateTime  @updatedAt
}

model NotebookDigest {
  id          String   @id @default(cuid())
  notebookId  String   @unique
  notebook    Notebook @relation(fields: [notebookId], references: [id], onDelete: Cascade)
  userId      String   // per-user store; constant in v1
  fingerprint String   // cheap aggregates over the content tables; mismatch = stale
  parts       Json     // DigestParts: documents with text and layers, notes, sections
  counts      Json     // DigestCounts: documents, notes, annotations, distillations, …
  chars       Int      // rendered size before budget cuts
  builtAt     DateTime @default(now())
}

model Section {
  id         String   @id @default(cuid())
  notebookId String
  notebook   Notebook @relation(fields: [notebookId], references: [id], onDelete: Cascade)
  title      String
  order      Int
  parentId   String?  // nesting, one level deep is enough for v1
  parent     Section? @relation("SectionNesting", fields: [parentId], references: [id])
  children   Section[] @relation("SectionNesting")
  notes      Note[]
}

model Note {
  id             String     @id @default(cuid())
  sectionId      String
  section        Section    @relation(fields: [sectionId], references: [id], onDelete: Cascade)
  content        String     // markdown
  gist           String?    // the phrase a collapsed row shows, written by AI to fit the row (§6); null until written, cleared when content changes
  status         NoteStatus @default(ACCEPTED) // manual notes are ACCEPTED on create
```

## 4. The Derivation Pipeline (the one primitive)

Single server route: `POST /api/derive`

```typescript
type DeriveRequest = {
  type: 'EXPLAIN' | 'SIMPLIFY' | 'SALIENCE' | 'EXTRACT' | 'DISTILL' | 'SUMMARIZE' | 'FORMALIZE' | 'ASK' | 'COMPARE' | 'ANALYZE' | 'VISUALIZE';
  documentId?: string;         // absent for corpus DISTILL and for COMPARE
  documentIds?: [string, string]; // COMPARE only: the two documents, both attached
  notebookId: string;
  anchor?: AnchorInput;        // required for EXPLAIN/SIMPLIFY/EXTRACT/ANALYZE/VISUALIZE; optional focus for DISTILL
  question?: string;           // DISTILL: the question the quotes must answer; ASK: the question about the range
  video?: { startTime, endTime, region?, frame? }; // EXPLAIN on a moment (§11); ASK: the range
  depth?: 'layman' | 'intermediate' | 'professional'; // SUMMARIZE only; default layman
  format?: 'article' | 'notes'; // FORMALIZE only: the destination shape
  sectionId?: string;          // FORMALIZE notes, COMPARE: where the notes land
};
```

Flow:
1. Load document blocks (cached prompt prefix) + ReaderProfile + notebook section skeleton.
2. Select prompt template by `type` (templates in `/lib/prompts/`, one file per type).
3. Stream response. A streamed text derivation (EXPLAIN, SIMPLIFY, ANALYZE, SUMMARIZE, ASK, and the assistant's answer) goes through `lib/derive/text-stream.ts`: a heartbeat space every 5 s until the first text delta (Kimi K3 reasons before it writes, in silence, and an idle connection dies at proxies), then the text; a failure reports in-band, the stream ending with `STREAM_ERROR_TOKEN` and the reason — the API's error, the output budget spent before the answer, or the model declining. The client drops the heartbeat spaces and shows the reason, never an empty answer for a failed call.
4. Route output by destination:
   - `EXPLAIN` → annotation bubble in the reader rail (persisted as a Note in a hidden "Annotations" section, so it's searchable, but rendered in the rail)
   - `SIMPLIFY` → bubble beside the article, level with the selection (ephemeral, not persisted; close to dismiss)
   - `SALIENCE` → highlight layer (persisted as document-level Json, per notebook)
   - `EXTRACT` → no longer runs. Stored extractions on `NotebookDocument.extractions` still paint as a labeled highlight layer and open the match card (the origin phrase and every passage, each row jumping to its text, Delete); the assistant does the matching now (§7)
   - `DISTILL` (the reader's Extract) → distillation on `NotebookDocument.distillations`, rendered as the extract page; a quote reaches notes only through the page's "Add to notes", which lands a `Note` with `status: PENDING`
   - `SUMMARIZE` → Summary tab in the side panel (persisted on `NotebookDocument.summaries`, one summary per depth; Regenerate overwrites)
   - `FORMALIZE` → the transcript rewritten (§11). format `article`: `{title, markdown}` on `NotebookDocument.formalized`, shown under the player as the view bar's article view; Regenerate overwrites. format `notes`: one `PENDING` note per topic, each with a time source resolved from the topic's transcript blocks
   - `ASK` → the answer to a question about a time range of a video or audio document (§11), streamed as text into the pane's Ask card; persists nothing. "Add to notes" lands it as a `PENDING` note with a time source for the range (`/api/notes`, origin `ask`)
   - `COMPARE` → two documents read whole: one `PENDING` note of agreements, disagreements, and what only one covers, with a source per cited span on both documents. No menu entry: `/api/derive` with type `COMPARE` and two document ids runs it
   - `ANALYZE` → a FIGURE or TABLE block read by the vision model — an SVG chart by Claude Opus 5.5, which reads the source whole (`SVG_CHART_MODEL`, `lib/derive/svg-chart.ts`) — streamed into the card beside the block — the same card as EXPLAIN, titled Analysis — and persisted in the hidden Annotations section like EXPLAIN (the figure toolbar's Analyze figure; a table is text, §6, and has no Analyze). Never a note.
   - `VISUALIZE` (§20, Unitos Ultra) → the selection as a picture, into the same card as EXPLAIN, titled Visualization, and persisted in the hidden Annotations section like EXPLAIN: an SVG `ImageAsset` and one annotation whose markdown is the image and its caption. When the model is not certain a picture carries the passage's core idea, the run declines with the reason and persists nothing.

**Regenerate.** Every AI tool's output can be run again. The reader's cards carry the circular arrow (`RegenerateIcon`), the pages and panels the word Regenerate; the tip says what runs and what it replaces. The run repeats the same derivation on the same input — the card's own selection, the extraction's own question, the answer's own question and range — never on whatever the input box holds now, so an edited box changes nothing. The new output replaces the old, and only once it is stored: the run persists its own result first and the one it replaced goes then, so a failed run, a stopped one, or a Visualize that declines never loses what stands. Simplify, Analyze, Visualize, and the page Ask replace their annotation (`DELETE /api/notes/<id>`, no toast — the new annotation is the notice); Extract replaces its distillation, Summarize its summary at that depth, Formalize its article. Ask and Find persist nothing and simply run again. A tool conversation is not regenerated: a turn is sent, never replaced.

Prompt templates always receive: reader context, document title, section skeleton, and the anchored text with surrounding context (±2 blocks). Every reader-facing template ends with the one style rule (`STYLE_RULE` in `lib/prompts/types.ts`, carried by every assistant surface — the panel, the selection chat and tool conversations, Ask on a recording, Stitch's reply, Explain, Analyze, Summarize: be straightforward, short, and concise; answer the question directly, and first; short sentences, plain words, one point per sentence; no idioms, no complex phrases, no preamble, no filler, no closing summary; technical language only where the material uses it; an example or a quote only where the answer needs one) and a word cap: Explain 150, Ask 150, Analyze 220, the assistant's answer 250 unless the question needs more, summaries 180 (layman), 400 (professional). Captions, compare points, find explanations, and glossary definitions are one sentence, two at most.

**DISTILL output contract:** model returns JSON `{quotes: [{blockId, start, end, quote, caption}]}` — the verbatim spans across the whole document that answer the question, each copied out as `quote` and captioned with exactly how it answers the question in the document's context (or which facet of the question it answers, when it answers one facet only). `quote` is what the span resolves on: offsets are what a model gets wrong — counted from the document, from the rendered `[block <id>]` line, or against a mis-copied block id — and the copied words are what it gets right, so a span takes the anchor ladder of §5 (`lib/derive/json.ts`): the offsets when they slice the quote, else the quote inside the named block, else the quote anywhere in the document, else the offsets alone. Only a quote the document does not carry at all drops. The prompt (`lib/prompts/distill.ts`) reads the question first, then scans with it in mind; a span is cut to what answers — a phrase, a sentence, or a whole paragraph — and every quote answers in a different way: a quote that repeats a point another quote already makes is dropped, so the page never over-quotes. Validate strictly; resolve every span against the real block text and drop what does not resolve; on parse failure, retry once with the error appended, then surface failure to user. Never write malformed output to DB. Stored quotes heal at render like salience and orphan visibly (§5). The HTTP response streams heartbeat bytes while the model works and ends with the distillation JSON (or the in-band error token), so the connection survives a minutes-long scan.

**COMPARE output contract:** both documents ride as one cacheable system message under their ids (the corpus DISTILL rendering, 220k characters per document, cut with a marker past that). The model returns JSON `{agreements, disagreements, onlyFirst, onlySecond}`, each a list of `{point, spans: [{blockId, start, end}]}`; an agreement or disagreement cites one span per document. Validate strictly; resolve every span against the real block text and drop what does not resolve; a point with no resolvable span still stands as text. The note (`lib/derive/analysis.ts`) lists the points under Agree, Disagree, Only in <first>, Only in <second>; its sources are the distinct resolved spans, at most 24. No point at all fails the run. Runs behind the heartbeat stream.

**ANALYZE output contract:** the block's visual attaches as it does for EXPLAIN (`lib/derive/figure.ts`: the image, the SVG source, or the PDF page region); a TABLE block attaches its markup, plus its page region when the PDF has one. The model is the one that reads visuals with the least invention — `DERIVATION_MODEL.ANALYZE`, the most capable model, the same one upload and parse trust. It sees the corpus section EXPLAIN sees (related passages from the other documents, the reader's notes and annotations) and streams markdown in exactly three sections, always in this order, each under its bold label (`ANALYSIS_SECTIONS` in `lib/prompts/analyze.ts`, per language): **Insights** — one or two sentences on what the visual is there to show, then 2 to 5 patterns read from the data and interpreted against the document (a trend, a break, an outlier, a gap between groups, a comparison the argument rests on, what the visual shows that the text does not say); **Quantitative** — the numbers behind each pattern, as printed, never rounded, ≈ before a value estimated off an axis or a bar, "(text)" after a number taken from the document instead of the visual; **Linking to context** — where the visual contradicts, weakens, or complicates a claim in the document, cited as `[block <id>]`, and where it connects to the project's other documents and notes; one line when there is neither. Under 220 words. The stream ends with `STREAM_NOTE_TOKEN` + the annotation's id, like EXPLAIN; the annotation is anchored to the whole caption or the table selection, reopens from the block's side label, and lists under Analyses in the Annotations tab.

**VISUALIZE output contract:** see §20. Two calls: the draw, then the check that reads the finished picture back.

**EXTRACT** no longer runs: the assistant's matches replaced it (§7). Stored extractions stay per notebook per document, oldest first — the index gives the label (M1, M2, …); spans heal at render like salience; an unresolvable span stays stored but unpainted; Delete removes one. (The v1 EXTRACT selection-to-note flow lives on as DISTILL's "Add to notes".)

## 5. Anchoring (make-or-break)

Anchors must survive re-parses and reflows. Dual strategy:

1. **Primary:** `blockId + startOffset + endOffset` against block plain text.
2. **Fallback:** `TextQuoteSelector` — `quotedText` + 32-char `prefix`/`suffix`. On load, if block resolution fails (block deleted or text changed), fuzzy-match the quote across the document via `@apache-annotator`.
3. **Never silently drop.** Unresolvable → set `orphaned: true`, render the note with a broken-link indicator and the quoted text preserved.
4. **The quote never changes.** `quotedText` is the words as quoted when the source was made, and a block edit never rewrites it: a note's quote and an annotation's words stay what the reader quoted. An edit inside the anchored words remaps the offsets (`lib/anchors/remap.ts`) and writes the words the anchor covers now to `Source.anchoredText` (null while they are still the quote); the ladder re-finds by `anchoredText ?? quotedText`, so the mark follows the edit while the quote stands.

DOM ranges are never the source of truth. Convert selection → block-relative offsets at capture time using data attributes (`data-block-id`) on rendered blocks.

**Passages.** A selection that crosses blocks is one passage (`lib/anchors/passage.ts`): the reader captures one segment per block it touches, in reading order — the first block's span from the selection's start, whole blocks between, the last block's span to the selection's end — and sends the first segment as `anchor` with every segment as `segments`. Equations and pages take no span and are left out (the popover says so). Every tool works on the whole passage: the routes (`/api/derive`, `/api/annotations`, `/api/notes`, `/api/assistant/act`) resolve each segment through the ladder on its own, the anchored text is the segments' quotes one paragraph each, and the note gets one source per segment, so its marks cover the whole selection and each mark resolves, orphans, and jumps on its own. Simplify numbers the passage's sentences on through the blocks — a block boundary is always a sentence boundary (`lib/sentences.ts`) — so a pressed rewritten sentence mirrors its source sentences in whichever blocks hold them.

## 6. Layout & UX

**Split view, both panes persistent:**
- Reader views: Normal shows one document; Side by Side and Top and Bottom show two panes, each with the full tool set (`?view=side|stack&doc2=`). The bar between the two panes drags to change how they share the reader — like the notes tray's bar: double-click resets to half, arrow keys nudge, and the split is remembered per browser, one per view kind (`unitos-pane-split:<view>`, 0.2–0.8).
- **Left:** document reader. Blocks rendered from DB, selection popover on highlight. **One toolbar per content kind** (`TOOLBARS` in reader-interactions.tsx): the popover shows the tools of the kind under the selection and nothing else, and a tool missing from a kind's list is never offered there. The first tool after the assistant is the kind's lead tool and reads as recommended. **With Jev configured** (`TYPESAFE_API_KEY`, `lib/jev.ts`) the text and equation toolbars predict their lead instead: when the popover opens on a selection, `POST /api/jev/lead-tool` `{notebookId, kind, blockType, text, tools}` (viewer) asks Jev one choice over the kind's tools — the state is the selection (its text to 600 characters, its word count, its block type) and the reader's own toolbar history (tool uses in the last 90 days, the last eight tools) — and the tool it picks at confidence 0.5 or above reads as recommended: its row takes the lead look (Simplify, Visualize, Comment, Link), or its bubble draws a clay ring (the highlight colors, Add to notes, Read aloud). The rows never move — an answer that lands late would move a button under the pointer — and the assistant stays first. A key term's toolbar and a figure's keep their fixed lead. Every prediction lands as one `ClickEvent` (`lead-predicted:<tool>`, ai-toolbar) beside the click it tried to predict, so the hit rate reads from the click log; no key, no prediction.

  | Kind | Blocks | Toolbar |
  |---|---|---|
  | Text | PARAGRAPH, HEADING, LIST, CODE, TABLE | Assistant, Simplify, Visualize, Comment, Link across texts, highlight colors, Add to notes, Read aloud |
  | Figure | FIGURE | Figure tools: Assistant, **Analyze figure**, Comment, Link across texts, highlight colors, Add to notes |
  | Equation | EQUATION | Equation tools: Assistant, Visualize, Comment, Link across texts, highlight colors, Add to notes |

  Analyze (§4) exists only on the figure toolbar; Simplify and Read aloud only on text. A table is text: its cells are rendered text, so a selection inside it is a selection like any other — the text toolbar, the marks painted on the passage (`table-marks.ts`, since the table's html is one string and cannot carry React marks) — and never the figure ring or the hold-and-circle gesture. Explain is on every kind: the selection explained in plain words, tuned to the reader's background, in the card beside the article; on a figure it deciphers the visual. A question about the selection goes to the Assistant, the first tool of every kind (§7). A new kind of content gets a new row here and a new list in `TOOLBARS`, never a condition on a button.
- **Layout tokens.** A text block's html may open with class tokens — `<p class="kicker center">`, `<h2 class="center">`, `<ol class="contents">` — and the reader lays the block out by them (`layoutTokens`, block-view.tsx): `center` centers the text; `kicker` and `meta` are small mono uppercase, muted and letter-spaced; `label` is the same with a hairline rule under it; `contents` is the contents list — no list padding, one entry per 32px line, a hairline under each line drawn by a repeating gradient, so no text node joins the block; `display` is a standalone statement at 28px; `quote` a blockquote paragraph with a left rule; `caption` a caption standing alone at 13px, muted. One token decides a block's look; `center` only aligns. The masthead rule: when a centered kicker or meta line opens the document, or its first heading is centered, the title renders centered, large and light (40px, weight 400, `.reader-masthead-title`), the kicker above it and the meta line below. The kicker is the document's first block, pulled out of the flow and still rendered through `BlockView`, so its `data-block-id` and its marks work.
- **Figure rows.** A figure's html is one `<figure>`, and its children lay out as a wrapping row centered in the column (`.reader-figure > figure`, flex): a caption takes the full width, a nested `<figure style="width:33%">` is one captioned column stacking its media over its caption, and media keep an inline width from the page (`style="width:48%"`, a percent of the article column) — two charts share a row at the page's proportions instead of each filling the column. No figure rule is `!important`, so the inline width always wins. Media in a row sit 2% apart, so their widths must sum to 100% less the gaps (two at 48%) or the row wraps; a column carries its gap inside its own width as padding, so three columns at 33% fill one row.
- **Fonts.** `Document.font` is null (Figtree), `serif`, `mono`, or `sans`: the parser stores `sans` for a page set in a sans face, and the font picker offers it. The article carries `data-font`; for `sans` the headings and the title leave the display face for the body's sans (weight 600, `letter-spacing: -0.01em`; the masthead title stays 400). Heading sizes never change with the font.
- **Right:** docked notes drawer showing the section skeleton of the current notebook. Pending notes render with amber left-border + Accept (`Enter`) / Reject (`Backspace`) / Edit (`e`). Accepting must be exactly one keystroke when a pending note is focused.
- **Reader views** (`reader-panes.tsx`; the button at the bottom left of the reader): Normal shows one document; Side by Side and Top and Bottom show two panes, each with the full tool set, the view and the second document carried in the URL (`?view=side|stack&doc2=`); a fresh open is Normal. A split view is its own layout, so two panes never crowd each other:
  - Each pane has a **pane header** — one row at its top, above the scroller, never over the text: the pane's document (a select; choosing changes the URL), the article menu (Contents, §26), Collapse (§28), and Extract. In Normal view the article menu floats at the top left of the pane and stays there as the article scrolls; in a split view it lives in the header, its list dropping below the header.
  - Tool cards stay **collapsed** in a split pane: a stored explanation, simplified rewrite, assistant conversation, or comment shows as its highlight and the tool's symbol at the end of the text, and opens only when the reader clicks the highlight or the symbol; the card then docks below the highlight at the article's width, as in a narrow reader. New output still opens as it streams.
  - With the tray folded the two panes fill the browser exactly. Opening a tab (the rail, a jump to a note or an annotation) does not shrink the panes: the reader keeps its width and the tray becomes a screen past its right edge, in a **strip** that scrolls sideways and snaps to one of two rests — the documents, or the documents' right part with the tray. Opening a tab scrolls to the tray; an edge button at either side (one at a time, on the side with a screen past it) and a sideways scroll move between the two; folding the tray scrolls back to the documents first, then the column closes. In Top and Bottom the pane header and the article column center in the part of the pane the strip leaves visible (`--strip-cut` on the strip, `--reader-cut` per pane), so no line starts under the edge; a Side by Side pane is narrower than the column, so its first pane peeks from under the edge instead. Below md the tray stays a bottom sheet and there is no strip. The workspace grid is one column that can never grow past the browser (`minmax(0, 1fr)`): a pane's widest line stays inside its pane instead of pushing the rail and the header off screen.
- The notes tray holds the open document's notes alone. `Note.documentId` is the document a note was written in: the open document when the note is typed in the tray's composer, spoken in it, or made from a passage, a video range, or an annotation (`POST /api/notes` takes `documentId` and derives it from a source when one is sent); the notes full page's composer and a note citing nothing leave it null, the project's. A note is the open document's when it was written in it or quotes it — a source of the note is in it (`noteInDocument`, `scopeSections` in `use-outline.ts`): the tray's sections are the project's, every one kept so a note can be added to any, with the document's notes under them; its pending queue is the document's, and a line under the queue counts the pending notes of other documents and of the project, which the notes full page holds. The notes full page is the whole project. Its **By document** button opens the project's notes as a grid over the page (`components/outline/document-columns.tsx`): one column per document that has notes, in attach order, then Project for the notes that belong to no document; one row per section, a child section under its parent; a note under the document it was written in, else the document its first source is in. The header row and the section column stay in view while the grid scrolls; the cards are the page's, so a note edits, pins, and selects there, Compare in the selection bar opens the compare view over the grid, and Esc or Notes closes it.
- Notes move by drag in the tray and on the notes full page, and the drag is a hold (`lib/hold-drag.ts`, `HoldSensor` in hold-sensor.tsx): a note has two modes, draggable and editing. Draggable, the default, a hold anywhere on the card (`HOLD_MS`, 150 ms, with the pointer still within `HOLD_TOLERANCE_PX`; a mouse may also pull `HOLD_DISTANCE_PX` at once) picks the note up — the card presses down while the hold runs (`.card-held`) and lifts and tilts as it is picked up (`.card-drag-overlay`, the `card-lift` keyframes) — and from then on it follows the pointer. A press that ends first is a click, and the control under it still gets it; a press that drifts first is a scroll or a text selection. A press on an input, a text field, or a control marked `data-no-drag` never drags. Editing, the pencil's mode, keeps the press in its fields, its bar, and its buttons (`data-no-drag`), and a hold on the header row still picks the note up — to reorder it, to move it, or to hold it over another note until the ring closes; its draft is saved before a merge reads it (below); a viewer's notes never drag. There is no grip on a note (a section keeps its grip). A search no longer pauses the drag: the drop's place is counted in the section's whole list either way. Nothing in a list moves while a drag runs — the card rides in a drag overlay under the pointer, its place left dimmed, and a clay line in the gap says where it would land: on the top half of a card the line goes above it, on the bottom half below it, and a list holding nothing takes the drop on its own space. Cards that slid out from under the pointer are what made the old preview flicker between a reorder and a merge; a line over cards that hold still says the same thing and never moves the target. One drag crosses sections — the whole surface is one board (`SortableBoard`, `SortableGroup` in sortable.tsx), so a note dropped in another section's notes moves there, at the place the line stood. Moving a note between sections is a main action, so it is the drag itself: there is no "Move to…" menu on a note. The drop's place comes from the note the line stood before, not from its index in the list (`dropIndex` in board-lists.ts): a list a composer owns a note in shows one note fewer than the section holds, and a note moving down its own list leaves its own place before it lands. A board holding lists of two kinds says which take which (`canDrop`), so a section drag never draws a line among notes. The notes full page adds section reorder, renaming, compare, and export.
- The notes full page is one press away from the tray: the four-arrows button (`MaximizeIcon`) beside the tray's search and its view toggle opens it, in place of the old bar under the notes. On the notes full page a click on a section's title opens the section's **board** (`section-board.tsx`): the section's notes filling the screen as tiles side by side, one grid (`.note-board`; the columns are as many 220px tiles as the board's width holds and no more than the notes, so few notes take wide tiles, up to 480px; `NoteTile`), each tile the note's id, its title, and its body. A tile shows its whole body when the board has the room: the board's height, less the composer and the gaps, is shared among the rows of tiles, and a tile grows to its share (`--tile-max`), never under the 3:4 tile — taller than wide — (`--tile-min`); the tiles of one row stretch to one height. A body the tile cannot hold whole is cut and fades out at the bottom (`.note-tile-cut`); a body shown whole reads to its last line. A board of many notes keeps its 3:4 tiles. A tile is in the note's draggable mode and nothing else: a hold anywhere picks it up, the line stands between tiles (a grid list, `layout="grid"` on `SortableGroup`: the left half of a tile lands the drag before it, the right half after it), and a tile held over another until the ring closes joins it. A click on a tile opens the note whole over the board — its card, with the pencil, the jump, the history — and ✕, a click beside it, or Esc closes it; editing happens there, never in a tile. The board's header carries the section's own actions (+ Note, Speak), the sections inside it as chips (each opens its board), the section it sits in (one press up), and Notes or Esc to close. The section's pencil, beside its title, renames it.
- Merge is a hold, not a pass, and it reads the cards, not the pointer: once the dragged note covers more than 80% of another note (`MERGE_COVER`, of the smaller of the two — a one-line note dropped on a long one covers little of it and all of itself, and either way it is over that note) a ring draws itself around the covered note over one second (`MERGE_DWELL_MS`), and at the full ring the merge runs. There is nothing to press: the hold is the whole gesture, and the ring is the reader watching it commit. Moving on before the ring closes puts the line back. A drag that passes over a note on its way somewhere else never merges anything. The pointer is not the card: a note picked up by its grip hangs below and right of the pointer, so a pointer on a note means the dragged note is somewhere above it — reading the pointer merged the wrong note, or none. What the reader sees is one note covering another, so that is what decides. The dragged note draws back a little while it covers one, so the ring underneath shows on all four sides. The floating card over the article is one more card a hold can merge into (`[data-floating-note]`); it takes the drop itself, its own words saved first. A note let go off every list and over an open document leaves the list: the board reports the drop (`onDropOutside`) and the tray floats the note where the card was seen, in its draggable mode.
- What the merge looks like (globals.css, `.merge-ring` / `.merge-fall` / `.note-absorb` / `.note-merging` / `.note-merged`): the ring closes, the held note falls into the note it merges into — landing on where that note ends up, not where it was, since the merge takes the held note out of the list and moves the other up a row — and that note blooms as it takes it in. The joined text is there at once and settles in. Merge with AI (the ticker's bulk action) works after the bloom: the note keeps its row, its controls stand down, and its id sits beside the spark and the sheened label every AI tool in the app uses (`ThinkingIndicator`), while a sheen crosses the card and the model writes the note that replaces both. Reduced motion keeps all of it still — the ring stands drawn, the fall is a fade, and the words carry the state.
- A merge is a join (`lib/notes/join.ts`; `POST /api/notes/merge {mode: "join"}`, the default): the notes' text lands in one note as it is, in the order the notes stand in — the note on top first, the note under it after, whichever was dragged; the route reads the order from the sections and the rows, the client's optimistic join from the tree. The first note's title is the merged note's title; a later note's title stays in the body as a heading over that note's text (`## `). A hold, a drop on the floating card, an annotation dropped on a note, and the ticker's Merge all join. Merge with AI stays on the ticker, beside Merge: the model writes the one note that takes their place — a title, then one section per key point (a level-two heading that states the point, one to three sentences in the reader's own words), with every quote of the notes placed verbatim under the point it supports; a point in only one note stays, a point in several notes is written once, and every image, link, and checklist item stays (`lib/prompts/merge.ts`, `lib/notes/merge.ts`; `mode: "ai"`). The route checks the quotes after the call — a quote the model dropped or rewrote is added at the end as it was (`withEveryQuote`), since the quotes are what the sources point back to; a call that fails joins instead. Either way the sources' anchors and replies move to the target, the sources are deleted, the target's gist is cleared, and the merge is an edit in the target's history. Accepted notes only — pending notes go through Accept/Reject first.
- A merge can be undone: what it took apart — the target's text before, and every consumed note with its anchors and replies — is kept as the meta of a `NOTE_MERGE` history event (`lib/notes/merge-snapshot.ts`), and the answer carries the event's id. A pill under the notes offers Undo for twelve seconds after each merge (`MergeUndoBar`; the next merge takes the pill). `POST /api/notes/merge/undo {undoId}` puts the target's text back, re-creates the consumed notes with their ids at their rows, moves their anchors and replies back, and deletes the anchors copied from annotations — while the target still holds the merged text; a note edited since is the reader's newer work, and the undo refuses. A merge is undone once. The History panel lists the merge ("merged notes into one").
- A full page load keeps the reader where it was (a note, an annotation, or an AI tool refreshes the page, and a new deploy or a dropped response turns the next refresh into a full load): the reading position — the block at the top of the pane and its offset, not a pixel count, so a figure above it that loads late moves nothing — and the drawer's folded state and tab save per tab in sessionStorage (`lib/reading-position.ts`); an inline script restores them before the first paint, and the reader holds the position while the layout under it settles, until the reader scrolls. A `?src`, `?block`, or `?link` jump wins over the restore.
- **First open.** A document opened right after it was added reveals as the reader scrolls, as if laid out in sequence (`reveal.tsx`): the add sets `unitos-reveal:<documentId>` in sessionStorage before the open (document-bar.tsx), and the reader takes the flag on mount. Every block wrapper starts invisible in its place; one IntersectionObserver on the pane's scroll box gives each block its turn as it enters the pane — blocks in view together in document order, 110 ms apart — and a text block unclips line by line (`steps(n)`, one step per line at 70 ms, 1.4 s at most) while a figure, a table, or a page fades in and rises 8px over 320 ms. A revealed block stays revealed: the wrapper keeps no class and no style after its turn, and it has no padding, border, or overflow, so margins collapse through it as before. Reading mode only — never the transcript, never over a reading position restore or a `?src`, `?block`, or `?link` jump, never under reduced motion, and never on a full page load, where the article is already painted.
- Note edits auto-save: while a note's editor is open, every edit saves on its own — a debounced PATCH after the last keystroke, a keepalive flush when the window closes — so nothing typed is lost. Done closes the editor; Cancel and Esc restore the content from before this edit, auto-saves included. Every keystroke is also written to localStorage (`lib/note-drafts.ts`, `unitos-note-draft:<noteId>`): the PATCH and the flush are network calls, and the local draft is what survives a crash, a power loss, or a lost connection between them. It is cleared when the server confirms the same content; on the next load, `useOutline` checks each remaining draft against its note — the same content, or a note changed elsewhere since (`updatedAt` later than the draft), clears it; anything else is written to the note. A section's composer (a new note) auto-saves the same way (`use-note-compose.ts`, `unitos-note-compose:<sectionId>`): after the last keystroke it creates the note on the server, then saves every later edit to it, and keeps the note out of the section's list while it owns it. Save releases the note; Cancel deletes it; Esc keeps a note with text and drops an empty one. Closing the tab, leaving the page, or losing power keeps the note: the next load reopens the composer on the local draft, owning the note it created. A note being edited is never deleted unless the user presses Cancel on a new note or Delete. The composer sits above the section's notes and the note it makes lands at the top of the section (`top: true` on `POST /api/notes`: order -1, then the orders are made 0..n again); every other way a note is made still lands at the end. "+ note" and Speak stay visible (`section-action.ts`), the same in the tray and on the notes full page: they are the section's main actions, and a control that appears only on hover is not found. Writing a note is the main one, so it is the biggest control in the row — a filled clay pill with a plus and the word Note, never a hairline chip; Speak stays a small pill beside it. A section carries no Add section of its own: one Add section sits at the bottom of the notes full page. The top of every note being edited — the tray card's header, the floating card, a section's composer — carries the save state (`save-state.tsx`): "Saving…" while the draft differs from what the server holds, "Saved" once the server confirmed it, "Not saved" when the last save failed (the next keystroke retries).
- A note has a title and a body (`lib/note-title.ts`): the title is the note's first line when that line is a level-one heading — `# Title` — and the body is everything after it; the note stays one markdown string (`Note.content`), so export, the digest, the prompts, and search read the title as the heading it is, and a note whose first line is anything else has no title. The title renders as one bold row above the body (`.note-title`); a collapsed note shows its title in place of the gist, and a note without one asks for a gist as before. Every editor — the tray card, the notes full page, the floating card, a section's composer — is a title field above the body's editor (`NoteTitleField`, `useNoteParts`): Enter in the title moves to the body, the composer opens in the title, and the saved note is the two joined. A heading typed at the top of the body becomes the title when the editor closes.
- One note structure, everywhere a note renders (the tray, the notes full page, a compare pane): a header row — collapse chevron and the note's id at the left (`#` plus the last six characters of its cuid; click copies it; searching `#k3x9pq` finds the note), edit (the pencil: a filled 28px button with a 16px pencil, the biggest control in the row, since editing is the note's main action), jump, pin, and select at the right (the circle; its tooltip names what the selected notes do together — merge, pin, delete, and on the notes full page compare) — then the body, then the actions. The editor keeps that shape: the header stays, and the note is edited as the document it renders to, so the note looks the same after Done as it did while editing.
- Any number of effects stack on the same words, and no surface shows their markers: the editor paints them live, the rendered note nests them, and the collapsed line strips them. The note's colors and underline are stored as tags (`<clay>`, `<u>`) that react-markdown drops, so the renderer turns them into links its `a` override paints (`components/markdown.tsx`); a link cannot hold another link, so nested tags are flattened first — every run of text is emitted once with all the styles covering it in its href, and a chip or a markdown link inside a tag is left alone, the styles passing over it. Converting one tag at a time nested the links and left the outer one showing as `[word](#dissect-style-clay)`.
- Editing renders live — headings, lists, quotes, bold, italic, underline, colors, ¶ chips, links, images — as markdown underneath (`lib/note-markup.ts` renders the document, `lib/note-doc.ts` reads it back after every edit). Typing "- " or "* " (Shift+8) starts a bulleted list, "+ " a dash list (the same list drawn with a dash for a marker; the renderer reads the marker back from the source, `remarkDashLists` in components/markdown.tsx), "1. " a numbered list, "[ ] " a checklist (stored as GFM task items, `- [ ] ` and `- [x] `; the box ticks by a click in the editor and, in the rendered note, by a click that saves the note without opening the editor), "> " a quote; Enter continues it, Enter on an empty item ends it, Tab nests an item and Shift+Tab brings it back out, Backspace at the start of a marked line drops the marker (a nested item outdents first; there is no Paragraph button, this is the whole of that action), Cmd+B/I/U and the bar's B/I/U style the selection or what is typed next, and the bar's four colors do the same — a color replaces the color the text had, line by line over any selection, so a selection that crosses styled words or lines still colors (wrapping the raw selection in a tag broke the moment it crossed a marker). Two bars: the tray's editor (and the floating card) carries the core tools — undo, redo, H1–H3, bulleted and numbered lists, the quote, B/I/U, colors, indent — and a link "More tools on the notes full page"; the notes full page's editor (a section's composer, a note's editor, a compare pane) carries them all: the dash list, the checklist, and the image picker too. The bar sits at the top of the editor, the title field under it, then the body: the bar is the editor's own, and the title reads as the first line of what it writes. Every typed shortcut works in both bars. Every tool's tooltip names the tool on its first line and its key or typed shortcut on the second (⌘ on a Mac, Ctrl elsewhere; `outline.tip*`). Each level draws its own marker — disc, circle, square; 1., a., i. — in the editor and in the rendered note alike. The editor nests by two spaces a level whatever the marker; markdown nests by the parent item's content column, so the renderer re-indents the lines to the columns markdown expects (`alignListIndents`, components/markdown.tsx) — without it a nested numbered item came out flat the moment the note was rendered. The editing card grows with the note, capped at the height of the pane it scrolls in — the tray's panel, or the window on the notes full page — and past the cap the text scrolls inside the card while the bar and the buttons stay; opening the editor brings the whole card into view. In the tray a note leaves the tray by the same hold: pick it up and let it go over the article, and the note becomes a floating card there, in its draggable mode (a note being edited drags by its header row, and floats the same way). While a card floats the tray folds and the article column moves to the pane's left edge, out from under the card; both come back when the card docks or closes. The floating card has the note's two modes: draggable, the default, shows the note — its title, its body, its sources — and a hold anywhere on it lifts it (`.note-lifted`) and moves it; the pencil opens its editor — the title field and the body's editor, with the same auto-save — and Done and Cancel return it to the draggable mode where it is. The card resizes from its corner in both modes. Dragged into the sidebar — the right column, the rail and the tray when it is open, at its full height (`inSidebar`); below md the rail's bar and the tray's sheet — and let go, it docks: the note goes back into the tray. The card draws in and rings while it is over the sidebar and says what letting go does (`.note-docking`). Back to the tray docks it too (docking from the editor reopens the tray card's editor on the draft). Wrap text, a toggle on the card remembered per browser (`unitos-note-wrap`): the card joins the article's scroll pane at its spot in the text, scrolls with the text, and the lines flow around it — the card announces the gap it needs (`lib/note-wrap.ts`) and the reader draws it as a pair of float spacers at the article's content top — the one position the card measures against exactly, since a float placed before a block lands at the block's margin edge, not its border edge — on the side of the column the card sits on, 18px clear of the card on every side; when less than 200px of text would remain beside the card, the text skips below it instead. Only paragraphs, headings, and lists flow around the card. A figure, a table, a video, a page, an equation, or a code block is one object that a sliver of column would ruin, so it clears the card and takes its place above or below it, whole — but only a block whose place overlaps the card's height clears (`wrapClears`, reader.tsx: once per gap the reader lays the blocks out with none cleared and measures them against the gap); a block above or below the card stays where it is. Clearing every such block pushed a video far above the card below it, so the card seemed to jump above the video. The tray card reads "In a floating card over the article" meanwhile.
- Every note, annotation, and edit jumps back to its exact position in the article: the jump button (the locate glyph) on a note's header opens the reader on the note's first source and flashes the quote (double-clicking a note card opens its editor instead — in the tray, on the notes full page, and on the floating card; a note shows no source chips under its body, only the quotes it holds, each pointing back to its source); on an annotation card it is the existing jump to the anchor; on an edit row it scrolls the open document to the edited block and flashes it, while the block is still in the document. **A quote points back on its own** (`lib/notes/quote-sources.ts`, `components/markdown.tsx`): a blockquote in a note whose words are a source's — the quote Add to notes wrote, or a quote dropped in — jumps to that source on a click, and a line under its words names the document (red, with "unresolved", when the source orphaned). No row of source chips renders under a note: the header's jump button reaches the note's first source, and every quote in the note reaches its own. **A quote deleted from its note takes its source with it** (`sourcesLeftByQuotes`, `lib/notes/quote-sources.ts`, on `PATCH /api/notes/[noteId]` with content): a source a quote covered before the edit and no quote covers after it is deleted, so the mark in the reader unpaints on the next refresh and no longer points at a note without the words; a source no quote ever covered (a paraphrase the assistant wrote) stays. A deleted note takes every source with it (cascade), and the reader fades its marks at once (`dissect:note-removed`).
- **Quotes from the reader by drag** (`lib/quote-drag.ts`): a drag that starts on a highlighted passage carries the passage — its anchor, one segment per block, and its text — under the drag's own type, with a small card of the first words under the pointer in place of the browser's ghost; a **bookmark** at the left of every hovered block in the reader, transcript lines included (`BlockBookmark`, reader.tsx), drags the whole block the same way, the block its anchor. Let go in a note's editor, the quote lands at the text position under the pointer, on a line of its own, as blockquote lines: while the drag is over the text a caret drawn by the editor, which never blinks, stands where the quote will land (`note-editor.tsx`, `caretPositionFromPoint`). Let go on a note card outside its editor, the quote is added at the end of the note. Either way the passage's anchor becomes a source of the note (`PATCH /api/notes/[noteId]` `{addSource}`, resolved through the ladder like a source on a new note), so the quote points back; in a section's composer the source waits for the note the composer creates and attaches then. A drop anywhere else ignores the quote, and a drop outside the app pastes its text. The drag reads the selection as it is when it starts (`captureSelection`), so it never waits on the toolbar. A **hold on a highlight** in the text — a mark with a source: a highlight, a comment's span, a note's quote, a tool's mark — lifts its passage the way a hold lifts a note card (`watchHold` with `pull: false`, `lib/card-drag.ts` kind `quote`): the pointer stays on the mark for `HOLD_MS`, the quote follows the pointer as a ghost of its first words, and let go on a note card of the tray or on the floating card it is added at the end of the note with the mark's anchor as its source, like a quote let go on a card outside its editor. A press that moves before the hold is a selection, as ever, and a shorter press is the click that opens the annotation; the article stops selecting while the ghost is out.
- **Merged notes keep their seams** (`lib/notes/join.ts`): Join text puts every later note's text under a subtitle — the note's title, or "Merged note" when it had none — written as a level-six heading, which the note draws small and dimmed over a rule, in the rendered note and in the editor alike. The subtitle is a line like any other: delete it and the texts read as one.
- Notes have two views, per browser and per project (`unitos-notes-view:<notebookId>`; the tray and the notes full page share it). **Collapsed**, the default: every accepted note folds to one line — its id, its title (its gist when it has none), and its source count. A search overrides the view: every note it found shows whole, and the words it found light up (`.search-hit`, `lib/search-hits.ts`) in the title row and the rendered body alike; a search that finds nothing says so ("No notes match"); clearing the search puts the view back. The gist is one short phrase, written by AI, that says what the note says and fits the row: at most 5 words and 30 characters (14 in Chinese), in the note's language, no trailing period (`lib/prompts/gist.ts`; `POST /api/notes/gist` writes it to `Note.gist` in one batched call when collapsed rows render without one; a content edit clears it). Until the gist arrives the row shows the note's first words, cut at a word boundary — never with an ellipsis. That fallback is the text the reader sees, never the markdown behind it: `lib/markdown-preview.ts` takes the markers off with the note's own parser (`parseNote`), so a stack of effects reads as plainly as one — a hand-rolled strip only knows the shapes it was written for, and left `**_word_**` reading as `_word_`. A chip reads as nothing and an image as its alt: a preview line has no room for a picture, and none for a URL. **Expanded**: every note shows whole — nothing clipped, nothing to scroll inside a card. One button beside the search — Expand all / Collapse all — switches the view; a note's own chevron opens or folds that one note against the view until the view switches again. Pending notes never collapse: they are read before they are accepted. A jump to a note (an issue card) opens it if it is collapsed.
- Annotations have the same two views (`unitos-annotations-view:<notebookId>`): every card in the Annotations tab — highlight, comment, explanation, analysis, assistant conversation, simplified rewrite — carries the note structure (chevron, the highlight's color, the id) and collapses to its id and its gist by default (the AI-written phrase a note's row shows, its first words until it arrives); Expand all shows every annotation whole, the assistant conversations included, with no scroll inside a card. Clicking an annotation's mark in the text opens its card if it is collapsed. Every card's header ends in a three-dots menu (`annotation-menu.tsx`), in reach collapsed or not: **New note from this annotation** makes a note of it in a section the reader picks (`POST /api/notes {sectionId, fromAnnotationId}`: the annotation's text and copies of its anchors, accepted, at the end of the section; one section asks nothing); **Add to a note…** joins its text into a note the reader picks from a searchable list of the accepted notes by section (`POST /api/notes/merge`, join); Jump and Delete are there too. Either way the annotation stays where it is, still painted in the article; a line under the header says what happened. An annotation on a figure, table, or equation carries a label — A1, A2, … counted over such annotations in document order, the same label the block shows in the article (`figureLabelBySource`, page.tsx) — and the label's tip says so; text annotations carry none, their highlight being their mark. Links keep their compact cards. Each group's label carries the symbol of the tool that made it — the comment bubble, Explain's question mark, Analyze's chart, the assistant's sparkle, Simplify's lines, the link chain — the glyph on the toolbar button and on the mark in the text, so a reader finds a comment or a link by the symbol they used. In the text the symbol is a small round chip at the end of the highlighted text, on the highlight's bottom edge right after its last character, in every view (`.mark-chip`); clicking it opens the card. A highlighted figure, table, or equation shows the same symbol in its side label. Every kind of annotation carries one **kind color** everywhere (`--kind-*` in globals.css, `annotationKindColor` in `lib/annotations/kind.ts`): comment blue, explain red, simplify green, analyze teal, visualize magenta, the assistant violet, a link slate; a highlight carries its own hue. The color is the mark's fill and underline and its chip in the text (a comment's mark fills in it; a tool's mark underlines in it and fills on hover and while its card is open), the border of the card over the article, the border of the card in the Annotations tab and on the annotations full page, the border of the annotation beside a note on the notes full page, and the annotation reference row's icon and rule in a note (the row's href carries `&kind=`). The **annotations full page** (`/n/[notebookId]/annotations`, `components/panels/annotations-full-page.tsx`, the four-arrows button beside the tab's view toggle) is every annotation of the project, grouped by the document its first anchor is in — an article, a video, an audio document — in attach order, a detached document's after them, then Project for the sidebar assistant's conversations, anchored nowhere; within a document in reading order (the anchor's block, then its offset; a media document by time). The cards are the tab's (`components/panels/annotation-card.tsx`, shared): the kind named in the header with its symbol, the kind color on the border, the same three-dots menu and actions, Expand all and Collapse all shared with the tab; a document's title opens it in the reader. Links stay in the tab.
- Compare, on the notes full page: select two or more notes and press Compare in the selection bar. The compare view opens over the page with one pane per note — each its own scroller holding the whole note card, so the notes read and edit next to each other — Side by side (columns) or Stacked (rows); the layout choice persists per browser (`unitos-compare-layout`). Add note… adds a pane, ✕ removes one, and Esc or Notes closes the view.

**Other UX rules:**
- A note's jump button scrolls the reader to the note's first anchor and flashes the highlight. If the document isn't open, open it. A collapsed row keeps its source count; no chips render under a note.
- No pill, tab, select, or collapsed row cuts its text with an ellipsis. A label is written to fit (the Add-document tabs: PDF, image, or Markdown; Video or audio; Google Drive; URL; Library); a document title in the top bar's document pill, the document list, or a pane's document select is cut at a word boundary, the full title one hover away (`clipWords`, `lib/markdown-preview.ts`); a collapsed note or annotation shows its gist.
- **Folders.** A folder is a named group of a project's documents, and a folder can hold folders (`DocumentFolder`; `NotebookDocument.folderId`, null = the project itself). The document list under the header's pill draws the project's folders first, by title, then the documents outside every folder, then New folder. On a wide screen (md and up) hovering a folder's row opens the folder's own list beside it — its folders, then its documents, then New folder — one open list per level, so the tree reads as a menu; a press opens it too. On a narrow screen a press opens the folder's list under its row, and the open document's folders open on their own so the reader sees where they are. A folder's row shows how many documents it holds, its own folders counted in, and the folders on the open document's path are drawn bold. A folder's ⋯ holds New folder inside, Rename folder, Move to folder, and Delete folder; a document's ⋯ holds Move to folder, which lists the project itself and every folder; a folder never moves into itself. Delete folder moves what the folder holds up one level: nothing leaves the project. A folder is the reader's own grouping; no AI feature reads it. Routes: `POST /api/notebooks/[id]/folders {title, parentId?}`, `PATCH` and `DELETE /api/notebooks/[id]/folders/[folderId]`, `PATCH /api/notebooks/[id]/documents/[documentId] {folderId}`; the tree is `components/reader/document-folders.tsx`.
- The text under the open selection popover keeps a selection tint (a mark of kind `selection`, the same color as the browser's selection): the browser's own selection goes the moment the assistant's command box or the comment box takes focus, and while the assistant runs, but the tint stays until the popover closes. Every block of the passage (§5) keeps it, so the tint is the selection whole from the moment the pointer lifts, never the first block alone. The native selection over that mark paints nothing, so the two never stack. Reading mode only — edit mode's blocks are the browser's editable regions.
- A deleted note's marks answer at once, wherever the delete came from — a tool card's Delete, the on-mark card, the Annotations tab, the notes tray (`dissect:note-removed`): the mark fades (`.mark-out`), takes no clicks, and unpaints before the refresh; a failed delete puts it back (`dissect:note-restored`). A stored AI annotation's mark — Explain, Simplify, Analyze, Visualize, the assistant — is its underline and its symbol alone, no fill, while its card is closed (`.tool-mark`): the clay fill belongs to the live selection under the toolbar and to an open card's anchor, so a stored rewrite never reads as a selection; hovering fills it, and opening its card fills it. Marks that open something deepen on hover. An Explain, Simplify, or Assistant run keeps its mark after its card closes, until the server's copy lands.
- SIMPLIFY opens a translucent bubble to the right of the article, level with the selection, sliding in with a smooth animation. The selection stays tinted while the bubble is open. The document text never changes. The output persists as a note in the hidden Annotations section (like EXPLAIN), so it is still there when the reader leaves and comes back — listed under Simplified in the Annotations tab. Sentence mirroring: the prompt numbers the original sentences and the model appends a source marker ([[1]] or [[2,3]], at least one number) after each rewritten sentence. Every sentence in the bubble is lightly tinted; pressing one turns it solid and tints exactly its source sentences in the article. Both sides split sentences with the same function (src/lib/sentences.ts), so marker indices map back to exact offsets — never model-quoted text.
- Edit mode has no Edit button: double-click a text block to edit it in place. A fading hint card beside the article teaches this until the first double-click. Done, Esc, or a press anywhere outside the article, its format bar, the edit-mode controls, and the selection tools returns to reading — whatever was being typed saves first, and the format bar never outlives the mode. Selecting text in edit mode opens the same selection popover as reading mode; unsaved typing saves before any anchor is stored.
- The edit toolbar: undo / redo, paragraph / h1 / h2 / h3 / bulleted list / numbered list, bold / italic / underline, text color, highlight, indent / outdent (two-space steps on the caret's line), remove paragraph. ## 7. Assistant Scopes + the Digest (Phase 6)

One assistant panel with two scopes, both reading the digest. The assistant covers content, never the account: there is no scope across projects.

| Scope | Context sent | Example queries |
|---|---|---|
| This page (wire value `document`; `documentId` required) | the open document from this project's digest, whole — its text, its layers, and the project's notes that cite it (`documentSystem` in `lib/digest/render.ts`) | "what does the method section claim", "where does this page give the number", "summarize the argument" |
| Project (wire value `notebook`) | this project's digest, whole | "map claims to evidence", "where do my notes contradict", "which sections are thin" |

This page is the default while a document is open and disabled with none open; the tasks (contradictions, gaps, unsourced) run at Project scope. A conversation holds both scopes: the scope is chosen per message. A reader has as many conversations of a project as they start. **Conversations**, at the head of the panel, lists them newest first — the title (the note's gist, else the first message's words), when it last changed, how many messages, the open one marked; a click opens one in the thread's place, the bin deletes one (its side chats and comments with it), Back returns to the thread, and History under the list opens the assistant history page. **New conversation**, beside it, starts an empty thread and keeps the one on screen in the list: it deletes nothing (`GET /api/assistant/conversation?list=1` answers the list, `?conversationNoteId=` one conversation, and `DELETE` is the bin's). The scope row (This page, Project) and the answer row (Fast Thinking, Deep Thinking, Web) sit right above the composer, one row each.

**Assistant history** (`/n/[notebookId]/assistant`, from History under the panel's Conversations list): every assistant conversation of the project, whoever had it, newest first, each on its own panel — the contributor who started it as a person badge (shared projects only, §19), the kind (Assistant, or the tool with its plus for a tool's output continued into a conversation, §21), when, the message count, and the turns whole. Side chats are left out: a side chat belongs to the conversation it came from and opens from that chat box. A conversation anchored in a document shows the document's title and the highlighted text it started from, and Open in the reader opens the document at that text (`?doc=&src=`); the sidebar assistant's own conversation shows as Project assistant, and Open in the reader opens the reader. Conversations are the notes of the hidden Annotations section that hold turns (`SYNTHESIS`, or a tool note with `Note.conversation`), each carrying its author.

**The digest** is the assistant's storage: one `NotebookDigest` row per corpus per user holding the serialized corpus — every document in full (text and video transcripts, block-tagged), every note (pending ones marked), every annotation (highlights, comments, explanations, simplified rewrites, assistant conversations), every distillation, extraction, summary, salience span, link, and edit. Never a similarity search: the assistant sees everything, so questions about counts, spread, and absence are answerable.

- **Staleness:** a fingerprint of cheap grouped aggregates over the content tables (note counts and `updatedAt`, block id sets, `BlockEdit` rows, layer Json hashes, titles) is compared on every read; a mismatch rebuilds the row. No mutation hooks to forget.
- **The ranked cut:** with Jev configured and the project's documents past the text budget, the documents render in the order the message needs them (`lib/digest/rank.ts`): one yes/no per document — does it hold material the question needs — from its title, its skeleton's gist, and its parts' titles, and the cut falls on the rest. Under the budget nothing is cut, so nothing is ranked and the digest stays byte-identical; over it the system message changed as documents were added anyway, and a message-shaped order is the better trade. Page scope is never ranked: one document has the whole budget.
- **Determinism:** the rendered digest is byte-identical until content changes, so both scopes cache their prompt prefix (§2).
- **Budgets:** past the character budget, document text cuts at block boundaries with a declared marker, never silently. Notes and layers have their own budget. The admin digest page still renders every project of an account together (`corporaSystem`): a document attached to several projects renders its text once; later projects point back to it.
- **Selection questions** stay in the reader: the selection popover's assistant chat (`/api/assistant/act`) already carries the anchor and the cached document prefix.
- **Actions from the sidebar assistant.** At This page scope, Ask can change the open document and the notes, the way the selection chat does: the same action types (`lib/assistant/plan.ts`, one code path for both routes: the schema, the quote-to-anchor resolution, the warnings for actions that do not validate), proposed in the answer, never run by the server. A message that asks for a change ends the model's answer with a fenced block (info string `actions`) holding the actions as JSON; the route holds the fence back from the stream, so the reader sees the answer alone, validates every action against the real document, and sends the plan after the answer as `STREAM_PLAN_TOKEN` + JSON. The panel hands the plan to the open document's reader (`dissect:assistant-plan`, by document id, so a split reader's right pane takes its own), and the reader's plan card shows it: every action with a checkbox, its description, and its concrete target; Apply runs the checked ones through the normal API routes; Cancel drops them. Nothing runs without Apply. Under the answer, the panel says how many actions were proposed, with Review in the plan card to reopen the card once closed; the answer saves without the actions. Project scope proposes nothing: a message that asks for a change is answered with where the change runs.
- **Undo.** Every applied action records the request that takes it back, and the toast after Apply carries Undo for eight seconds: a text edit patches the block's text back, a format change its kind, a removed block restores through `/api/blocks/restore` with its own id, an added paragraph, highlight, comment, note, section, or link deletes, and a style toggles again. Undo runs them newest first and says how many came back. The selection chat's plan takes the same path.
- **Matches.** The selection chat does the work the Match-it tool did, and answers questions Explain does not. With a selection, the plan carries `matches` beside `reply` and `actions`: the passages across the document that deal with what the selection focuses on — 3 to 8, from anywhere in the document, never the selection itself, the most revealing first, each a verbatim quote of one block with one sentence on what it says about the topic. The prompt has the model gather them first and answer from them, every claim in the reply citing its passage as `[block <id>]`. The matches are the model's working: the reply lists none of them, and the card shows the reply alone — a claim's `[block <id>]` citation renders as the ¶ chip that jumps to the block. The reply is what the card shows and what the conversation note stores. The first message of a conversation always gathers matches; a later message gathers them only when it asks where else the document deals with something. Without a selection the list is empty.
- **Assistant history origins.** Every panel of the history page says where its conversation comes from, as a badge after the kind: Sidebar assistant (started in the assistant tab of the side panel, anchored nowhere — the panel says it stays in that tab and carries no link), Selection chat (started from a highlighted text; the document's title, the text, and Open in the reader), or Tool conversation (a tool card's output continued, §21; the same document line and link).
- **The queue:** a message sent while an answer runs waits under the thread, faded, with ✕ to remove it; the composer's button reads Queue once a message is composed, and the box says so. Queued messages go out in order, one per finished answer — after a Stop too. New conversation clears the queue. Every assistant chat queues the same way (`components/assistant/queued-list.tsx`): the panel, the reader's card and its side chats (a message queued in a side chat goes out into that side chat), tool conversations (Explain+, Simplify+), and the media pane's assistant.
- **Attachments:** the paperclip takes images (shown to the model), PDFs and text files (their text), and video and audio files (transcribed through the media document's own ladder, §11, one line per stretch of speech stamped with its time; the file stages in chunks through `POST /api/uploads` like a media upload and `POST /api/assistant/attach-media` transcribes it, 200 MB at most). ## 8. Build Phases (vertical slices, in order)

Each phase must be fully working end-to-end before starting the next.

### Phase 1 — Notebook outliner (no AI, no documents)
- CRUD: notebooks, sections (drag-reorder, one nesting level), manual notes.
- Notes full-page view. Markdown rendering.
- **Done when:** can create a notebook, build a section skeleton, write/reorder notes, reload with everything intact.

### Phase 2 — Documents: ingest, render, attach
- Upload PDF / paste URL → parse to blocks → persist → render in reader pane.
- PDFs up to 50 MB. Vercel caps a request body at about 4.5 MB, so the client splits bigger files into chunks (`/api/uploads`) and `/api/uploads/complete` assembles them into the same ingest path.
- Attach documents to notebooks. Split view shell (reader left, notes drawer right).
- URL replica fidelity (`lib/parse/url.ts`, parser version 16): the walk reads the page's baked layout (`data-unitos-hidden`, `data-align`, `data-style`, `data-font-size`, `data-font`, `data-box`, from `lib/parse/figure-style.ts`) and works without it. A box (`data-box`) is an element the page paints its own background under, different from the background behind it, or sets in its own font while it holds a chart; text in a box that holds a figure's media is the figure's words (a chart's title, legend, axis labels, source line), never its caption — a caption sits outside the box, on the page's background, unless it is labeled ("Figure N", or a `figcaption`), which is a caption wherever it sits (`lib/parse/figures.ts`). The figure's words keep the page's look: the bake writes their font size, weight, style, color, transform, spacing, and alignment as inline style, a legend swatch keeps its size, color, corner, and gap, and the box carries its background, padding, and corner (`data-box-style`); in the figure's html a wrapper holding only inline content becomes a `<p>` (a legend of spans stays one row), the box stays as the one `<div>` a figure keeps, with the box's look inline, and the sanitizer keeps exactly those properties with plain values (`lib/parse/sanitize.ts`; `.reader-figure figure > div` in globals.css stacks the box's content at the column's width). A caption inside the box keeps the reader's caption look. An element hidden at a desktop viewport is pruned. A contents list — a `nav` labeled contents, or a list whose links all point at the page's own fragments — stays: its header row is a `label` paragraph ("Contents · 5 sections"), its entries one LIST block with html `<ol class="contents">`, one entry per line (`<label> <title>`, nested entries indented two spaces), the label a `code` style span and the title a link span whose `targetFragment` becomes `targetOrder` in `resolveContentsLinks` right before the blocks are saved (after the model passes; fragments never reach the database). A contents target set as a paragraph (`<p id>`) becomes a HEADING at 1 + the entry's depth; tag levels shift so the document's shallowest level (the banner aside) is 1; with sizes baked in, a short bold or larger paragraph with no sentence end becomes a heading placed into the ladder of the document's heading sizes, and a large short statement is a `display` line. Text blocks carry html layout tokens the reader renders: `kicker` (a short label line first, before the banner heading), `meta` (≥ 2 sibling rows of "Label: value" joined with " · "), `label`, `contents`, `display`, `quote` (from a blockquote), `caption` (a figure caption standing as a paragraph), `center` and `right`. Bold, italic, underline, and code runs (`strong`/`b`, `em`/`i`/`var`, `u`/`ins`, `code`/`kbd`/`samp`/`tt`, and `data-style`) become style spans on PARAGRAPH, LIST, and HEADING blocks, measured on the final block text; a span over a whole heading drops. The page's font family travels as `ParsedDocument.font` into `Document.font` on creation only. A newsletter section (a form's wrapper with ≤ 300 chars naming the signup) and a trailing run of ≥ 6 short paragraphs after the last section's content (the footer's link columns) drop mechanically. Progress adds a `layout` stage, and the `save` stage carries the final figure check as JSON (`figures`, `captionsWithoutFigure`).
- **Done when:** a 30-page PDF renders with correct block order, headings, and tables legible; same document attaches to two notebooks without re-parsing (dedupe by fileHash).

### Phase 3 — Anchoring + manual extract
- Text selection → anchor capture → "Add to section" → Note (ACCEPTED, manual) with Source.
- Source chips on notes; chip click → scroll + flash.
- Anchor resolution on load, including fallback matcher and orphan handling.
- **Done when:** highlight survives page reload AND a forced re-parse of the document; orphaned anchors render gracefully.

### Phase 4 — First derivation: EXPLAIN
- `/api/derive` route, prompt caching wired, streaming into annotation rail.
- Persist as Note in hidden Annotations section.
- **Done when:** second EXPLAIN call on the same document measurably reuses the cached prefix (log cache hit tokens); response streams in <2s to first token.

### Phase 5 — Context + remaining derivations
- Context (background / purpose / application) + injection into all prompts.
- SIMPLIFY (inline swap/revert), SALIENCE (overlay), EXTRACT (pending queue with keyboard flow).
- **Checkpoint:** compare EXPLAIN output with/without profile on the same passage. If not meaningfully better than generic output, stop and rethink prompts before building more.

### Phase 6 — Assistant scopes + the digest
- The digest store (§7): `NotebookDigest` rows, fingerprint staleness, deterministic render.
- Assistant panel with the two scopes. Contradiction detection, gap detection.
- Admin digest page at `/admin/digest`.

### Phase 7 — Glossary + export
- Glossary extraction (terms/acronyms/symbols); hover definitions in reader. It is built when the reader first opens the glossary, never at ingest: one model call over the whole document, for a list many documents are never asked for. `POST /api/documents/[documentId]/glossary {lang}` (editor) builds it when the document has none and answers the entries. Definitions are written in the reader's language (§4). The entry stores one definition per language — `Document.glossary`: `[{term, definition, blockIds[], lang, definitions: {en?, zh?}}]`, `lang` the language `definition` was written in, `definitions[lang]` mirroring it; an entry saved before `lang` was stored is unknown-language. A document opened in another language asks `POST /api/documents/[documentId]/glossary {lang}` (editor) for that language's definitions once per browser session; one model call over the term list writes them, and the hover shows them when they land. Until then the term still underlines and its hover shows only "Click for tools". The term itself is never translated: it reads as the document writes it.
- Export notebook → Markdown and .docx with footnotes resolving to `documentTitle, blockId` citations.

## 11. Video and audio

A video is a document. Its transcript is its blocks; a video anchor is a time range instead of a text span. Everything downstream — notes, source chips, pending/accept, the derivation pipeline — is unchanged. The video file is never modified; annotations are a layer on top of the player.

An audio file is the same document with no frame. Upload takes mp3, m4a, aac, wav, flac, or ogg (sniffed by magic bytes like video; `VideoAsset.mimeType` audio/* marks the document audio); the pane renders the audio player — a compact stage with a waveform decoration, timed comments fading in over it, no fullscreen — and drops everything frame-bound: no circling (the annotate button opens the composer on the current moment), no Visual thumbnails (cards carry time and text), no frame capture on Explain. Transcript, Find, the assistant, time anchors, and the derivation pipeline are identical.

### Data model additions

```prisma
model VideoAsset {
  id               String           @id @default(cuid())
  documentId       String           @unique
  document         Document         @relation(fields: [documentId], references: [id], onDelete: Cascade)
  kind             VideoKind        @default(UPLOAD)
  youtubeId        String?          @unique // kind YOUTUBE only; dedupes re-adds
  mimeType         String?          // kind UPLOAD only
  size             Int?             // kind UPLOAD only
  chunkSize        Int?             // kind UPLOAD only
  duration         Float?           // seconds; written by the client after metadata loads
  width            Int?
  height           Int?
  transcriptStatus TranscriptStatus @default(NONE)
  transcriptError  String?          // FAILED only: the reason, shown in the transcript pane
  transcriptStartedAt DateTime?     // a PENDING older than 10 minutes is a dead run; may start again
  chunks           VideoChunk[]
}

model VideoChunk {
  id      String     @id @default(cuid())
  videoId String
  video   VideoAsset @relation(fields: [videoId], references: [id], onDelete: Cascade)
  index   Int
  data    Bytes
  @@unique([videoId, index])
}

enum VideoKind { UPLOAD YOUTUBE }
enum TranscriptStatus { NONE PENDING READY FAILED }
```

- `Document` ↔ `VideoAsset` is one-to-one. A document with a VideoAsset is a video document; the reader renders the video pane for it instead of the text reader.
- The upload box takes a video or audio file (mp4/webm/ogg/mov or mp3/m4a/aac/wav/flac/ogg) or a YouTube link in its URL box. A file becomes kind `UPLOAD` (an audio/* sniff makes it an audio document); a YouTube link becomes kind `YOUTUBE` (title from oEmbed, deduped by `youtubeId`, no bytes stored). A YouTube link pasted into Add URL lands in the same path.
- Every video document has exactly one `VIDEO` block at order 0. Video anchors point at it when no transcript block fits.
- Transcript lines are `TRANSCRIPT` blocks with `Block.startTime`/`Block.endTime` (seconds). Same text machinery as every other block.
- Upload bytes live in `VideoChunk` rows, streamed by `GET /api/video/[documentId]` with HTTP Range support so the scrubber seeks without downloading the file. 200 MB cap per video. Postgres holds the bytes for the same reason it holds PDF bytes: zero-config deploys. Blob storage is the upgrade path, not a v1 requirement.
- A YouTube video plays through the IFrame player behind the same overlay, controls, markers, and Visual strip. Its frame cannot be drawn from the iframe (cross-origin), so the real frame comes from the storyboard sheets YouTube publishes for its scrubber, proxied through this origin so the canvas stays readable. Those frames feed both the Visual cards and Explain.

### Time anchors (§5 extended)

`Source` gains three nullable columns: `startTime`, `endTime` (seconds), `region` (Json). A source with `startTime` set is a video anchor:

- It cites a span of the video. `blockId` points at the VIDEO block or a transcript block; `quotedText` holds the transcript excerpt for the range (or the formatted time range) so chips read well.
- `region` is an optional drawn shape in percent coordinates of the video frame, so it stays glued to the same spot at any player size. The draw tool makes a freehand closed loop — `{kind: "path", points: [[x, y], …]}`, each coordinate 0–100; `{kind: "ellipse", cx, cy, rx, ry}` stays valid for older annotations. Pixels are never stored.
- Resolution: time anchors skip the text-matching ladder and never orphan. The video file never changes.
- Clicking a source chip on a video anchor seeks the player to `startTime` and flashes the annotation — the video equivalent of scroll + flash.

### The video pane

- Player: plain `<video>` with custom controls. The scrubber carries a marker dot per annotation; clicking a marker seeks to it.
- Overlay: a transparent SVG layer sized to the frame. Annotate (the magnifier button) pauses the video; the drag draws a freehand loop that closes itself, or "Use the whole frame" skips the loop; a comment card saves the note (Annotations section, time source; range defaults to [t, t+4s], editable).
- Replay: while playing, every annotation whose range contains the current time fades in on the overlay and fades out past its end.
- One surface under the player: the tool bar — Circle & comment, the Find box, the transcription status — then Find results, Visual, the view bar, and the text the view bar has lit. Nothing video lives anywhere else.
- Visual: a strip of annotation cards — the frame at that moment with the loop drawn on it, the time range, the note. Clicking a card seeks there and opens what was written at that moment. The video stays untouched; this is the visual note layer.
- View bar: the tabs under Visual naming the text under the player, one view showing at a time — Transcription always, Article once a FORMALIZE article exists (transcript.tsx `ViewBar`, the pane's `view` state). The lit tab is the text's title; the bar's right side carries the lit view's actions — Transcribe again for the transcription, Open as document, Copy markdown, and Regenerate for the article. A new article lights its own tab: Formalize into an article shows the article under the player instead of navigating to its document.
- Transcription: in article form under the view bar — no box, no scroll of its own, the lines at the article's measure and the article's type, so they read and edit like any document. Click a line to seek; the current line highlights and playback follows it by scrolling the pane the smallest amount that brings it into view, and holds still for four seconds after the reader scrolls by hand.
- Every row is headed by its time and, when the recording has more than one voice, the name of the voice speaking it: `0:01 RJ: What's up, everybody?`. A row is a run of lines by one speaker — the paragraph breaks at every speaker change as well as at a pause — and the head is `[data-anchor-skip]`, so a block's DOM text stays the stored text (§5). The name is a button for an editor: clicking it renames that voice everywhere.
- The line being spoken is the last line that has started (`activeLineAt`, lib/video/types.ts), never the first line whose range contains the moment. Transcripts stored before ranges were pulled apart (**Ranges** below) overlap, and the first containing range lights the earlier line while the later one is being spoken; at a pause it lights nothing at all. Reading the last start instead is right for both: a line stays lit until the next line starts, and the last line goes out five seconds after its end. A transcript line is an anchor like a circled spot: hovering one offers Comment and Explain on that line's time range, and a line covered by an annotation is underlined and opens it. The lines are the reader's blocks: the video pane hosts the reader's interaction layer (`ReaderInteractions` with `transcript`, reader-interactions.tsx; the lines render through `TranscriptBody`, reader.tsx) with the player and the video tools above the lines, so selecting transcript text opens the article's text toolbar — Assistant, Explain, Simplify, Match-it, Comment, Link across texts, highlight colors, Add to notes, Read aloud — and marks, links, and tool cards work on the lines through the same code path. No edit mode, no article menu, no Distill or Extract on a transcript.
- On open, a caption floats over the player for a few seconds naming the tools — circle to comment, search the video, click a line to seek — then fades.

### Transcription

Transcription starts on its own the moment a video or audio is added — the transcription is the point. The UI calls it the transcription everywhere; code identifiers keep `transcript` (`TRANSCRIPT` blocks, `transcriptStatus`). The pane never shows a Transcribe button: it shows Transcribing…, then the lines; Retry and Paste transcription appear only when every rung failed, and Transcribe again redoes a finished transcription. The job runs a provider ladder ordered by source, cleans the lines, writes them as TRANSCRIPT blocks, and stores which rung succeeded (`POST /api/documents/[documentId]/transcribe` runs the same job for retries):

- **YouTube video:** caption tracks from the player API, keyless — the transcript YouTube itself shows, so it goes first: exact, free, and seconds to fetch. Clients in the order that answers a datacenter IP (ANDROID, then IOS, then ANDROID_VR — the app clients still answer where the web clients demand a bot check), then the watch page scrape. A client's track list is read the way YouTube picks the panel's default: the default track when the response names one, then a human track in the spoken language, then the auto-generated track, then the first human track (a translation). Cues fetch as json3 with the track URL's own `fmt` replaced (a track URL can carry `fmt=srv3`, and a second `fmt` appended is ignored), srv3 XML as the second format, up to three tracks per client (`lib/video/captions.ts`) → the same captions read by a real browser, where one is configured (`lib/video/browser-transcript.ts`; `BROWSER_WS_ENDPOINT`, the CDP websocket of a browser service, or `CHROMIUM_PATH`, a Chromium binary on the server, with `CHROMIUM_ARGS` for flags; the browser opens the watch page, fetches the page's own caption track from inside the page, then clicks Show transcript and reads the panel. YouTube answers a browser on a datacenter IP with the same captcha it gives a plain fetch, so on Vercel the rung needs the service; on a desktop or a self-hosted server a local Chromium is enough) → the audio-only stream downloads through the same app clients and takes the upload ladder below (`lib/video/youtube-audio.ts`): of the track the player plays (a video with several audio tracks — a dub, described audio — marks the original one default, and a transcript of any other would not match what the reader hears), the smallest stream under the upload cap, an indexed MP4 stream first — a transcript reads the words, and 48 kbps carries them as well as 128 at a third of the bytes — and a stream over the Whisper cap splits at its own segment boundaries (`lib/video/fmp4.ts`: the stream's `sidx` box gives every media segment's size and duration, and the init segment plus a run of whole segments is a valid fragmented MP4), so an hour-plus video transcribes through Groq Whisper at $0.04 an hour with no video tokens spent; a video whose smallest stream is over the cap says so → Gemini reads the video by URL, last (`GEMINI_API_KEY`; `gemini-3.7-flash`, then the `gemini-flash-latest` alias, so a retired model can never take the feature down; Google's own network, so YouTube's per-IP bot checks cannot touch it): about 100 tokens a second of video, three times what the audio costs, and one call over an hour of video outruns the ladder's clock, which is why it comes after the audio. YouTube bot-checks a server IP that asks too often, and every server-side rung shares that IP, so the ladder ends with the one rung that never depends on it: the reader pastes the transcript (below).

Gemini's timestamps are asked for as a clock ("M:SS", "H:MM:SS"), the form Gemini refers to moments of the media it reads in; a clock it reads off lands closer to the moment than a count of seconds it works out. They also drift with the length of what it reads in one call — minutes late by the end of an hour, every line then under the wrong moment — so transcription windows past about fifteen minutes of media (`GEMINI_TRANSCRIBE_SINGLE_CALL_TOKENS`): `countTokens` on the whole video and on one known minute gives the video's own token rate, and the two divide into a duration. Windows of fifteen minutes run together (six at a time, four hours maximum) so the wall clock is about one window rather than their sum. A window answers on its own clock or on the video's, and which varies per call; the earliest timestamp tells them apart — an answer on the video's clock never starts before the window does — and a window clock is shifted back onto the video's. One dead window leaves a gap instead of losing the transcript. The speakers pass keeps the 700k-token single call: it hears every voice at once, which is what keeps one id per voice honest.
- **Uploaded video or audio:** Deepgram Nova-3 (`DEEPGRAM_API_KEY`, `lib/video/deepgram.ts`; one synchronous call over the whole file, 2 GB allowed, $0.26 an hour with diarization included: every utterance comes back with its start, its end, and the voice that says it, from the audio itself — the one rung whose timestamps and speakers need no second pass, so it goes first; `detect_language`, `smart_format`, and `utterances` on) → Groq Whisper (`GROQ_API_KEY`; whisper-large-v3-turbo, $0.04/hour with a free tier — the best transcription quality per dollar among the rest) → OpenAI Whisper (`OPENAI_API_KEY`) → Gemini, inline when the bytes fit a request (≤14 MB) and through **Gemini's file store** when they do not. The Whisper rungs cap an upload at 25 MB; an MP3 past the cap splits at frame boundaries (lib/video/mp3.ts), and an indexed MP4 stream (the YouTube audio rung's) at its segment boundaries (lib/video/fmp4.ts), into under-cap chunks that transcribe a few at a time and shift back onto the audio's clock — hour-plus podcasts work; other containers cannot be cut safely and keep that cap.

  An hour of video is far past every one of those caps, so the file store is what carries it (`lib/video/gemini-files.ts`): Google's resumable upload puts the bytes there — 2 GB allowed, so the app's own 200 MB upload ceiling is the real cap — and the file is then referred to by its URI exactly as a YouTube URL is, which puts uploads on the same long-context path, windows and all (`geminiWindowed` serves both; only video can be asked for by time range, and an audio file long enough to need windows is past the upload ceiling anyway). A stored file lives 48 hours and its URI is kept on `VideoAsset.geminiFileUri`, so a retry inside that window skips the upload — sending an hour of media is the slow part of a run, and a run that lost the function's clock should not repeat it. The upload goes through the app's proxy-aware fetch like every other outbound call. Without `GEMINI_API_KEY` the 25 MB cap still ends it, and the reason says which key would lift it.

Transcription runs at low media resolution throughout — it needs the audio, not the pixels.

**Speakers:** who says each line (`lib/video/speakers.ts`). A `TranscriptSegment` carries `speaker` when its rung told the voices apart: Deepgram's utterances do, and a line never joins two voices (`groupSegments` closes a line where the speaker changes). Such a transcript only needs names: `nameSpeakers` reads the cleaned lines as text, one call per 400 lines until every voice is named, and names the voices the conversation names; the media is not read again. Any other transcript takes `detectSpeakers`: after the lines are cleaned, one Gemini pass reads the recording again beside them, listens at each line's time and tells the voices apart by pitch, timbre, pace, and accent — in a video also by who is on camera with their mouth moving — with the conversation as a check (a question and its answer are two voices, a reply that says a name is addressed to that voice), and answers a speaker id per line plus the roster. Either way the ids land on `Block.speaker` and the roster on `VideoAsset.speakers` (`[{id, name}]`). Names come only from the recording: someone introduces themselves, a host names a guest, one person addresses another by name. A voice nobody names is numbered ("Speaker 2"), and the pass never guesses a name from the subject, the channel, or a person it recognizes — a wrong name on every line is worse than a number. One voice throughout stores no roster at all: a transcript with one speaker reads better with no name on it. A recording too long for one call takes windows in order, each carrying the roster forward so a voice keeps one id from the first window to the last; a window past the deadline stops the rest and leaves those lines unnamed rather than renaming a voice. The pass runs on what the ladder left of the function's clock and never blocks the lines: a failure stores the transcript unnamed. **Detect speakers** on the view bar runs it on its own (`POST /api/documents/[documentId]/speakers`, its own 300 seconds) — for a transcript that landed before, or one that was pasted. It rewrites the lines' speaker and nothing else: the words, the times, and the block ids stand, so every note, mark, and link anchored to a line survives. Clicking a name on a row renames that voice (`PATCH`, same route); the id stays, so every line it says follows at once.

**Ranges:** every rung's segments land in `normalizeSegments` (lib/video/segments.ts) before anything reads them — trimmed, in start order, and pulled apart: a segment running past the next segment's start ends where that one begins. YouTube's auto-captions overlap by seconds, because a cue is written for a rolling two-line caption rather than for a transcript, and an overlapping range makes every consumer read the wrong line for a moment — the read-along highlight, the anchor a note lands on, the lines a range question reads. A real gap between two segments is left alone: nothing is spoken in it. Lines are then cut out of the segments (`groupSegments`), so lines never overlap either.

**Cleanup:** before the blocks are written, every transcript (all sources) is cleaned line by line — filler words (um, uh, er), stutters, immediate word repeats, and false starts removed; punctuation and casing fixed — so the transcript reads like written prose. Gemini cleans when a key is set (lib/video/tidy.ts; batch calls, same line count in and out, never a paraphrase); a deterministic rules pass is the keyless fallback. A cleaned line has to be its own line's words: cleanup takes words out and never puts new ones in, so every cleaned line is checked on its own (`lineIsSound`) — its words must read as a subsequence of the input line's words, with a small allowance for a spelling the model normalized — and a line that fails keeps the rules cleanup of its input while the rest of the batch stands; a model that kept the line count but moved words between lines would leave those lines under the wrong time. Time ranges and speakers never change; a line cleaned down to nothing drops.

Each rung fails with a plain reason; the ladder tries the next and reports every reason once all have run. `VideoAsset.transcriptStatus`: NONE → PENDING → READY | FAILED with the reason stored. Upload and playback work without any key; the transcript pane offers Transcribe and states plainly what is missing.

**Legs.** The ladder has a time budget — 200 seconds of the 300-second function, and a rung does not start with under 20 seconds left — but running out of it is never a failure: FAILED is written only after every rung has actually run. When the clock runs out with rungs left, the ladder stops with `LadderOutOfTime`, the job stores the rungs tried so far and their reasons on `VideoAsset.transcriptTried` (`[{rung, reason}]`), keeps the status PENDING, and starts the attempt's next leg on a fresh function: on Vercel a request to the app's own transcribe route (`POST /api/documents/[documentId]/transcribe` with `x-transcribe-leg: 1` and `CRON_SECRET` as the bearer, at `APP_URL` or the deployment's `VERCEL_URL`), which answers at once and runs the leg in `after()`; off Vercel, the same job in the same process. A leg runs the ladder past the rungs already tried (`skip`); a rung that ran out of time inside its own work counts as not run and runs again. The transcript pane shows the rungs tried so far under Transcribing…. Retry and Transcribe again are new attempts and run the whole ladder from its first rung; so do the voice note and the assistant's media attachments, which take the same `transcribe` ladder inside their own request. A leg the app could not start (no `CRON_SECRET`, the request refused) runs in the leg that asked, and if the function ends under it the run reads as stale and offers Retry — never as FAILED with rungs untried.

**Pasted transcript:** when every rung failed, the pane offers Paste transcript beside Retry. The reader opens the video on YouTube, clicks ...more, clicks Show transcript, copies the lines, and pastes them; `POST /api/documents/[documentId]/transcript` (`{text}`) stores them. `lib/video/paste.ts` reads the panel's copy format (a time on one line, its words on the next), a time and its words on one line, and SRT and WebVTT cues; a segment without its own end runs to the next segment's start; text with no times is refused with the reason. The pasted lines take the same cleanup and block writes as a transcribed transcript.

**The transcript pane is article-shaped:** lines flow into first-line-indented paragraphs, split at speech gaps (or at length once a sentence ends), each paragraph opening with a seekable time chip. A line is still the unit: click to seek, hover for Comment / Explain / Open note, follow-along highlight during playback.

### Video derivations (same pipeline, §4)

- The cached document prefix tags timed blocks: `[block <id>] (TRANSCRIPT 12.4s–18.2s)`. One cache entry per video document, like every document.
- `FIND` — the video content reader. `{type: FIND, query}` → JSON `{matches: [{blockIds, explanation}]}`; the server resolves each match's blocks to a time range. Renders as cards with seek chips. "Add to notes" lands a `PENDING` note with a time source — never persisted without the user.
- `ASK` — a question about a range. Ask about a range in the tool bar (open to viewers too: it persists nothing) opens a card with a start and an end time — the current moment and five minutes on, or the end — and a question; `{type: ASK, question, video: {startTime, endTime}}` answers from the transcript lines inside the range (repeated in the prompt with their times; the whole timed transcript stays the cached prefix, so the answer can say where else the recording deals with it, and that it is outside the range). The answer streams as text into the card; "Add to notes" lands it as a `PENDING` note whose time source is the range. Needs the transcript.
- A question about a circled spot goes to the media pane's assistant (`/api/assistant/act` with `video`): the client captures the frame at that moment — from the file for an upload, from the storyboard sheets for a YouTube video — cropped to the drawn loop, and attaches it; the model reads the frame plus the timed transcript. Audio has no frame; the assistant works from the transcript alone. Explain on a circled spot, a whole frame, or a transcript line streams EXPLAIN with the time anchor (`/api/derive` with `video`, the same captured frame attached) and saves the explanation as an annotation at that moment, so it joins the overlay and Visual; Regenerate runs the same moment again and replaces it.
- `FORMALIZE` — the transcript rewritten, the media pane's two assistant skills. format `article`: a formal article for publishing the ideas — title, section headings, clean written prose, nothing invented — stored as `{title, markdown}` on `NotebookDocument.formalized` and shown under the player as the view bar's article view, with Open as document, Copy markdown, and Regenerate (overwrites, like summaries) on the bar. The article is its own document in the corpus (`lib/video/article-document.ts`), and the article view renders that document's blocks through the reader's interaction layer (`ReaderInteractions` with `embedded`, inside the transcript layer's scroller): the selection toolbar, marks, links, and edit mode work on the article in place, on video and audio alike. An embedded layer has no article menu, no Distill, no reading position, and no scroll box of its own; each layer takes only the selections whose blocks sit under its own root (`data-reader-root`), so the transcript layer around the article never answers a selection in the article. An article stored before it became a document renders as markdown until Open as document gives it one. format `notes`: personal bullet-point notes — topics in transcript order, each `{heading, bullets, blockIds}` — landing as one `PENDING` note per topic with a time source resolved from its blocks (§1: nothing enters notes without the user). Runs behind the DISTILL heartbeat stream; needs the transcript.

### The assistant on the media pane

An Assistant button in the tool bar opens a chat card under it (editor-gated, document scope — the model reads the whole timed transcript through `/api/assistant/act`). Facing video and audio content the card carries the two FORMALIZE skills as suggestion chips — "Formalize into an article" and "Formalize into bullet-point notes" — disabled until the transcript lands; typed questions answer in the chat. The chat executes no plan actions on media documents yet and says so when a plan proposes any. Send becomes Stop while a turn is running (closing the card stops it too, same as the reader's chat) — the sent message stays, no reply lands.

### Phase V1 — Upload, store, play
- Video files through the chunked upload path → Document + VIDEO block + VideoAsset → video pane with custom player.

## 12. Community

A corpus can be shared (Google Docs pattern). The owner (Notebook.userId) adds collaborators by email with a role; accounts key on the email (§2), so an invite works before the account exists. Everything inside the corpus — documents, notes, annotations, distillations, extractions, edits — is the shared surface; the derivation pipeline (§4) is unchanged.

### Data model additions

```prisma
model NotebookCollaborator {
  id         String     @id @default(cuid())
  notebookId String
  notebook   Notebook   @relation(fields: [notebookId], references: [id], onDelete: Cascade)
  email      String     // lowercase; invite works before the account exists
  role       CollabRole @default(EDITOR)
  addedById  String?
  createdAt  DateTime   @default(now())
  @@unique([notebookId, email])
}

enum CollabRole { EDITOR VIEWER }

model NotebookPresence {
  id         String   @id @default(cuid())
  notebookId String
  notebook   Notebook @relation(fields: [notebookId], references: [id], onDelete: Cascade)
  userId     String
  documentId String?  // the open document; null = the notes full page
  lastSeenAt DateTime @default(now())
  @@unique([notebookId, userId])
}
```

Plus columns: `Notebook.rev Int` (change counter), `Note.createdById String?`, `BlockEdit.userId String?`, `User.symbol String` and `User.color String` (the badge).

### Roles

| Role | Held by | Can |
|---|---|---|
| owner | Notebook.userId | everything, plus delete the corpus and manage sharing |
| editor | CollabRole EDITOR | read and write: notes, sections, annotations, derivations, block edits, links, documents |
| viewer | CollabRole VIEWER | read only; FIND (persists nothing) is the one derivation open to viewers |

Enforcement is server-side in `lib/collab.ts`: `notebookAccess(notebookId, min)` for corpus routes, `documentAccess(documentId, min)` for document routes (best role across the corpora the document is attached to), `sectionAccess`/`noteAccess` resolving objects to their corpus. A non-member answers 404 (existence undisclosed); a member below the required role answers 403. The UI mirrors the same rule: viewers get no selection popover, no edit mode, no assistant, no write buttons, and a "Viewing only" badge.

### Attribution

Every write is labeled with its author: `Note.createdById` (manual notes, highlights, comments, EXPLAIN/SIMPLIFY annotations, assistant output), `BlockEdit.userId` (text edits, formats, styles, links, block add/remove), `createdById` inside stored distillations and extractions. The author renders as a person badge — picture, or symbol on color (`lib/person.ts`; defaults: first letter of the name, color hashed from the account id) — on note cards, annotation cards, the Edits panel, the extract page, and the match card. Labels render only on shared corpora; solo work stays unlabeled.

### Live sync

Every write bumps `Notebook.rev` (document writes bump every corpus the document is attached to). Open workspaces poll `GET /api/notebooks/[id]/sync` every 4 seconds: the call stamps the caller's `NotebookPresence` row and answers `{rev, people}` — who else has the corpus open (25-second window). When the rev moves, the client refreshes the page — deferred while an input, textarea, or editable block has focus or a selection is open, so typing is never clobbered. Presence renders as badges in the workspace header.

### Replies

Every note (annotations included — a highlight, comment, explanation, or assistant conversation is a note), every edit in the Edits panel, and every link carries a discussion: `Reply` rows (`noteId`, `blockEditId`, or `docLinkId`; author; content), flat, oldest first. Collaborators comment on each other's work there. Editors reply; viewers read; a reply deletes by its author or the owner. Open replies always render under their card; any editor resolves a reply (`resolvedById`; `PATCH /api/replies/[replyId]`), and resolved ones collapse behind a count. The Reply affordance appears for any editor once sign-in is on, shared or not: a reply on one's own note is a dated update under it. A collapsed note's row counts its replies beside its source count. `POST /api/replies`, `DELETE /api/replies/[replyId]`; replies bump the rev like every write.

### Attribution rule

On a shared project every note says who wrote it — the author's badge and name under the note's text, the badge alone on a collapsed row — one's own notes included, so a collaborator reads the author at a glance. Annotations and edits keep the lighter rule: the author label marks the other person's work, and one's own render default, unlabeled. Every action still carries its author — the History panel lists everyone's, your own included.

### History

The History panel (the clock-rewind button beside Share) is the corpus's whole record: every edit and every deletion, newest first, each entry signed. It merges `BlockEdit` rows across the attached documents (text edits, formats, styles, links, paragraph add/remove) with `NotebookEvent` rows for what BlockEdit cannot see — note removals (content snapshot), section removals, document detachments — recorded at delete time with the deleter's id. A person's badge in the panel filters the feed to their actions. The rail's per-document Edits tab keeps the pencil icon; the clock-rewind icon is the corpus history's.

**Small edits.** A run of small edits — a typo fixed, a comma moved, a word recased, a style toggled — by one person in one document folds into one row, "n small edits", with Show to open it (`components/collab/history-control.tsx`). What counts as small (`lib/history/trivial.ts`): code decides the clear cases from the edit's before and after — a style edit; nothing but whitespace, punctuation, or case changed; two characters or fewer changed — and, with Jev configured, Jev decides the rest that changed 120 characters or fewer, one choice per edit, typo or substantive (0.6); a longer change is substantive without asking. The verdict is written to the edit's `meta.trivial`, so an edit is judged once, on the first open of the project after it.

Every note has its own history too: History, beside Copy and Delete under an accepted note, opens the list of who wrote the note and every edit of its text since — newest first, each signed and dated, each holding the text it left (`NoteEdit` rows; `GET /api/notes/[noteId]/edits`, with the people who appear). Auto-save writes every few keystrokes, so a save within ten minutes of the same person's last edit updates that edit instead of adding one: one sitting is one entry (`lib/notes/edits.ts`, called from the note PATCH and from merge). Show text opens a version; Restore, for editors, makes it the note's text again — an edit like any other, so it lands in the history. The local reader's edits carry no account. Version history (§29) is the blank document's own record and stands apart from History; a restore lands in History as the paragraphs it adds, removes, and retypes, like any save.

### Stale tabs

Cookies are per browser, not per tab: signing out or switching accounts in one tab changes every tab's cookies. Sign-in therefore also sets a readable account cookie (`dissect-account`, the account id — grants nothing; the session cookie alone authorizes). Account-scoped pages mount an account guard that latches the account the page was rendered for, watches the cookie (on focus, on visibility, every 5 seconds), confirms a mismatch against `GET /api/auth/account` (which also re-stamps the cookie, healing sessions from before it existed), and freezes the tab with an account-changed notice instead of silently becoming the new account. Live sync stops polling on mismatch so a stale tab never stamps presence or refreshes as someone else. `api()` sends the tab's rendered account as a header; the middleware answers 409 when it no longer matches the cookie, so a stale tab's write can never land as the wrong account.

### Sharing surfaces

- Share dialog in the workspace header: the owner adds by email with a role, re-roles, removes; a collaborator sees the list and can leave. `GET/POST/DELETE /api/notebooks/[id]/collaborators`.
- The dashboard shows "Shared with you": the corpora shared with the account, with the owner's name and the role. Editors can rename from the card menu; only Leave replaces Delete.
- Storage (Settings, TIERS.md): under the plan card, one bar for the account's files — the documents attached to its projects (their stored bytes), the images it dropped or its parses captured, and the videos of its documents, each its own color (`lib/storage.ts`, `components/storage-bar.tsx`) — against the tier's storage limit (`STORAGE_LIMIT_BYTES` in `lib/tiers.ts`, per tier, per account), with one line saying how much is used. No limit is set yet: the bar stays empty and the line says so; setting the number fills it. Nothing gates on it yet.
- The profile (Settings): picture (uploaded, resized client-side to a small JPEG data URL, stored on `User.picture`), name, symbol, color, and the one Background field (`PUT /api/account`, `PUT /api/profile`). Sign-in fills name and picture only when empty — it never overwrites what the person set. Service/env status lives on `/admin`, not in Settings. Settings also shows Connections — the services connected to the account and what each reaches: sign-in (the email, and whether a password is set) and Google Drive (§14: link, what the stored grant reaches, Link again for all files, Unlink) — and Your data: every stored field and count about the account, read-only (`lib/account-data.ts`: email, name, picture, password, created and last-active dates, live sessions, background, projects, documents, notes, digests, clicks, AI calls, feedback, notifications, the Drive grant, what stays in the browser, and the hosting provider's logs), with a link to the Privacy Policy and the deletion contact.

## 13. Connections

Every piece of content in a corpus should connect. Two mechanisms:

### Recommended links

**Recommend links** in the graph runs the scan (`POST /api/notebooks/[notebookId]/connect`, `lib/connect.ts`, prompts in `/lib/prompts/connect.ts`, model `CONNECT_MODEL` at `CONNECT_EFFORT`): every document of the project, oldest first, read against the others, video transcripts included. It is the only way links are proposed — nothing scans when a document joins a project. The scan reads whole documents against whole documents, which is the most expensive thing the app does to a project, and a document being added is not a request for it. One press is one run: at most 8 documents and 250 seconds, whichever ends first, and the notice says how many documents the run did not reach so the reader can run it again. An account gets `LINK_SCAN_RUNS_PER_MONTH` (3) runs a calendar month, counted as `LinkScanRun` rows; the button carries the number left and the run is recorded before the scan starts, so a run that fails still spends its place — its model calls were paid for. For each document scanned, the two passes below. Two passes, both over whole documents. The scan reads the new document whole (200,000 characters, cut at a block boundary with a declared marker past that) against every other document whole (the same cap each, 480,000 across them; a document past that is declared left out), with the project context beside them — the reader's background (the profile) and the reader's accepted notes across the project (30,000 characters) — and proposes at most 8 links. A link holds only when both passages say the same specific thing: the same claim, finding, number, method, entity, or event, or a direct contradiction; topic alone, a shared common word, or a passing mention never connects; the notes and background rank connections, never create them. Then the check: one call per other document the candidates point at, both documents whole with the same context, keeping only the links that hold and rewriting a vague reason to name the specific thing both passages say; a check that fails to answer keeps its candidates as proposed, a failed call being no evidence against a link. The scan reads the content only — the article text or the transcript; document titles are never sent to the model, so two documents with similar titles but unrelated content do not connect. Each kept link becomes a `DocLink` with `recommended: true`, a `reason`, and both quotes resolved verbatim against the real blocks (unresolvable output drops, SPEC.md §4 discipline). At most 6 per document scanned; duplicates skip.

A recommended link paints nowhere until accepted — the user approves everything (§1). It lives in the Graph, under its Recommended links list (folded beside the canvas; every recommended link of the project, newest first): the reason, both quotes, the two documents, the author badge, a reply thread, and Accept / Dismiss. The Annotations panel lists accepted links only. Accept clears `recommended`, paints the link, and records a LINK_ADD by its accepter; Dismiss deletes it without a history entry. A recommended link is also accepted or dismissed where a curve lists its links (`graph-view.tsx`): the row carries the "Recommended link, not yet accepted" pill with Accept and Dismiss beside it for an editor; the row answers at once and the refresh brings the graph's own data.

### Corpus extraction

Extract's second scope (code: corpus DISTILL): the reader asks the whole corpus one question (`POST /api/derive` with `type: DISTILL, scope: "corpus"`, no documentId). The corpus rides as one cacheable system message — every document rendered `[document <id>] "title"` then block lines, later documents cut whole with a declared marker past the budget — and the model returns the same DISTILL quote contract; the server maps each quote to its document by block id (block ids are unique across the corpus) and stores the distillation on `Notebook.distillations`, newest first, capped at 20. The corpus extract page (Distill tab → "Extract from the project") renders each quote under its document's title chip; clicking a quote opens that document; Add to notes lands the quote `PENDING` with a source in its own document — one distillation, sources across the corpus. Delete goes through `PATCH /api/notebooks/[id]` `removeDistillationId`. Quotes heal at render and orphan visibly (§5).

### Graph

The Graph (rail button; full-screen overlay; `reactflow`, the release-edu tree pattern) draws the corpus as a connected whole: every attached document a node, every linked pair one curve. A link with both ends in the same document is a loop on that node: a curve that leaves the node's top and returns to it, like a loop in a graph of graph theory, with the same width, tone, count pill, and link list as a curve between two documents. The more links between two documents, the thicker and deeper the curve: width and clay depth both scale with the count — one link a fine light line, eight or more the widest and darkest — and the tone is deepest at the curve's middle. Hovering a curve, or clicking it to pin, opens the pair's link list at the curve: every link's description (the reader's, or the AI's reason for a recommended link) and both quotes; a recommended one is marked. A click on a link expands it in place and pins the curve (`components/graph/link-detail.tsx`): why the link was made — the reason in full — then each end as its own panel: the document's title, the passage the quote sits in with the quote lit (the block's words before and after it, 260 characters each side at most; the quote alone when the block is gone), and Open in reader, which opens that document on the link (`?doc=&link=`). The Recommended links list beside the canvas expands a link the same way; Accept and Dismiss stay on the card. A pair held together only by recommended links draws dashed until one is accepted — the Recommended links list is where that happens. Each end's passage travels with the link data (`fromBlockText`, `toBlockText` on `GraphEdgeLink` and `RecommendedLinkView`, read by `documentsGraph` in `lib/graph/view.ts`). Nodes drag; click one to open the document; ⇧-click, or a click while Stitch is picking, selects it for Stitch (§22); pan and zoom Obsidian-style. Stitch and Generated content live in the overlay too (§22). Node, edge, and recommended-link data come from the workspace page (`GraphNode`, `GraphEdge`, `RecommendedLinkView` in `lib/types.ts`); reactflow lazy-loads when the overlay opens.

## 14. Google Drive upload

A new way to add a document: pick it from Google Drive instead of the local disk. The reader's Google account is already the common case (§2's auth), so Drive access rides the same OAuth client.

- **Grant:** the client asks for a Drive token with Google Identity Services and opens the Google Picker with it. `GOOGLE_DRIVE_ACCESS` picks the scope every grant asks for (`lib/drive/types.ts`): `all` (default) is `drive.readonly` — read access to every file the account can read, so any picked file and any pasted Drive link imports. Google calls this scope restricted: the deployer lists it on the OAuth consent screen's Data access page, and until Google verifies the app (brand verification plus a CASA security assessment) the consent shows Google's unverified-app warning, with a lifetime cap of 100 users who saw it; a consent screen still in Testing status grants it to its test users only and expires the grant after seven days (the mint then reports it revoked and the link clears). `picked` is `drive.file` — the files the reader picks in the Google Picker only, non-sensitive, no verification. Neither scope writes to Drive. The picker always carries the Cloud project number (`setAppId`, the numeric prefix of the OAuth client id): without it a `drive.file` grant never reaches the picked file and Drive answers 404 for every pick. A per-visit token lives in the browser only, for one picking session; nothing about that grant is written to the database. `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` (§2) is reused; the deployer additionally lists this app's origin under that client's "Authorized JavaScript origins" in the Google Cloud console. `GOOGLE_PICKER_API_KEY` (optional, a Picker-only API key) improves file previews in the picker; the feature works with `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` alone. `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` unset = the option stays hidden, the same DUAL MODE as sign-in (§2).
- **Link Google Drive (the durable grant):** with Google sign-in configured, an account can link Google Drive once — the same hand-rolled code flow as sign-in (`GET /api/drive/link` → consent → `/api/auth/callback`, the one redirect URI registered on the OAuth client — Google rejects any other with redirect_uri_mismatch — where the Drive state cookie tells the link apart from a sign-in), asking the configured scope with offline access; the refresh token and the scope Google granted land on `User.driveRefreshToken` and `User.driveScope` (a grant stored before the scope was counts as picked files only). On an account that can link but has not, Add from Google Drive links first (full-page consent, back to the workspace with `?drive=linked`, then the picker opens) instead of asking the browser for a per-visit token. Linked, the picker gets its token from `POST /api/drive/token` (minted server-side; a revoked grant clears itself there) — no consent popup per visit — and a **pasted Drive or Docs link** imports server-side through the same grant: `parseDriveFileId` recognizes the link, `/api/drive/import` takes a bare `fileId`, reads name and mimeType from Drive metadata, and mints the token itself (an all-files grant reaches any file the account can read; a picked-files grant reaches picked files only, and Drive's refusal reports as that — pick it from the Drive tab, or link again for all files — never as "private or removed"). Unlinked, a pasted Drive link answers with a pointer to the Drive tab, never a parse failure. Link lives in Settings under Connections (the Google Drive row: what the stored grant reaches, Link again for all files when it reaches picked files only while the deployment asks for all, Unlink = revoke + clear); the Drive tab says the first pick links and what the grant reaches. The local reader (sign-in off) has no account row and keeps the per-visit grant.
- **The picker's views:** the five tabs Google Drive itself shows in its sidebar, in this order — My Drive (the account's own Drive from its root, `setParent("root")`, folders to walk into), Shared with me (`setOwnedByMe(false)`), Recent (files only, newest first), Starred (`setStarred(true)`), Shared drives (`setEnableDrives(true)`). The picker opens on My Drive and names each tab from how its view is set. One view cannot be every tab: with `setEnableDrives(true)` Google puts only shared drives in that view, and a view with no parent lists the whole Drive flat, newest first — with folders on, that is every folder of the Drive as a wall of stale folders, with the reader's own Drive nowhere. Folders are in the picker's mime filter (`DRIVE_FOLDER_MIME_TYPE`) — the filter is a whitelist over every item a view lists, so without it a Drive of folders lists as empty — and stay unselectable: a folder is walked into, never imported. Recent's filter leaves the folder type out, so no folder lists there.
- **One ingest path, three sources:** a picked file is sniffed by mime type (`lib/drive/types.ts`) into the same handlers a local upload already uses — Drive is a new source, never a new parser (§4's discipline extended to ingest):
  - Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Drawings export to PDF through Drive's own conversion (`files.export`, capped at 10 MB by Drive), then ingest exactly like an uploaded PDF.
  - A PDF already in Drive downloads (`files.get?alt=media`) and ingests exactly like an uploaded PDF — Import PDF judgment, the pages directive, and conversion included (§16).
  - A video or audio file downloads the same way and ingests exactly like a direct video/audio upload (§11) — same chunked storage, same transcription.
  - Anything else (images, Forms, raw .docx/.xlsx/.pptx, …) is declined with a plain reason; the picker's own mime filter keeps most of these from being selected at all.
- **Provenance and dedupe:** a picked video or audio file's Drive download URL (no token in it) becomes the document's `sourceUrl`, so re-picking the same file dedupes like a re-added web link (§11's own dedupe). A picked PDF, Doc, Sheet, or Slide dedupes by the downloaded bytes' hash instead, the same as any other PDF upload — Drive gives no stable pre-download key for those.
- **Surface:** "Add from Google Drive" is a small button beside the add-document dialog's drop zone and URL box (§15). Picking queues the picks in the dialog beside the files and links already there (`{kind: "drive-file", token, file}`), and Continue hands the queue to the upload box, one `/api/drive/import` request per pick; the return from Link Google Drive opens the picker on its own and its picks go straight to the box. Every download fetches with `supportsAllDrives` (without it Drive answers 404 for every file on a shared drive, which the picker lists); the stored `sourceUrl` stays the plain download URL, so dedupe keeps working. A refusal names Google's own reason after the app's line ("Google Drive says: …"), and the Drive API being off on the Cloud project has its own line. Drive adds need the server and never queue offline.

## 15. The upload box

Files, a URL, and a blank document are the three ways in. The add-document dialog (opened by the dashed +) is one big drop-or-choose space for files — PDF, image, video, audio, Markdown, one accept list, no kind to pick — with a URL box beneath it, and two small buttons off to the side, Add from Google Drive and Library (attaching an existing document, not an upload, opens no box). Dropping, choosing, or submitting a URL hands off at once to the upload box (`components/reader/upload-assistant.tsx`): no review, no format pick, no instructions to answer first — it imports right away, one streamed request per file or Drive pick (a URL is always one document), progress in place. While an add runs the box can be hidden — ✕, Escape, or a click outside — and the add runs on: the document bar shows a small running pill (a spinner and "Adding {title}…") that brings the box back, and the box comes back on its own when an add ends with something to read (a failure, a lost figure). One box at a time: an add that arrives while one runs waits in the document bar (`pending`) and starts when the running box closes, so neither replaces the other and no queued item is lost; each request mounts a fresh box (keyed by a run counter).

Every add ends with a finishing step before the document opens, so it opens complete — text, figures, links, and glossary in place instead of filling in after the open: `GET /api/documents/{id}/finish` says what is left: `images`, every visual the reader will request — PDF figure and page renders, remote figures in figure and table html. Nothing else is left, since the glossary is built when the reader opens it and links when the reader asks for them (§13), then the box loads every image once into the browser's cache (`lib/finish.ts`: six at a time, 30 s each, three minutes in all), and only then closes and opens the document. An add that has run 20 s opens its first document before the finishing step is done, when the document reads well — saved, with at least 90% of its captions carrying their figure (the save stage's figure check): the box hides, the running pill reads "Finishing {title}…" while the figures load on, clicking it shows the progress, and the close that ends the add refreshes the open document instead of opening it again; a shorter add opens complete. The progress card shows the add's elapsed time beside its step count. A pasted Drive link on a linked account imports directly (§14); the reader's media-figure toast and re-parse keep the floating progress card.

**A blank document.** Blank document, the first small button under the queue, is for a document the reader writes here: `POST /api/documents/blank` `{notebookId, title}` (editor) creates a document with no file and no source, titled Untitled document, holding one empty PARAGRAPH block with `originalText: ""` (user-authored, like an inserted paragraph), attaches it, and answers `{id, title}` — no box, since there is nothing to import or finish. The dialog closes and the document opens with `edit=1` in the URL: the reader mounts in edit mode (reader-interactions.tsx; viewers, transcripts, and embedded layers never do) with the caret in the first block, and the edit toolbar (§6) does the rest — headings, lists, bold, italic, underline, indent, undo. Done or Esc leaves edit mode as always; an empty paragraph keeps a line's height in reading mode, so a double-click finds it again. The title edits in place like any document's. Re-parse does not apply: the document has nothing to parse again.

**The wall check.** With Jev configured (`lib/jev.ts`), a URL import judges the fetched page before any model pass reads it (`lib/parse/page-kind.ts`): one choice — paper, news, blog, docs, forum, consent wall, sign-in wall, paywall, error page — from the title and a sample of the extracted text. A wall at confidence 0.7 or above stops the add with the reason (`FetchPageError` "wall": the page answered with a consent, sign-in, subscription, or error page, not the article; save it as a PDF). The rule around Jev is code: a page with more than 4,000 characters of text is never a wall, whatever Jev says. The kind of an article is logged and not stored; nothing branches on it yet.

**The caption check.** The figure audit's label regex knows "Figure 2", "Table 4", "图 3". With Jev configured, every short text block the regex passed over that stands with no figure beside it is asked one yes/no — is this line a caption (`auditFiguresWithJev`, `lib/parse/figure-audit.ts`) — and the ones Jev is sure of (0.8) count as captions without their figure in the save stage's report, so the done line and the box's stay say so. The reader's figure marks (`captionGaps`) keep to the regex: a browser render can only fetch a figure it can name.

**The queue.** The dialog queues links, files, and Drive picks of every kind together (`UploadItem` in upload-assistant.tsx): Enter after a link parses it out into the queue (several links pasted at once queue each; a YouTube or media link queues as a video; a Google Drive link imports on its own, §14), dropping or choosing files queues them, Add from Google Drive queues the picks (§14), and each queued item shows with ✕ until Continue hands the queue to the box. One link, files alone, or Drive picks alone go to the box as before; a mixed queue, or several links, is a batch (`{kind: "batch", items}`), which the box adds one request at a time in queue order, `File i of n` progress, one failure never killing the batch.

**Two or more documents.** An add that will land two or more documents — a batch, several files, several Drive picks — shows what it will add and waits for Add; each document opens on its own page, the same as an add of one. An add that lands one document imports right away.

**Figure check.** Before the save, the vision check compares the page and the reader's rendering as pictures and fixes the figures the pictures show placed wrong (§2). The save stage then audits the parsed blocks against the figures the page describes (`lib/parse/figure-audit.ts`, deterministic): a block that opens like a caption ("Figure 4", "Table 2", "图 3") with no figure beside it is a figure the parse did not load. The box's done line states the verification ("7 figures loaded · every caption has its figure") for a single add; a caption without a figure keeps the box open until Close. The media check rides beside it (`ParsedDocument.mediaCheck`, §2): how many images, videos, and charts the page's content holds and the names of those the parse did not load; the done line states it, and the document bar shows one line after the automatic re-parse when media is left unloaded. A page that draws its figures with scripts (an empty chart svg in the static HTML) needs a browser to render before the parse (`lib/parse/render-page.ts`; `BROWSER_WS_ENDPOINT` or `CHROMIUM_PATH`, the §11 browser); a re-parse carries the same check on its save stage (`scriptedFigures` beside the counts), and the document bar shows one line after the automatic re-parse when a caption is left without its figure — naming the figure, and the browser to configure when scripts draw it. The browser render reports what it did (`RenderReport` in `lib/parse/render-page.ts`: whether one ran, and why it did not deliver — the connection refused, the time up, the chart capture failed, a loop not found in the time sampled): the report rides with the save stage as `renderError`, in the document bar's line, and on the document as `Document.figureRenderAt` and `figureRenderError`. In the reader, every caption left without its figure carries the figure's place above it (`components/reader/figure-capture.tsx`): while a render runs, a small moving picture and "Unitos is moving Figure 4 over…" (the document bar shows the same pill); when it failed, why, with Try again; when no browser is configured, the variable to set. Where a browser is configured and no render has run for the document, opening it runs one — the same silent re-parse the parser-version upgrade runs, once per document until Try again — so a browser configured after the add brings the figures over on the next open. A Browserless endpoint that sets no `timeout` is tried with 300 s, then 120 s (the free plan's maximum; a timeout past the plan's is refused with 400), then as given (`lib/browser.ts`): the service ends a session at 60 s otherwise. The browser reports its session's length (`sessionLengthOf`), the capture's deadline is the earlier of its budget and the session's end, and the recording measures its frames as it goes: a loop that will not be done in time at a frame per step is recorded every second, third, or fourth step at the matching delay — a coarser GIF rather than none. A figure's video is the page's own file and often does not arrive — the host refuses a request from another origin, the link has expired, the reader is offline — and a video that does not load draws as nothing, so the caption reads as if the clip were never there: every video of a rendered figure gets its place (`components/reader/figure-media.tsx`), a spinner and "Loading the video…" while it loads, the film icon, "This figure is a video. It is not loading." and Try again when it does not. The video hides while its place stands and takes its own room back the moment its metadata is in. The place is `[data-anchor-skip]`, so the block's DOM text stays exactly the caption (§5). Nothing is asked for before the reader comes to it: an article's clips and gifs are the page's own files, often megabytes each, and a browser handed them all at once starts them all at once — the figure on screen then arrives last. The parse stores `<video preload="none">` (an autoplay loop's autoplay moves to `data-autoplay`) and `<img loading="lazy" decoding="async">`, and the reader starts each video when it comes within 800px of the viewport, putting autoplay back there; a document parsed before this carries no `preload="none"` and starts as it always did, and its images are given the two attributes as the html renders. The place's own waits — the spinner after 1.2s, not-loading after 15s — start when the video starts loading, never before.

Import PDF still judges every PDF (§16), and an image always imports as a handwritten page — both decided automatically, server-side, on the parsed content alone; nothing in the box steers them.

**The range step.** A video or audio file has one step the box waits on: once its bytes are up and before the add completes, the box plays the file from the browser's own copy (`components/reader/media-range.tsx`) and shows two handles over its length; the reader moves them to keep part of the recording, hearing the preview seek to the handle, and presses Import {start}–{end}, or Import the whole recording. The part goes to `POST /api/uploads/complete` as `clipStart` and `clipEnd` (seconds) and is stored on `VideoAsset.clipStart`/`clipEnd`; null is the whole. The file is stored whole and plays whole. The transcription keeps the lines inside the part alone (`clipSegments`, `lib/video/segments.ts`, applied before the lines are cut, so Transcribe again and a pasted transcript keep to it too), so the assistant, Find, Ask, Formalize, and every derivation read that part and nothing else. A media link, a YouTube link, and a Drive pick have no step: the box has no copy to play. A re-upload that dedupes keeps the existing document's part.

## 16. Handwritten documents

Import PDF judges each PDF (AI judgment, not a file-type rule): a computer-text article parses to text blocks as before; rough handwritten notes and drawings become a **handwritten document** — the pages themselves render in the reader, with two tools on them: conversion to text and Circle & ask. This amends §9's "no scanned-PDF OCR": image-only PDFs now land usefully as handwritten documents instead of empty articles.

### Classification (in `ingestPdf`, every PDF path — upload, chunked, Drive)

1. Parse the text layer as always. Article-scale yield (≥250 chars/page) that reads like language = article, no model call. A junk text layer — handwriting apps embed garbled recognition output ("rightrightfracleftleft…"; detected as ≥15% of characters in unbroken 25+ letter-digit runs, URLs excluded) — never counts as article yield.
2. Below that, or on junk, render sample pages (first, middle, last) and ask a vision model (`CLASSIFY_MODEL`, prompt in `/lib/prompts/classify.ts`): typeset computer text → article; handwritten notes, drawings, sketches, or scanned pages whose content the text layer missed or garbled → handwritten.
3. No key or a failed call: junk = handwritten; else yield decides alone (<40 chars/page = handwritten).
4. The upload box (§15) sends no directives of its own — every PDF add goes through the judgment above. `pages` and `convert` still exist as fields on every PDF add route (`/api/documents` multipart, `/api/uploads/complete`, `/api/drive/import`), defaulting to `pages: false, convert: true` — the as-is import format (`pages: true, convert: false`) stays reachable by an API caller that sets them explicitly, just not from the reader-facing box.

Every document's menu carries Re-parse document: a text document parses its file or URL again with the current parser (`POST /api/documents/[documentId]/reparse`), a handwritten document re-makes its pages and converts again, and a video or audio document transcribes again (`POST /api/documents/[documentId]/transcribe`; the lines are replaced). A document with no file and no URL — pasted text, a generated document — shows the row disabled with the reason. The row is one of three on every document's menu, with Print document and Delete document (the document leaves the project and the library, `DELETE /api/documents/[documentId]`; refused while notes cite it). The judgment can be wrong, so Re-parse on a PDF asks which shape first: the row folds open to two choices — Handwritten pages (`{as: "handwritten"}`: the PDF stays as its pages, drawn as they are, and the handwriting converts again) and Computer text (`{as: "article"}`: the text reads into blocks with the current parser) — with the document's current shape marked; the page reads which stored files are PDFs from their first bytes (`AttachedDocument.pdf`), so a Markdown file's row runs at once. The route takes the choice as `POST /api/documents/[documentId]/reparse {as}`. Anchors on replaced blocks re-resolve by quote or orphan visibly (§5).

### Data model

- `Document.handwritten Boolean` plus `conversionStatus ConversionStatus` (NONE → PENDING → READY | FAILED, the transcript pattern; OFF = the reader said not to convert — nothing auto-starts, the strip offers Convert to text), `conversionError`, `conversionStartedAt`. The PDF bytes stay in `Document.fileData`.
- `BlockType` gains `PAGE`: one block per PDF page at orders 0…n−1, `Block.page` the 1-based page, `Block.text` "Page N" (so chips, search, and the digest read well). `GET /api/documents/[documentId]/page/[blockId]` serves the page image.
- **Page image.** Each PAGE block's page renders once and is kept: `PageImage` (one row per PAGE block: `width`, `height`, `data` — JPEG at 1400px wide). The sizes land with the document (`storePageSizes`, in `ingestPdf` and the handwritten re-parse); the renders fill in after the response (`renderPageImages`, in `after()` on every PDF add route and the re-parse route); the page image route renders any page still without one on its first request and keeps it. The reader's `<img>` carries the stored width and height, so every page keeps its shape before its bytes arrive and the browser loads pages lazily, near the viewport only. A document from before page images gets its sizes on the reader's next open (`pageSizesFor`) and its renders on request. A re-parse recreates the blocks, and the cascade drops the rows with them.
- A **page anchor** is a `Source` with `region` set (the §11 percent-coordinate shape) on a PAGE block, offsets 0/0, `quotedText` "Page N". It skips the text ladder — pages never change — and orphans only when the PAGE block is gone (shape switch).
- A **PDF figure** is a FIGURE block with `page` set and, when the parse found the figure's place on the page, `region` (the same shape): the text of a vector chart or a display equation, or the blank area above a "Figure N" caption. `GET /api/documents/[documentId]/figure/[blockId]` renders the page and crops it to the region; without a region it serves the whole page, as before. The caption is the block's text and its only DOM text (§5); Explain attaches the same crop.

### Conversion (pages → text blocks)

Conversion starts on its own when a handwritten document is added with `conversionStatus: NONE` — the text is the point; OFF (the reader's "do not convert") starts nothing — and `POST /api/documents/[documentId]/convert` runs the same job for Retry, Convert again, and the OFF strip's Convert to text (`lib/handwritten/convert.ts`, prompt in `/lib/prompts/convert.ts`, model `CONVERT_MODEL`). Pages render to images and transcribe in batches that run together, so the wall clock is about one batch. The model transcribes the author's wording verbatim and imitates the notes' formatting: headings by prominence, lists with the reader's own markers, tables with the invisible cell separators (§5 holds inside converted tables), standalone math as EQUATION TeX, drawings as one-sentence bracketed descriptions, illegible words as "[illegible]" — never a guess. Converted blocks land after the PAGE blocks, each stamped with the page it came from; the reader shows pages first, then the converted text as a normal article — anchors, the selection popover, and every derivation work on it. Convert again deletes only the non-PAGE blocks, so page anchors never move. One failed batch fails the run with its reason — a partial text never lands silently; past 60 pages the cut is declared in a final paragraph.

The strip under the pages shows the status: Converting…, the failure reason with Retry, or the Converted text header with Convert again. A PENDING older than 10 minutes is a dead run and may start again.

### Images

An image adds like a PDF — dropped in the upload box, picked from its drop zone, or queued offline — and imports as one handwritten page. Before ingest the server wraps it into a one-page PDF (`lib/handwritten/image-pdf.ts`; png, jpg, gif, webp, bmp, told apart by their bytes in `lib/handwritten/image.ts`, the one definition the drop filter, the upload box's file input, and the documents route share) and runs `ingestPdf` with `pages` forced true server-side — no classification call, no directive to send — one PAGE block, the page image, Circle & ask, and conversion offered after, exactly as a scanned page — a new source, never a new parser. The stored bytes are the PDF, so dedupe, the page image route, re-parse, and the shape switch read them as they read any PDF. A JPEG embeds as it is (DCTDecode; baseline or progressive, 8-bit, gray or RGB), and its EXIF orientation becomes the page's `/Rotate`, so a phone photo shows upright; any other format, and a JPEG outside that set, decodes through `@napi-rs/canvas` onto a white ground and stores pixel for pixel (Flate) up to 6 MP, as JPEG above that, drawn no larger than 24 MP. The page fits inside Letter and never upscales — the size sets the aspect only; every render scales to its own width. Google Drive still declines images (§14).

### Images dropped into a note or into the reader

An image dropped on a note goes into the note; an image dropped on a paragraph while the reader is in edit mode goes into the article, as a figure right after that paragraph. A link dropped on a note — from a page, a bookmark, or the address bar — goes into the note too (`lib/note-links.ts`, `useNoteDrop` in use-note-drop.ts): on its own line as `[title](url)`, the anchor's text or the address as its title, and the rendered note draws a link on a line of its own as a link card (`rehypeLinkCards`, markdown.tsx) — the link's text, the site under it, the row a target. A photo fits the note (`.note-image`): the note's width at most, a capped height, never stretched past its own size, centered, on display and in the editor alike; the reader's own width from the corner handle still wins. A note card and the floating card take both drops, into the draft while editing and saved onto the note otherwise; the tray's editor takes an image by paste as before. Both paths store the bytes once: `POST /api/images` takes the file as the whole body, reads the format from the bytes (never the file name), and answers with the id; `GET /api/images/[imageId]` serves them, cached for a year — the id is a cuid and the bytes never change. A note points at that URL in its markdown (`![name](/api/images/<id>)`, the file's name as the alt so a note read without the image still says what was there); a figure block points at it in its html. `components/use-image-drop.ts` is the one drop handler both surfaces use: it tells a drag carrying files from any other, refuses what the tier does not allow before anything leaves the browser, and stops the drop there — anything that is not an image keeps travelling to the window, where a dropped file is added as a document (§15).

The note editor shows a dropped image as the image, not as its markdown: `![alt](url)` is an atom in the note grammar, like the `[block id]` chip — the whole tag shows as one thing the caret steps over, and reading the document back writes the same markdown (`lib/note-markup.ts`, `lib/note-doc.ts`). An image reaches a note three ways: dropped on the note, pasted into the editor, or picked with the bar's image button on the notes full page (png, jpg, gif, webp, bmp; a GIF plays). It sizes itself to the column — never wider, its height following — unless the reader sets a width: the handle at the image's corner in the editor drags it (60px to the column's width; the column's width means "size itself" again), and the width rides in the image's url (`?w=320`, `imageWidth` and `withImageWidth` in lib/note-markup.ts), so the markdown stays standard and the rendered note reads the same width back. A collapsed note's one-line summary reads an image as its alt text: a preview line has no room for a picture and none for a URL.

**Tiers (TIERS.md).** An account whose Premium trial ended drops images up to 5 MB; above that the account needs Unitos Premium. No tier stores an image past 25 MB. The client checks the size before the upload so the refusal is instant, and the route checks it again — the client's check is a courtesy, not the gate.

### Circle & ask

On a page, holding the mouse and dragging draws a freehand loop (the §11 draw, on a page instead of a frame). Releasing opens the Circle & ask card under the loop: a question box, two actions — **Ask** (the typed question), **Comment** — and the four highlight color dots (§6's hues, shared via `components/reader/hues.ts`). A color dot saves a **lasso highlight**: the whole PDF is a figure, and the loop persists as a highlight painted in that color (a `Note` with `color` and a page anchor; typed text rides on it as the note). The guide's Circle & ask section, right after Extract, covers handwritten pages too. Ask runs through the one pipeline (§4): `POST /api/derive` `type: EXPLAIN` with `page: {blockId, region, question}` — the one entry point EXPLAIN still serves; the server renders the page and the circled part from the stored bytes, attaches both (the page carries context, the crop carries the spot, enlarged), and the prompt's page variant answers — transcribe what is there, never guess at illegible handwriting. The answer streams into the card and persists as an annotation with a page anchor; Comment posts to `/api/annotations` `{page}`. Marks paint on the page as SVG loops carrying `data-source-id`, so source chips jump to them and flash them like text marks, and clicking a mark opens its annotation. Viewers see marks, draw nothing.

## 17. Unitos Premium: offline work

Offline, the open tab keeps working — reading what is loaded, and for an account with Unitos Premium active (a running trial, a granted Premium, or Ultra; `premiumActive` in `lib/tiers.ts`), the non-AI writes. Writes made offline queue in IndexedDB (`lib/offline/queue.ts`) and sync in order when the browser is back online. AI features (derivations, the assistant, the upload review, conversion) need the server and stay unavailable offline for everyone: the routes that need a model are one list (`lib/offline/ai-routes.ts`; the service worker keeps the same pattern), and a call to one of them offline answers with the plain message — "AI is off while offline. Notes, highlights, comments, and edits save on this device and sync when you are back online." — from `api()` before the request leaves, and from the service worker as a 503 for a stream the client fetches itself, so every AI surface shows the same words in its own error place. Merge with AI answers the same; Join text queues. The offline pill in the header says AI is off beside what saves.

- **The flag:** `User.tier` (PREMIUM | ULTRA) and `User.trialEndsAt` (TIERS.md). A new account is PREMIUM with the trial's end two months out; past that date on PREMIUM the account is expired and this gate closes. The operator grants for good from the admin accounts page (Tier: Ultra, Premium for good, or Premium on a trial until a date; `POST /api/admin/accounts/tier`). No billing yet. The single local reader (sign-in off) is ULTRA: there is no account to gate. Every page reads the tier once through `accountTier` (`lib/tiers.ts`) and every route reads it off the session's user row, so no surface can disagree with another. The tier shows as the tier mark beside the person's badge — the white crystal for Unitos Premium, the black diamond for Unitos Ultra (`components/tier-mark.tsx`) — the tier chip in the dashboard header, and the plan card under Plan in Settings. The reader shows no tier.
- **What queues:** note edits (auto-save included), note create and delete, section renames and reorders, replies, block text edits and deletes, highlights and comments (`/api/annotations`; the optimistic paint stays), and content uploads — a file's bytes queue whole (single-request or chunked replay, same caps as online), a URL queues as the plain ingest request. Importing needs the server to parse and store, so an offline add skips the box and queues instead.
- **What does not queue:** any write whose response the caller reads (a created section's id, the style route's healed spans), everything AI, and every write on an account whose trial ended — those fail with the plain offline message.
- **Sync:** at-least-once, oldest first, writes before uploads. A record leaves the queue when the server answers; a 4xx drops it with a console warning (stale by then); a network failure stops the drain until the next online event. The workspace header shows the pill: offline with the queued count, then the sync until the queue drains.
- **The save indicator** (`components/save-indicator.tsx`, `lib/save-state.ts`): one small dim line in the header of the reader and of the notes full page, left of Share — "Saving…" while a write is in flight or a note's draft waits for its save, "Saved" once every write landed, "Not saved" when the last write failed, and offline with Unitos Premium "Saved on this device · syncs when online". Every `api()` call counts, and the note editors count their drafts from the keystroke; nothing shows until the tab's first write.
- **The boundary:** offline work lives in the open tab, or in a project's offline copy. A reload while offline loads a saved project from its copy; any other page answers with the offline page. Queued records survive the reload and sync on the next online visit.
- **Offline copies (Unitos Ultra):** a project saved for offline opens without a network, like a Google Drive file made available offline. The copy is the project's pages — the reader on each document and the notes full page — and the images those pages show, kept in the browser's cache under the project's own cache name (`unitos-project-<id>`), with one row per project in IndexedDB (`lib/offline/saved.ts`, store `saved`). Save for offline is the pill in the reader's header (where Add context stood) and an item in the project's ⋯ menu on the dashboard; saved, the pill reads Offline and the card shows the Offline badge, and a press on either removes the copy. A save shows the bottom progress bar (`components/progress-bar.tsx`): pages, then images, counted as each fetch lands. Offered to every account; a non-Ultra press answers with the plain Ultra message, and the route that lists what to save (`GET /api/notebooks/<id>/offline`) answers 403, so no copy is made. The service worker (`public/sw.js`, registered by `components/offline/service-worker.tsx` in production) caches the app's static files as they load and the offline page (`/offline`) at install; a page load is network first, and while online it refreshes any page or image a copy holds; offline, the copy answers, and a URL no copy holds answers with the offline page, which lists this account's saved projects from IndexedDB and goes to Projects by itself when the browser is back online. The dashboard refreshes copies older than an hour in the background; a copy whose project is gone or no longer readable is removed. Sign out removes the account's copies. Videos and the assistant's pages are not part of a copy: a video document's transcript and notes open, the video itself needs the network. Offline writes in a saved project queue as above (Unitos Premium, which Ultra holds).

## 18. Notifications

The admin sends notifications to accounts; the admin never has access to an account. `/admin` is the operator's console — feedback, digest, usage, notifications — behind its own password (§2). It holds no session for any account, opens no account, and changes nothing on one: no impersonation, no profile edits, no premium toggle. The notification is the one thing the admin sends into an account, and it flows one way — the recipient reads and dismisses; nothing comes back. Feedback is the reader's one message to the admin, and the admin's reply to it is a notification too (kind `feedback`).

**Photos and links on feedback:** the feedback form takes photos (Add photo, a paste into the message, at most 6; each uploads through `POST /api/images` as a note's image does, and the route checks every id is one this account uploaded) and links (Add link, absolute http(s) URLs, at most 10). `Feedback.images` holds the image ids, `Feedback.links` the URLs; the inbox shows the photos as thumbnails that open whole and the links as links, and `GET /api/admin/feedback` returns both.

**The feedback pipeline** (`.claude/skills/feedback-pipeline`): a daily Routine reads the inbox (`GET /api/admin/feedback?status=new,seen&take=2000`), clusters the requests that fit this spec, makes each change on a `feedback/<slug>` branch, and opens one pull request per change with the feedback quoted and a review checklist, marking the feedback `seen`. A person reviews and merges; Vercel deploys `main`. The next run finds the merged pull request by its `Feedback-Ids:` line, replies to each sender ("Shipped: …", the admin's reply notification) and marks the feedback `resolved`. The pipeline never pushes to `main`, never merges, never replies before the change is on `main`, and opens at most five pull requests per run. Feedback it cannot place — vague, out of scope, or rejected by a closed pull request — keeps its status for a person.

### Data model additions

```prisma
model Notification {
  id         String   @id @default(cuid())
  kind       String   @default("update") // "update" | "account" | "feedback"
  title      String
  body       String   // markdown
  feedbackId String?  // kind "feedback": the feedback this replies to; SetNull when that feedback goes
  feedback   Feedback? @relation(fields: [feedbackId], references: [id], onDelete: SetNull)
  recipients NotificationRecipient[]
  createdAt  DateTime @default(now())
}

// Feedback gains the account that sent it:
//   userId  String?  // soft reference like Notebook.userId; null = sent signed out, no reply possible
//   replies Notification[]

model NotificationRecipient {
  id             String       @id @default(cuid())
  notificationId String
  notification   Notification @relation(fields: [notificationId], references: [id], onDelete: Cascade)
  userId         String       // soft reference like Notebook.userId; "user-1" = the local reader
  dismissedAt    DateTime?    // null = open on the dashboard
  @@unique([notificationId, userId])
}
```

- **Kinds:** `update` — an update to Unitos (a new function, a changed behavior); `account` — a change made to the account (Unitos Premium turned on, a limit changed); `feedback` — a reply to feedback the account sent (made only by Reply in the feedback inbox, never composed on the notifications page). The kind renders as a chip on both sides.
- **Sending** (`/admin/notifications`, `POST /api/admin/notifications`): kind, title, body, recipients — every account, or accounts chosen from the list (name and email, nothing else; `lib/notifications.ts` is the admin's whole view of accounts). One `Notification` row and one `NotificationRecipient` row per recipient. With sign-in off the local reader is the one account. The page lists every send, newest first, with its recipient count and how many dismissed it; Delete (`DELETE /api/admin/notifications`) removes a send for every recipient.
- **Receiving:** the dashboard shows the account's open notifications above Projects — kind, date, title, body (markdown) — until Dismiss (`PATCH /api/notifications/[id]`, the recipient only) stamps `dismissedAt`. Dismissed rows stay, so the admin's count holds; only the admin's Delete removes them.
- **Replying to feedback** (`/admin`, `POST /api/admin/feedback`): `POST /api/feedback` records `Feedback.userId`, the account that sent it (signed out: null). Reply writes one `Notification` of kind `feedback` — title the feedback's message on one line, body the reply (markdown), `feedbackId` the feedback — with one `NotificationRecipient`, the account that sent it; new feedback turns seen. The inbox names the account under each feedback and lists its replies with whether the account dismissed each; the dashboard card reads "Reply to your feedback", the feedback's message, then the reply. Feedback with no account, or an account the recipient list does not know, cannot be replied to (400). Delete on the notifications page removes a reply like any send. The first account to sign in adopts the local reader's feedback and notifications with the rest of its data (§2).
- **Release notifications** (`lib/releases.ts`, `lib/releases-server.ts`): a **release** is one shipped set of new functions, one entry in `RELEASES` — its date, the dictionary keys of its title and body, and the controls it added. On every dashboard open, `ensureReleaseNotifications` makes sure that for every release whose day ends after the account was made, the notification row for the account's language exists (`Notification.releaseKey` `<release>:<lang>`, kind `update`, dated the day it shipped, written the first time an account in that language opens the dashboard after the deploy) and the account is a recipient of it; a dismissed row is never reset; the local reader is told of every release. So the news of a deploy lands on every dashboard with the deploy, with no admin send. The admin's notifications page lists a release send like any other.
- **The New glow** (`components/new-feature.tsx`, `useNewFeature(<id>)`): every control a release added — Collapse, the annotations full page's four arrows, By document, Conversations — glows with a pulsing sage ring and a New pill until the reader presses it, for `NEW_GLOW_DAYS` (45) after the release shipped; this browser remembers what was pressed (`unitos-new-seen`), and the server's render shows no glow. The guide (`components/guide-dialog.tsx`) explains every one of them.
- **The boundary, enforced:** the admin routes touch `Notification`, `NotificationRecipient`, and `Feedback` only; no admin route reads or writes `User`, `Session`, `ReaderProfile`, or a corpus. No email: the notification lives in the app.

## 19. Translation

The reader's language (the `dissect-lang` cookie, §2) and a document's language can differ: a Chinese paper opened in the English reader, an English podcast transcript in the Chinese reader. Translation closes the gap through DeepL — the one translation provider, Chinese and English both ways (`DEEPL_API_KEY`; a Free API key ends in `:fx` and takes the free host on its own, any other key the Pro host; `DEEPL_API_URL` points a local run at a stand-in). Nothing here is a model call of the app's own: DeepL translates, the app caches and shows.

- **The offer.** When `DEEPL_API_KEY` is set, a bar above the article (and above a media document's transcript) says which language the document is in and offers Translate to the reader's language. The document's language is judged from its characters (`lib/translate/detect.ts`: Chinese when a fifth or more of the letters are CJK, English when the letters are Latin, unknown otherwise — no model call); a document already in the reader's language gets no offer, an unknown one is offered anyway, since DeepL detects the source itself.
- **The translation.** `POST /api/documents/[documentId]/translate` `{lang}` (editor) translates every PARAGRAPH, HEADING, LIST, TRANSCRIPT, TABLE (plain text under 5,000 characters), and FIGURE caption block the document has, in batches of at most 50 texts and about 100 KB (DeepL's limits), and stores each as a `BlockTranslation` row keyed by block and language with a hash of the block text it came from. A block edited since reads as stale and translates again on the next Translate; everything else is served from the cache, so a document costs its characters once per language. `GET …/translate?lang=` answers the cache (viewer). Usage telemetry records the characters sent, at $25 per million (provider `deepl`).
- **The reading.** Each translation reads under its block, quieter, marked by a rule, in the reader's language; in the transcript a paragraph's lines read together under it. Anchors, highlights, links, and every tool stay on the original text — the translation is a layer, never a replacement. Hide translation puts the article back; the choice is remembered per document in the browser, and a remembered document shows its cached translation again on open without a DeepL call. Edit mode shows no translations.
- **Not translated.** Notes, annotations, and the assistant's answers: those are written in the reader's language already (§4). Code and equations. A table past the plain-text cap.

## 20. Visualize (Unitos Ultra)

Visualize turns a selected passage into one picture that delivers its core idea at a glance: a directed diagram for named things and the relations between them, a simulation for a law of change — the state it governs evolving through time, integrated by the server — a still picture for a mechanism, a physical arrangement, or the shape a formula describes, or a short animation for a process whose order is the point. It is a derivation (§4, type `VISUALIZE`, template `lib/prompts/visualize.ts`), Unitos Ultra only (TIERS.md; the toolbar shows the tool to every account and answers a non-Ultra click with the plain Ultra message; the route answers 403), and runs at the highest effort on the model that draws best (`VISUALIZE_MODEL`, `VISUALIZE_EFFORT` in `lib/derive/config.ts`: Claude Opus 5.5, `max`), because the picture has to be faithful or refused.

- **The passage in the article comes first.** The model reads the whole document (the cached prefix, §2) and says what the passage does there: it states a problem; extends a problem stated earlier; solves one; offers an alternative to a structure, a method, or an explanation given earlier; is one step of a process the article builds; is an example of a claim made earlier; or stands on its own. The core idea is the passage's point as it sits in the article — what it adds to what the article set up. When the passage answers, extends, or replaces something stated elsewhere, the picture shows both — the problem and this solution, the earlier structure and this alternative — with the passage's own part standing out, because a reader who sees this passage's part alone does not see what it is for. What the article states elsewhere is there to be drawn: drawing it is not inventing. In a diagram every node carries a `role`, `passage` or `article`; when a diagram holds both, the passage's nodes draw in the accent on the fill and the article's faint — a muted dashed outline on the paper, muted text — and a diagram of the passage alone draws plain. In a picture the passage's part takes the accents and the rest the ink. The caption says the relation.
- **The judgment.** With the passage placed, the model names its core idea in one sentence, works out both ways to draw it, and draws only when all four hold: a picture shows this better than words; the picture needs nothing the passage, its context, or the article elsewhere does not state; a reader who sees the picture without the passage takes away that core idea and not another; and the model is certain this picture is the best way to show it, not merely a possible way. Otherwise it declines. A decline is a valid outcome: the card says there is not enough certainty for a picture that carries the core idea of this passage, shows the model's reason — which names what was tried, so the reader knows the passage was worked on and not skipped — and points at the assistant, Explain, and Simplify; nothing persists.
- **The two ways to draw, and the analogy among them.** The literal picture draws the passage's own structure. The analogy draws a familiar thing that shares the idea's shape. A passage whose own structure carries the idea takes the literal picture — an analogy there is only a second thing to understand. A passage whose idea is abstract, unfamiliar, or has no drawable structure of its own is where the analogy earns its place, and is often the difference between a picture and a refusal. The model names three candidate analogies and takes the one that passes all five: every part maps to a part of the passage and it can say which to which; it adds nothing the passage contradicts; the reader knows the analogue already, without being taught it (the reader context says what they know — an analogy from their own field beats a general one); it breaks outside what the passage claims; and it carries the idea better than the literal picture would. No candidate passes all five, no analogy: the literal picture, or a refusal. In a diagram the node label is the analogue's part and its detail is what that part stands for, so the mapping reads off the picture; the caption names the analogy and says what stands for what.
- **The equation in motion.** When the passage is a law of change — an equation with a time derivative, or a description of how a state evolves under a rule — the picture is the state evolving under that law, the way a textbook animates the heat equation or a wave packet, never a drawing of it: the model could not write a true frame, so it writes the spec and the server integrates (`lib/derive/simulate.ts`). The spec is `{law, equation?, x0, x1, initial, velocity?, potential?, momentum, boundary, coefficient, variables?, duration, xLabel?, uLabel?}`. The laws: `heat` (u_t = α u_xx; Crank–Nicolson on a fixed or insulated domain, explicit on a periodic one), `wave` (u_tt = c² u_xx; leapfrog inside the CFL bound, from the initial state and velocity), `advection` (u_t + c u_x = 0; the exact solution u₀(x − ct), so a pulse travels without a scheme's smear), `schrodinger` (iψ_t = −a ψ_xx + Vψ with ħ = 1; Crank–Nicolson in the complex plane on a box or an insulated domain; the state is initial(x)·e^{ikx}; drawn as |ψ|², Re ψ faint under it, V dashed), and `ode` (dv/dt = f(t, v…) for up to four variables; fourth-order Runge–Kutta; drawn as curves against time revealed by a curtain whose edge is the present). Boundaries: `fixed` holds the ends at their t = 0 values, `insulated` passes no flux, `periodic` makes the ends one point. Formulas are a small language of their own (`compileFormula`: numbers, the law's variables, arithmetic, comparisons, pi and e, and a fixed set of functions with `step` and `pulse` for square starts) parsed once and never evaluated as code; a formula that does not parse, a state that is not finite, or a duration the scheme cannot cover fails the run with the reason. The drawing: 36 frames over an 8-second loop as one SMIL `d` animation on a 480×320 plot with axes, the passage's labels, the law as the passage writes it above, the state at t = 0 kept faint under the motion, the boundary marked (dots for held ends, bars for insulated, the word for periodic), and a time bar from t = 0 to the duration. Coordinates are whole pixels in x, so a field law is about 75k characters and the wavefunction about 125k — under the SVG ceiling. The check reads a simulation's spec, not its frames.
- **Output contract.** JSON `{judgment: {structure, certain, reason}, visual: null | {kind: 'diagram' | 'simulation' | 'picture' | 'animation', caption, diagram?, simulation?, svg?}}`, validated strictly (`lib/derive/visualize.ts`), retried once on a failed parse like every JSON derivation. `certain: false` or a null visual is a decline. A diagram is a spec — `{direction: 'right' | 'down', nodes: [{id, label, detail?, role?}], edges: [{from, to, label?}]}`, 3 to 12 nodes, labels at most 6 words — that the server lays out itself. A picture or an animation is SVG source the model writes to the prompt's rules (a 480-wide viewBox, the reader's palette, text at 14 to 20, SMIL for motion) and the server reduces before storing: only the listed elements survive (shapes, text, defs, gradients, clip paths, and the SMIL animation elements), no script, foreignObject, image, style element, event attribute, or reference outside the file — a `url()` that does not name a fragment of the file is dropped with its attribute; a link keeps what it wraps; the root keeps its viewBox and loses width and height; every color attribute is normalized to lower-case six-digit hex. An SVG that is not well-formed, has no viewBox, or is empty fails the run with the reason. The SVG ceiling is the same number in the contract and in the reduction (300000 characters), so an SVG past it is caught by validation and retried with the reason rather than reduced and then thrown away.
- **The layout.** Ranks by longest path, a cycle's closing edge marked and arced around the nodes. An edge that skips ranks travels through one waypoint per rank it passes, and the waypoints order and place with the boxes, so a long edge bends through the gaps instead of crossing the boxes between its ends. Ordering is eight sweeps, down and up, each rank sorting by the mean seat of its neighbours in the rank before or after; the arrangement with the fewest crossing edges wins and the model's order breaks every tie. An edge's label is measured with the nodes, and the waypoint that carries it is the size of that label, so the rank keeps room for it and no label lands on a box. Boxes are sized from measured text (`@napi-rs/canvas`, an estimate without it). The direction flips when the drawing would be a strip too wide for the card. The viewBox is the drawing's own bounds — every box, every label, the far side of every back edge's arc — so nothing is cut.
- **The check.** A second call on the same model at the same effort (`VISUALIZE_CHECK`, `lib/prompts/visualize-check.ts`), after the picture is drawn and laid out. It is the one pass that sees the finished picture: the pass that drew it never does — a diagram's layout is the server's, and a picture's SVG has been through the reduction, so what the reader gets is not what the model wrote. It reads the picture back, with the whole document above it — a simulation by its spec, since its frames are the server's — against five questions (the core idea as the passage sits in the article, every part mapping to the passage or the article, nothing missing that the reduction cut, legible at 320 px, the caption) and answers `{keep, reason, visual?}`: keep it, replace it whole, or withdraw it — a withdrawal declines the run with its reason, because a picture that misleads is worse than no picture. A check that fails, or a replacement that does not render, leaves the picture as drawn: the draft is the work and a second call that did not answer never costs it. A picture past `VISUALIZE_CHECK_MAX_SVG` is kept as drawn, unchecked — reading back an SVG that large costs more than the check is worth.
- **Light and dark.** A stored picture shows in an `<img>`, where the page's theme tokens do not reach it, so the SVG carries its own dark palette (`lib/derive/visual-palette.ts`): one style block re-points the exact values the diagram and the simulation draw with and the prompt gives the model, under `prefers-color-scheme: dark` (the image route's CSP allows an inline style and nothing else). The model never writes a style element; the server always does, after the reduction. A color the model writes another way stays as drawn, which is why the prompt asks for the palette's exact values and the reduction normalizes what it can.
- **Destination.** The SVG is stored as an `ImageAsset` (`image/svg+xml`, the account's; `/api/images/[imageId]` serves it with a Content-Security-Policy that runs nothing) and one annotation lands in the hidden Annotations section with `derivationType: VISUALIZE`, one source per segment of the selection, and the markdown `![caption](/api/images/<id>)` followed by the caption in italics. The reader's card is EXPLAIN's card titled Visualization; Delete removes the annotation and its mark.
- **The viewer.** A visualization opens large in the app, never in a new tab: one viewer for the workspace (`components/reader/visualization-viewer.tsx`), the picture at up to 78vh over a dimmed page with its caption under it, closed by Escape, the backdrop, or ✕. Every visualization image on the page opens it — the picture in the card, in the Annotations tab, in a note — because `Markdown` renders an image under `/api/images/` as a button that raises one event (`dissect:open-visualization`, the image and its caption, which is the alt text); the card's Open button raises the same. An animation plays in the viewer as it does in the card. The mark carries the tool's symbol, reopens the card from the stored markdown, and lists under Visualizations in the Annotations tab. An animation plays wherever the image shows: SMIL runs inside an `<img>`.
- **The run.** Behind the heartbeat stream (§4): spaces while the model reasons, then `{ok, noteId, kind, caption, content}` or `{ok, declined, reason}`, or the error token with the reason. Cancel aborts the model call and persists nothing. Regenerate (§4) draws the passage again; a regenerate that declines — the judgment's, or the check's — keeps the picture that stands, because it stored nothing to replace it with.

## 21. Tool conversations and the log

Every AI tool's card can continue into a conversation, and every conversation anchored in the text — a tool's, or the assistant's from the selection popover — shows its condensed log when the reader hovers its mark. Continuing a tool's output into a conversation is Unitos Ultra (TIERS.md); the log is not gated.

- **Continue.** An Explain, Simplify, Analyze, or Visualize card, once its output is saved, carries one button at its foot: Continue in a conversation. It is offered to every account — a non-Ultra account sees it with the plain Ultra badge (like Visualize's own entry in the toolbar), and a press answers with the toast that this is Unitos Ultra, opening nothing; a turn sent anyway (`toolNoteId` set) answers 403. For an Ultra account, pressing it turns the foot into a text box and the card into Explain+ (Simplify+, Analyze+, Visualize+): the same card, the tool's symbol with a plus, the output above, the turns under it in the same scroll body, the box at the bottom. Every turn goes deeper on the output: the server (`/api/assistant/act` with `toolNoteId`) reads the selection from the annotation's own sources, puts the output in the prompt as what the reader ran and got, and answers from the output, the selection, and the document, adding to the output and never repeating it. It is the assistant's own route, so a turn can still propose actions, which go through the plan card as ever. Stop mid-turn, closing the card, and Delete work as on the assistant card. A conversation started while Ultra, read after a downgrade, stays readable with no box to send more.
- **Where the turns live.** On the tool's own annotation: `Note.conversation`, `[{role, content}]` oldest first, at most 60 turns. The output stays in `content`, so Simplify's sentence mirroring, the gist, and the Annotations tab read it as before; the Annotations tab shows the turns under the output, labeled Conversation, and the digest renders them after the output in the assistant conversation's transcript format. The assistant's own conversation keeps its transcript in `content` (§4); `lib/conversation.ts` reads both forms into one shape. Deleting the annotation deletes the conversation.
- **The mark.** The tool's symbol at the end of the highlighted text (`.mark-chip`) and on a figure's side label carries the plus once the annotation holds turns (`Highlight.plus`; `ToolSymbol` in block-view.tsx draws every symbol with or without it), and its tip says the card opens with its conversation. The card reopens from the mark with the turns and the box open. An open card's turns count before the refresh delivers the server's copy, and the turns a card sent this session are kept by note id (`toolConversationsRef`, reader-interactions.tsx): the page's copy arrives with the refresh, which takes seconds on a large document, and a card closed and reopened before then shows the session's copy — whichever copy is longer is the newer one. Without this the reopened card showed the output alone, and the conversation looked lost.
- **Expand.** Every conversation card carries Expand at its right — the assistant's card, and a tool's card once it holds turns or output. It opens the conversation whole over the pane: the turns in one wide column that scrolls, the box at the foot, always in reach. A tool's output is the conversation's first message. The card stays open under the view, so the same box sends from either place, and Close (or Escape) puts the pane back at the scroll position it left. The Annotations tab carries the same Expand on every annotation that holds a conversation; a stored conversation read there has no box.
- **How tall a card gets.** A card grows with its content up to the pane's height, then its body scrolls inside it (§6). A card anchored low in the pane still grew past the pane's bottom edge, and the box at its foot went out of reach — a long conversation kept pushing it further down. So a card that grew is lifted until its foot is back inside the pane; a card taller than the pane sits at the pane's top and scrolls inside. Only a card the reader can see moves: one scrolled away stays at its anchor. Expand is the way out when the conversation is longer than a card can hold.
- **The log.** Hovering a mark whose annotation holds a conversation — the tool's or the assistant's — shows the log card where the conversation's card would open (the same slot the card claims, the same connector line; `data-log-card`, never a side card, so it takes no slot and pushes nothing): one line per message, the reader's lines as chat bubbles, the assistant's plain, so the conversation's point is clear at a glance. The pointer has to rest on the mark for 320 ms; the card stays while the pointer is on the mark or the card and leaves 220 ms after it goes; a scroll or a click closes it, and clicking the mark opens the card in its place. An open card shows no log for its own conversation.
- **Writing the log.** `POST /api/notes/[noteId]/log` (viewer) answers the stored log when its `turns` matches the conversation's message count, else writes one (`lib/notes/conversation-log.ts`, `lib/prompts/conversation-log.ts`, the gist's model and effort): one line per message, each one complete statement that finishes its point, at most 25 words and 150 characters (65 in Chinese), in the message's own language, the reader's lines saying what the reader asked, the assistant's the answer's core point; a message the model skips shows its first words. A line over the cap is cut at the last sentence end that fits, else at the last clause break with an ellipsis, so a log line never stops mid-thought. For a tool conversation the output is the first message logged. Stored on `Note.log` as `{turns, v, lines: [{role, text}]}`, where `v` is the line rules the log was written under (`LOG_VERSION`): a log stored under older rules is written again on the next hover, so a change to the rules reaches logs already stored. The reader caches it per conversation length for the session, so a hover asks once. Usage telemetry records the call as `log`.

## 22. Stitch

**Stitch** is the assistant over the project's documents, from the graph (§13): one command across two or more documents — gather every passage on a topic into a new page, connect the passages that answer a question, find where the documents contradict each other, write one page that combines them. Every document of the project is its own page; the graph is where they are read together.

- **Where it lives.** The Stitch box sits at the foot of the graph overlay (`components/graph/stitch-box.tsx`), open when the graph opens and folding to a pill; the graph needs two documents for it to show. Beside the canvas, Generated content (`components/graph/generated-list.tsx`) folds out like Recommended links: every page Stitch wrote for the project, newest first, with the command that made it; a row opens it in the reader, and Delete removes it like any document from the library (refused while notes cite it). One folded list shows at a time.
- **Which documents.** Every attached document, or the documents picked in the graph: a ⇧-click (or ⌘/Ctrl-click) on a node picks it, and Pick documents in the box turns every click into a pick until Done; a picked node draws a clay ring, and the box lists the pick as chips (✕ drops one; Clear drops them all) and says what the command reads — "Every document (5)" or "3 documents picked". Send ends picking. A document that leaves the project leaves the pick.
- **The route.** `POST /api/notebooks/[notebookId]/stitch` `{command, history, documentIds?}` (`lib/graph/stitch.ts`, prompts in `/lib/prompts/stitch.ts`, `STITCH_MODEL`, usage under `stitch`; the heartbeat stream, §4). `documentIds` names the pick, distinct and attached to the project; absent, every attached document. Fewer than two attached documents is refused. Editor role. The model reads and writes block aliases, never the stored ids: a document's letter by its place in the read (A, B, … Z, AA) is its `[document <letter>]` tag, and a block's alias is the letter and the block's number in the document, in reading order (`[block B12]`); an alias is a few tokens where a stored id is a dozen, and copied right far more often, and the route resolves every alias (`blockByRef`, aliases and stored ids alike) and rewrites the reply's `[block …]` tags to the stored ids before the reply leaves (`replyWithIds`). A video or audio document reads as its transcript lines (the `TRANSCRIPT` blocks, each tagged with its seconds; the `VIDEO` block is the title, not content) and a handwritten document as its converted text (the `PAGE` blocks are page numbers, not content). A document with nothing to read — its transcript pending, stale, failed, or never run; its conversion pending, failed, or never run; or no text — is declared to the model as `(nothing to read: …)` with the reason, and every prompt names it as not read: the model never cites it and says so in the reply.
- **The skeleton.** The cost of a command must scale with what the command needs, not with the project, so no pass reads the documents' text whole. Every document carries a **skeleton** (`Document.skeleton`, `lib/graph/skeleton.ts`, prompt in `/lib/prompts/skeleton.ts`, `SKELETON_MODEL` at `SKELETON_EFFORT` (low: a reading), usage under `skeleton`): the document collapsed to about a tenth of its length — a gist of one sentence, one summary of one to three sentences per part of the contents (§26; built first when the document has none, the headings when the build fails), and one line per block, at most 40 words, every claim, number, name, and term kept as printed and the wording dropped; a heading is its text, a figure its caption and numbers, a transcript line what was said, a reference entry its first author and year. Built one model call per window of `SKELETON_WINDOW_CHARS` (100,000) of readable text, the windows at once, each window's system message the cached document prefix over its blocks; a line the model skips is the block's own first words, cut at a sentence end. Each line carries the hash of its block's text (`blockHash`), and that is how the skeleton follows the document: a block whose text changed, or a new block, reads as its own first words in place of its line (`currentSkeleton`, no model call), and when more than `SKELETON_STALE_FRACTION` (a tenth) of the document's characters are changed, new, or removed (`skeletonDrift`: a removed block's line counts at ten times its length, since the text is gone) the skeleton is rebuilt whole. The build runs in the background (`refreshSkeleton`, in `after()`) after an add lands a text document, after a re-parse (which also clears the stored skeleton, its ids being gone), when a transcript or a conversion lands its text, and after every block edit or removal — the drift check is all an edit under a tenth costs; a build older than `SKELETON_STALE_MS` is a dead run and may start again. Stitch takes the stored skeleton when under a tenth changed and builds a missing or stale one at once (`ensureSkeleton`); a build that fails reads the document as its first words, so a command still runs. The skeleton stays byte-identical from turn to turn while the document does, so the select pass's prefix caches per set of documents (§2).
- **The passes.** Documents under `STITCH_WHOLE_THRESHOLD` (120,000 characters) together skip the reading passes: the answer pass reads them whole. Past it, up to three passes at `STITCH_MODEL`. (1) The select pass reads every document's skeleton in one call — the gist, each part's summary above its first line, the lines under their aliases (`skeletonSystem`) — at `STITCH_SELECT_EFFORT` (low) and names the blocks the command needs: aliases most relevant first, consecutive blocks as one range (`B10-B15`, `expandPick`), from every document that bears on the command. (2) When the skeletons together run past `STITCH_SKELETON_BUDGET` (200,000 characters), a route pass runs first at `STITCH_ROUTE_EFFORT` (low): it reads only the gists and the part summaries (`routeSystem`), each part tagged `[part at <alias>]` with its first block's alias, and names the parts (up to 80); the select pass then reads those parts' lines only, the gaps declared, a document the route pass named nothing of keeping every line. (3) When those lines still run past the budget, they are ranked against the command (`lib/graph/rank.ts`: BM25 over the line's text and its part's title, Latin script by word, CJK by character bigram, no index and no model) and cut to it — every document's top lines first, up to a quarter share, then the rest most relevant first (`cutLines`) — so no document goes unread. A route pass that fails ranks every line; a select pass that fails, or names nothing of a document, reads that document's opening instead, cut to the document's share of the budget. The picks merge most relevant first across documents (`interleave`), then cut to 400 blocks and `STITCH_SELECTED_BUDGET` (200,000) characters of real text (`cutSelection`). The answer pass reads those blocks' real text at `STITCH_EFFORT` (high) — every document in order, its header saying how many of its blocks are shown, a gap between shown blocks declared (`selectedSystem`) — then the turns so far (at most 20, a turn with no text left out), then the command. So a quote or a link is never taken from a skeleton line: the answer pass sees only stored block text, and every quote and every link resolves against it as before.
- **The passes on Jev.** With Jev configured (`TYPESAFE_API_KEY`, `lib/jev.ts`, `lib/graph/stitch-jev.ts`), the route pass and the select pass are Jev's: they are judgements, not writing — does this part, does this block, hold material the command needs — and Jev answers one calibrated yes/no per part and per skeleton line, so every line of every document read gets its own decision instead of one model naming 400 blocks from a 200,000-character skeleton. The route pass is one call per document, one noul per part, the command and the document's gist beside the parts; a part reads at 0.4 or above, and every document's top two read whatever their number. The select pass is one call per chunk of 32 skeleton lines of one part (the lines the route cut to, or every line), one noul per line, the command, the gist, and the part's title and summary beside them; a line is picked at 0.5 or above, and the picks stand most likely first within each document, as the GLM pass's did. The state of a call stays one part's lines, since Jev loses accuracy as the state fills with text the question does not need; eight calls run at a time; the thresholds live in code. A pass that fails — more than a quarter of its calls, or Jev unreachable — falls back to the GLM pass, so the command always runs. Usage lands under `stitch` on the `jev-latest` row. The answer pass is unchanged: Jev writes nothing.
- **What was read.** The result carries one `StitchDocument` per document read: `read` (every block of it was under the reading passes — its skeleton's lines, or its text whole) or `empty` with the `reason` and, for a failed transcript or conversion, the stored error as `detail`. The box lists them under every reply — "Read 3 of 5 documents", then one row per document: "312 transcript lines read", or in red why it was not read ("Not read: transcription failed. The video's captions could not be read."; a known transcription error class renders in the UI language through `transcriptErrorKey`, the rest as stored). With fewer than two documents read the command does not run: no model call, nothing stored, an empty reply, and the box says Stitch needs two documents it can read, with the rows saying why. The model answers with three outputs and uses only the ones the command needs: `links` — a block in one document to a block in another document, with a reason, up to 24, each end the whole block or a verbatim quote of it; `document` — a new page built from the documents: `heading` parts, `quote` parts (one whole block, or a verbatim part of it when the part carries a quote), and `text` parts (the model's own writing in markdown, each with the sources it rests on); and `reply` — the answer to the command in plain sentences: a question gets its answer, concise, each claim naming the document it comes from; a command to gather, link, or write gets one to three sentences on what was done. Every block id and quote resolves against the real block text before anything is stored: a quote resolves in its named block (exact, then the whitespace-tolerant match, §5), then exact in any block when the model named the wrong one, and a quote that does not resolve falls back to its named block whole — real text of a document, never the model's copy; an id that names no block drops, a link whose two ends are in the same document drops, a link that already exists skips, and the same passage twice on one page is one passage. So "a page of every paragraph that mentions X across all of these" is quote parts; "connect the sections that answer this question" and "where do these contradict each other" are links, the reason saying how the passages relate or what each side claims; "write one page that combines what they say about X" is text parts with sources; a question gets its answer in the reply. The passes together, a skeleton built on the way included, get `STITCH_DEADLINE_MS` (270 s) of the route's 300: past it the run is cut and the reader is told to narrow the command, never left with a stream that ended empty.
- **Links land recommended.** Every link Stitch proposes is a `DocLink` with `recommended: true` (§13): it draws dashed in the graph and paints nowhere until Accept — the reader approves everything (§1). The reply says how many were proposed, and Recommended links beside the canvas is where they are accepted.
- **A generated document.** The document Stitch writes is a real document of the project: its parts become markdown, the markdown becomes blocks (`lib/parse/markdown.ts`, the FORMALIZE article's path, §11), the document carries `generatedCommand` (a document with one is a generated document of the project it is attached to), and it attaches to the project — every reader tool works on it, and it is a node of the graph. Provenance (§1): each part links back to the block it came from — a quote part to the passage it copied, a text part to each of its sources — as accepted `DocLink` rows from the generated block (the whole block as the anchor) to the source's range, so every paragraph clicks back to its source in the reader. A page with no part that resolved is not stored.
- **The conversation.** The box keeps the turns per project for the browser tab, so closing the graph and opening it again keeps them; New conversation clears them. Send becomes Stop while a turn runs; a stopped turn stores nothing more. Suggestion chips fill the box with the four commands above. A viewer reads the documents and sees no box.

The trial (TIERS.md): while the account's trial runs, or before sign-in, the Premium card says the trial's length in gold (2 Months Free Now) and, signed in, when the first payment lands. Public: a signed-out visitor sees the plans and Sign in to choose a tier. A card offers Start Free (Premium, on the trial) or Get the tier for a tier the account can buy, Your plan for the tier it holds, Manage subscription when it holds a subscription to the other tier, and nothing for a tier below the one it holds. The button carries the chosen interval to the order page.
2. **The order page** (`/billing/order/<premium|ultra>?interval=month|year`). The tier chosen at the interval carried from the plan page (monthly if absent) beside the order sheet: the account it goes to, the tier, the price (a yearly price with its monthly rate in brackets), what is charged now, how it renews and when, Cancel any time, a link to switch the interval without leaving the order, then Pay with Stripe (`POST /api/billing/checkout {tier, interval}` → a Stripe Checkout session in subscription mode, `lib/billing/checkout.ts`) and the legal line: the Terms, the Privacy Policy, and that card details go to Stripe, never to Unitos. Cancel on Stripe returns here with the plain canceled line, at the same interval. Pay dims the page under the Stripe hand-off overlay (`components/billing/pay-button.tsx`: a spinning ring, Opening Stripe…, and that Stripe brings the reader back) until the browser leaves.
3. **Stripe Checkout.** Stripe's hosted page. The session carries the account and the tier in its metadata and in the subscription's metadata, so every later event names both. 4. **The confirmation page** (`/billing/confirmed?session=…`). Stripe returns here. The session must belong to the signed-in account. Paid, or started free on the trial (Stripe reports `no_payment_required` and a trialing subscription), the page records the purchase through the same path as the webhook (`applyCheckoutSession`, `lib/billing/events.ts`) — idempotent, so the two can land in either order or twice — and the tier is on before the webhook arrives. 5. **Receipts** (`/billing/receipts`, `/billing/receipts/<id>`). **What a payment does to the account** (`lib/billing/events.ts`, from `POST /api/stripe/webhook`, signature-checked with `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`). The gates keep reading `User.tier` and `User.trialEndsAt` only (TIERS.md): the events write them. An event names its account by the Stripe customer first (`User.stripeCustomerId`), then by the metadata's `userId`; it names the tier by the price first, then by the metadata's `tier`. A completed checkout (`checkout.session.completed`, or `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` when the payment method settled later) applies the subscription and records the invoice through the same path as the confirmation page; a first payment that never settled (`checkout.session.async_payment_failed`) and a renewal that did not pay (`invoice.payment_failed`) are logged and change nothing: Stripe retries, and the subscription's own event holds or ends the tier. A paid subscription (`invoice.paid`, `customer.subscription.*` active or trialing) writes `tier` = the tier bought and `trialEndsAt` = null, and keeps `subscriptionId`, `subscriptionTier`, and `subscriptionEndsAt` (the paid period's end) on the account for the billing pages. An ended subscription (canceled, unpaid, or expired without a first payment) writes `tier` = PREMIUM and `trialEndsAt` = the paid period's end: the account reads as Unitos Premium until then and expired after, whatever the operator had granted before. Past due or paused holds the tier until Stripe ends the subscription. A refunded charge (`charge.refunded`) marks its purchase refunded and changes no tier: Stripe's cancellation does that. The webhook runs whatever the switch says: a payment that happened is recorded.

**The Stripe account** is configured by `scripts/stripe-setup.mjs`, run once per account (the sandbox, then live) with a key that may write products, prices, webhook endpoints, and the customer portal: one product per tier (metadata `unitos_tier`), a monthly and a yearly price on each (lookup keys `unitos_<tier>_<monthly|yearly>`; a changed amount archives the old price and a new one takes the lookup key), the webhook endpoint `<origin>/api/stripe/webhook` with the nine events, and the customer portal (switch tier between the two products, cancel at the period's end, update the card, see invoices). It prints the four price ids and, at the endpoint's creation, its signing secret. Rerun, it changes only what differs.

**The switch.** Billing is off until the operator turns it on from the admin billing page (`/admin/billing`, `POST /api/admin/billing`, `lib/billing/switch.ts`; the `AppSetting` row `billing`, no row = off). Off, every `/billing` page answers 404 and the app shows no link to billing; the admin, signed in to `/admin`, sees the pages as a preview with a line saying so. On needs sign-in on (there must be accounts to bill), `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`, `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, and the four `STRIPE_PRICE_*` variables set, and all four prices readable from Stripe; the refusal names what is missing. The admin page also shows each Stripe value's set state (`STRIPE_TAX` among them), the webhook URL to register, the four prices (two tiers × monthly and yearly) as Stripe reports them, how many accounts hold a subscription, and every receipt.

**The links in the app**, shown only while the switch is on (`billingLinks`). The tier button (`components/billing/tier-button.tsx`): the tier chip in the dashboard header, pressed. A Unitos Ultra account goes to the plans page. A Unitos Premium account — on the trial, granted, or expired — opens the upgrade panel (`components/billing/ultra-upgrade.tsx`): what Unitos Ultra adds to Unitos Premium, in the Ultra material over the dashboard, the plan page's own list (`billing.ultraFeature2`–`6`, so the two cannot disagree), then Get Unitos Ultra (the Ultra order page) and See plans (the plans page). No Plans link sits beside the chip: the chip is the one button. The subscription panel (`components/billing/subscription-panel.tsx`), under Subscription in Settings: the plan as the tier chip; its status — on the trial, the trial's end and that no card is on file, with the line that a card keeps Unitos Premium after it; with a subscription, the subscription as Stripe reports it (`lib/billing/subscription.ts`, one read per Settings load: free until the trial's end, renews on a date, canceled and paid until a date, past due; the stored columns stand when Stripe does not answer) — the billing interval, the card on file (brand and last four digits); then the actions: with a subscription, Manage subscription, Change plan, Update card, and Cancel subscription — each opens the Stripe billing portal at that flow (`POST /api/billing/portal {flow: update | payment | cancel, back: settings}`, `portalUrl`; Stripe returns to Settings) — and Receipts; without one, See plans and Receipts; under the rows, what the tier holds. With the switch off the panel shows the plan and its state alone. The plain Ultra message in the reader (Visualize, Continue) carries a Plans action that opens the plan page in a new tab. The local reader (sign-in off) never sees them: there is no account to bill.

**The billing ask.** Sign-up takes a name and an email (§2), no card; the card comes after 30 minutes of active time. Active time (`lib/active-time.ts`, `User.activeSeconds`) is how long the account has used the app: the tab visible and the reader acting in it. The active time clock (`components/active-time-clock.tsx`, mounted by the dashboard, the reader, the notes full page, and the assistant page beside the account guard) counts it in the browser, one second at a time while the tab is visible and an input — a pointer, a key, a scroll, a touch — landed within the last minute, and posts one tick per minute of it to `POST /api/active-time {seconds}`, the rest on pagehide; a tick past 120 seconds is refused, and a zero tick on every page open asks whether the ask is due. The route adds the tick to the account and answers whether the ask opens (`askDue`): the switch on, outside the beta (every account has Ultra then), the account past 30 minutes, holding no subscription, a checkout today starting free (`checkoutTrialEnd`: the trial runs and ends at least two days out, so the ask's promise holds), and no ask in the last day (`User.billingAskedAt`, stamped when the route says yes, so a tab closed on the ask does not open it again on the next page). The ask (`components/billing/billing-ask.tsx`) is a dialog in the Premium material over the page: keep Unitos Premium free for two months, nothing charged until the trial's end, cancel any time. Add a card opens the Unitos Premium order page, where Stripe takes the card now and charges when the trial ends; Not now closes it, and it opens again after a day more of the clock. A reset account starts its active time and its ask over. Sign-in off: no row to count on, never an ask.

**The beta** (TIERS.md). `BETA=on` gives every account Unitos Ultra in the app while it is set: `accountTier`, `ultraActive`, and `premiumActive` (`lib/tiers.ts`) answer Ultra whatever the record says, so every gate opens and every tier mark is the black diamond. Billing runs beside it, disconnected: the pages read the record through `tierState` and sell the tiers as always, a purchase writes the record as always, and the state line on the plan page and the plans page says first that the tier chosen takes effect when the beta ends. The plan page and the plans page are public doors in the middleware, so a visitor from an ad reaches them signed out. The admin billing page shows whether `BETA` is set. Unset it and redeploy to end the beta.

## 25. The tool quality loop

Every AI tool answers to one question: did the reader get something they could not have written without the document, and could not have found faster by reading it? The loop measures that and moves it.

- **What a valuable output is.** One rubric per tool (`scripts/eval/rubrics.ts`): the tool's promises, written as questions a reader would ask of the output — Simplify: understood on first read, every claim and number kept, nothing added, markers correct; Extract (DISTILL): the quotes answer the question, each adds something, each caption states the answer with its number, nothing stretched; Summarize: findings first with numbers, wording at the asked depth, no sentence that fits any document; the assistant and the selection chat: the answer first, every claim on a cited block, the passages across the document gathered (the matches), what the document does not answer said plainly; Ask and Find: from inside the range, quoted with times, nothing invented. Every assistant-voice template carries the same three lines — `STYLE_RULE`, `GROUNDING_RULE`, `SPECIFICITY_RULE` (`lib/prompts/types.ts`) — so the rubric and the prompt say the same thing in the same words.
- **The cases.** `scripts/eval/cases.ts`: one case is one tool on one fixture with one reader context — a paper, a memo, news, docs, an essay, a Chinese article, a transcript (`scripts/eval/fixtures/*.md`, original texts, block-tagged the way the routes render a document) — for readers set to nothing, a novice, an expert, a professional with a purpose, in English and Chinese, with questions the document answers, answers in part, or does not answer at all. The set is the contract for "across every context": a context the set lacks gets a case before its tool is judged good.
- **The run.** `npm run eval` (`scripts/eval/run.ts`): each case goes through its tool's real template (`lib/prompts/*`) on the real model with the route's effort and budget; the output is checked mechanically — the word cap, every `[block <id>]` resolving, every span and quote verbatim (`resolveSpan`, `matchInText`), Simplify's markers covering every sentence, the answer's language, no banned opener ("In other words", "This passage") — then judged by Claude Fable 5.1 (or Kimi without an Anthropic key) against the rubric: a score per criterion, an overall, the worst criterion with the exact words that show it, and one concrete change to the template that would raise it. The report (`.eval/runs/<stamp>/report.md`, `results.json` with every prompt and output) is the table per tool and the eight weakest cases; `--baseline latest --gate` compares with the last run and fails on a mean drop of 0.5 or a check that newly fails.
- **The readers' signal.** Every tool's output carries a rating (`components/rating-buttons.tsx`, `POST /api/ratings`, `ToolRating`): 👍 or 👎 on the Simplify, Analyze, and Visualize cards, on every answer of the selection chat and the sidebar assistant; a 👎 opens one optional line for what was wrong. The row keeps the tool, the input it ran on, the output, the reader's language, and the comment. `npm run eval:ratings` (`scripts/eval/import-ratings.ts`) turns the thumbs down of the last 30 days into fixtures and cases — the document as the tool saw it, the selection or the question, the comment as what a good answer must fix — so the loop works on the answers real readers disliked, in the contexts they read in.
- **The check** (`lib/derive/check.ts`): with Jev configured, a tool's output is judged against its rubric at once. The criteria that are judgements — plain words, the answer first, nothing added, each quote adds something, honest about what is not answered — are asked as yes/no questions of the input and the output; the criteria that count (every number kept, every marker present) stay with the eval's mechanical checks, since Jev does not count. Simplify, Summarize, and the assistant's answer are checked after the reader has them (a streamed output is on screen before a check can answer); a criterion under 0.5 fails, and a failed output is flagged: one `ToolRating` row with rating `flag` and the failed criteria as its comment, beside the readers' thumbs, which `npm run eval:ratings` imports like a thumb down. Extract, whose output is JSON, is checked before it is shown: a run failing two criteria or more runs once again, the run with fewer failures is kept, and a kept run that still fails is flagged.
- **The loop** (`.claude/skills/toolloop/SKILL.md`, `/toolloop`): pull the ratings, run the baseline, read the weakest cases whole, change the one template the weakest tool uses — the rule for the fault class, never the case — run that tool against the baseline, keep the change when its mean rises and no case fell under 3, revert otherwise, one template per commit; stop when no case is under 3 and every check passes, or a change no longer moves the mean. A fixture is never edited to pass; a case is never dropped for scoring low.

## 26. Contents

**Contents** is the article's parts, each a jump to where it starts: the one control of the article menu at the top left of the article (§6), in Normal view floating at the top left of the page, where it stays as the article scrolls; in a split view in the pane header, its list dropping below the header. A transcript's contents are its chapters, in the media pane (below). Two clicks make the contents: Contents opens the list, and with nothing stored the list asks whether to generate them — one line saying AI reads the article and writes its parts, the Generate contents button, and the disclaimer that a part's title or start may be off — and Generate contents runs the one model call. Nothing is written by AI before the reader asks for it.

- **Chapters.** A media document's contents are its chapters: one part per topic of the transcript, each pointing at the transcript line it starts on. The media pane's view bar carries Chapters (`components/video/chapters-menu.tsx`), beside the transcript's actions: stored chapters list as jumps, each with its time, and a click seeks the player to the line; none stored, the ask and Generate chapters (an editor), which runs the one Jev pass (`lib/video/chapters.ts`; `TYPESAFE_API_KEY`, else a 503) through the same route with `generate: true`. Jev reads the transcript in chunks of 40 lines with the four lines before each for context and answers one yes/no per line — does a new topic start here — and code makes the chapters: the lines at 0.6 or above, at least 45 seconds apart (the surer line wins when two are closer), at most 40 (the surest kept), the first line always the first chapter; a transcript under 12 lines has none. A chapter's title is its first line's opening words, since Jev writes no text. The parts store as `Document.contents` like an article's.
- **The parts.** Built when the reader presses Generate contents, never at ingest — one model call over the whole document, for a list many documents are never asked for — and stored on the document (`Document.contents`: `[{title, blockId, level}]`, in reading order; `lib/contents.ts`, prompt in `/lib/prompts/contents.ts`, `CONTENTS_MODEL` at `CONTENTS_EFFORT` (low: a reading of where the parts begin, like Distill's), usage under `contents`). The model reads the document under the cached prefix and segments the whole of it: where the document has headings, a part starts at a heading and its title is that heading as written, nested headings level 2 under their level 1 part; where it has none, the model segments by what the text does and writes a title of at most 8 words in the document's language. A part names the id of its first block; the route resolves every id against the stored blocks (`resolveParts`): an id that names no block drops, a part that starts before the one before it drops, two parts at one block are one, a part cannot start at the title block, a page marker, or the video block, and a level 2 part with no level 1 part before it is level 1. At most 80 parts; a document under 6 readable blocks has none. Titles are never translated.
- **The route.** `POST /api/documents/[documentId]/contents` `{generate?}` (viewer): stored parts are answered as they are, no model call, `fallback: false`; none stored, the document's headings (`headingContents`: every `HEADING` block past the title is a part, `h1`–`h2` level 1, deeper level 2 under a level 1) with `fallback: true`, no model call. `generate: true` from an editor runs the one model call and answers the parts stored (`fallback: false`; no parts for a document too short to have any); a failed call is a 422 the list shows with Try again, and no model configured a 503. A viewer's generate answers the headings. A re-parse carries the stored contents onto the new blocks (`carryContents`, `lib/contents.ts`): each part finds the new block whose text is its old block's — the whole text, else its first 60 characters — in reading order past the part before it; a part whose text is gone drops, and when fewer than half of the parts carry, the contents clear, so the next Generate contents writes them anew. The parts a reader asked for are never thrown away for a parse of the same text. The skeleton (§22) builds the contents on its way when the document has none, so a document whose skeleton has built shows its parts on the first click.
- **The list.** `components/reader/contents-menu.tsx`: the Contents button, and the list under it. With stored parts: the disclaimer, then the parts — level 1 bold, level 2 indented; a click scrolls the reader to the part's block and flashes it (`dissect:flash-block`) and closes the list; Escape and a click outside close it. With none: the ask, Generate contents (an editor; a viewer reads that an editor can generate them), the disclaimer, and under a rule the article's headings when it has any, each a jump like a part. While the call runs the button says so; a failed call says why, and the button stands for Try again; a document too short to have parts says so. The parts are kept per document for the browser tab, so a reopen shows them at once, and read again on every open, so a re-parse's new block ids replace the kept ones.

## 27. Slides and sheets

Two more file formats, each with its own parser and its own reader form: **slides** (a .pptx, or a Google Slides file from Drive) and **sheets** (a .xlsx, a .csv, a .tsv, or a Google Sheets file from Drive). Every AI tool, every annotation, and every note works on them exactly as on an article: their words are rendered text, selected and marked like a table's (§6), anchored by block offsets with the quote fallback (§5), and read by the assistant and every derivation through the one document prefix (§4). Google Docs and Drawings still export to PDF (§14).

**The one rule.** A SLIDE or SHEET block's html is a replica of the slide or the sheet, and the replica's DOM text — every text node outside `[data-anchor-skip]` — equals the block's text exactly. Text pieces are joined by invisible gaps (`.cell-gap`, a tab or a newline, the table convention of §5); what is not the document's own words — a slide number, the layout's and master's decoration, a sheet's column letters and row numbers, the style element — is skipped. So a selection inside a replica captures block offsets (`lib/anchors/dom.ts`), and the block's marks paint on the passage inside the html (`table-marks.ts`), never a ring around the whole block.

**Data model.** `BlockType` gains `SLIDE` and `SHEET`. `Document.format` is `"slides"` or `"sheets"` (null for everything else): re-parse picks the parser by it, since a .csv has no magic. The stored file is the .pptx, .xlsx, .csv, or .tsv, deduped by `fileHash` like a PDF. Neither block type edits, styles, or removes (`/api/blocks/[blockId]`); a re-parse reads the stored file again.

### Slides (`lib/parse/slides.ts`)

One SLIDE block per slide, `Block.page` the 1-based slide number, in the presentation's own order. The parser reads the .pptx part by part (`lib/parse/office.ts`: the zip, the XML, the relationships, the theme):

- **Replica.** The slide as a frame that scales with the column (`container-type: inline-size`; every length inside is a share of the slide's width in `cqw`, so a 24pt title is the same share of the slide at any size): every shape at its place and size in percent of the slide, its fill (solid, gradient, picture) and outline, its geometry (a preset as rounded corners, an ellipse, or a clip path; a custom geometry as an SVG path with its fill and stroke; connectors and lines as SVG), its text in the slide's fonts, sizes, weights, colors, alignment, indents, bullets and numbering, line spacing, autofit scale and vertical anchor; pictures stored as images of the document (`ImageAsset`, claimed on save like a captured chart, replaced on re-parse; an SVG beside a raster wins); tables as tables with their merges; a chart as its title and its data table; groups flattened with their transform. Placeholders inherit place, fonts, and bullets from the layout and the master, as PowerPoint draws them; the theme resolves scheme colors with their modifiers. The master's and layout's own shapes draw under the slide's, their words skipped. Slide-number, footer, and date placeholders are furniture: drawn, skipped.
- **Text.** Reading order: the title first, then the shapes top to bottom, left to right (a 4% band decides "same row"); the z-index keeps the slide's stacking whatever the order. A bulleted paragraph carries its bullet (`• `, `1. `) in the text; empty paragraphs at either end of a body drop. Speaker notes follow the slide's words after the line `Speaker notes:` (stored English, §2) and render as a strip under the slide. The document prefix says what a SLIDE block is (`formatLines` in `lib/derive/context.ts`).
- **Fonts.** Every typeface the deck names rides on the slide frames (`data-fonts`); the reader loads a web font for each (`lib/office-fonts.ts`, Google Fonts): the metric-compatible stand-in for an Office or system font (Carlito for Calibri, Arimo for Arial, Tinos for Times New Roman, Anton for Impact, …), or the typeface itself when Google Fonts hosts it. The declaration names the typeface first, so a machine that has it uses it. Sheets do the same for their cell fonts. The slide and sheet styles live in `lib/office-css.ts`, put in `<head>` once by the reader and used as-is by the server's picture render.
- **Charts.** A chart part draws as an SVG (`lib/parse/chart.ts`): bars and columns (clustered, stacked, percent stacked), lines, areas, pies and doughnuts, scatter plots, with the title, axes, gridlines, legend, and data labels, in the file's colors or the theme's accents. The SVG's words are skipped; the chart's data — the title, the categories down the first column, one column per series — lies under it invisible as the block's words, so the assistant reads the numbers and a selection sweeps through them. A chart kind the drawing does not cover shows its data table. Google Slides charts are pictures already.
- **Pictures.** A Google Slides import asks Drive for the .pptx export and its PDF export (`/api/drive/import`, kinds `slides` and `slides-file`; `fetchExported` in `lib/drive/fetch.ts`). The PDF's pages are the slides as Google draws them: their sizes land with the document (`storeSlidePictureSizes`), the renders after the response (`renderSlidePictures`, JPEG at `PAGE_IMAGE_WIDTH`, one `PageImage` per SLIDE block), and the frame carries `data-picture`. The reader (`MarkedHtml` in block-view.tsx) loads the picture from the page image route over the replica; once it loads the replica's words turn transparent and its shapes hide, the marks still painting on the words — a PDF viewer's text layer. A picture that fails to load, or a slide that has none yet, shows the replica alone. A re-parse carries the stored pictures over by slide number; the PDF itself is not kept. An uploaded .pptx (or one picked from Drive as a file) gets its pictures after the response by the best means the deployment has (`lib/handwritten/slide-pictures.ts`): LibreOffice where it is installed (`SOFFICE_PATH`, or `soffice` on the PATH) converts the deck to a PDF and the pages render like Drive's; else the configured browser (§15's `BROWSER_WS_ENDPOINT` or `CHROMIUM_PATH`) draws each replica with its web fonts, its pictures served from the database, and photographs it. Once stored, the frames are marked `data-picture`. Neither available: the replicas stand alone.
- **Caps.** 500 slides; past that the block list ends with a paragraph saying where it was cut.

### Sheets (`lib/parse/sheets.ts`)

- **The sheet repairs on Jev.** A .csv has no frozen panes and a workbook often none, so the header row scrolls away with the records; a column of numbers typed as text ("1,200 kg", "$4.50") lines up left like words. With Jev configured, one call per sheet (`repairSheet`) asks whether row 1 is a header row and, for every column that mixes text and digits, what the column holds (number, date, identifier, text). A header row at 0.7 freezes (`frozenRows` 1, as a frozen pane would); a number column's number-like text cells take the number kind and line up right. The cells' text never changes. Slides need no repair: speaker notes are separated by PowerPoint's own placeholder type, not by a reading.

One HEADING block (the sheet's name) and one SHEET block per visible sheet, in workbook order; a .csv/.tsv is one sheet named after the file (the delimiter sniffed unless the name says tabs; RFC 4180 quoting).

- **Drawings.** A sheet's drawing part (`xl/drawings/`) lays its pictures, charts, and shapes over the grid at the cells they are anchored to, inside the scroll box, so they scroll with the cells: a picture stored as an image of the document, a chart drawn as an SVG with its data laid under it as the block's words (the same chart drawing as slides; references without cached values resolve against the sheet's own cells), a shape as its fill and words (skipped). A drawing past the grid's last cell lies on the empty space beyond it; the grid's text never grows for it.
- **Replica.** The sheet as a grid in a box that scrolls both ways (`max-height: 78vh`), so frozen rows and columns, the column letters, and the row numbers stay in view while the page's scroll stays the reader's. Every cell at the sheet's column width and row height, showing the value as the sheet formats it (the number format applied through `ssf`, so 0.1 with a percent format reads `10%` and a serial date reads as a date), in the cell's font, size, weight, color, fill, alignment, wrap, and borders — one class per cell style in a scoped `<style>`, so a large sheet stays small; merged cells span; a formula rides on its cell as a tooltip; a hyperlink is a link; numbers align right, booleans and errors center; text longer than its cell runs over empty neighbors and is clipped otherwise. Theme and indexed colors resolve through the workbook's theme.
- **Text.** One line per row, cells separated by tabs, values as formatted. A merged-away cell's tab rides inside the cell that covers it, so the rows stay aligned. Hidden rows and columns are left out of both the grid and the text; trailing empty rows and columns are trimmed, a colored band near the words kept. The document prefix says what a SHEET block is and that the HEADING before it is the sheet's name.
- **Caps.** 10,000 rows, 256 columns, 200,000 cells per sheet; a cut sheet ends with a paragraph saying where.

### Verifying them

`scripts/qa/ui-office.mjs` runs the whole path against a server on :3311: the multipart and chunked uploads of the fixtures under `scripts/qa/fixtures/office/`, the parse, the pictures made after the response (LibreOffice or the browser), a note anchored on a slide's words and one on a sheet's cell, the reader (replicas, pictures over them, marks, the text toolbar, charts, sheet drawings, web fonts), and a re-parse that keeps pictures and anchors. Every run uploads fresh bytes, so runs never dedupe into each other.

### Adding them

Every add path takes them: the upload box's drop zone and file input (`SLIDES_ACCEPT`, `SHEETS_ACCEPT` in `lib/office-file.ts`), a drop anywhere on the page, the chunked upload (`/api/uploads/complete`), and Drive (`classifyDriveFile`: Google Slides → `slides`, Google Sheets → `sheets`, a .pptx in Drive → `slides-file`, a .xlsx/.csv/.tsv in Drive → `sheets-file`; the picker lists them). The server never trusts the name for a zip: `sniffOfficeFile` reads the format from the zip's parts; a .csv/.tsv is read by name only when the bytes are not a zip. The assistant's Drive attachments keep Drive's PDF export for Google Slides and Sheets (they ride as text). Translation treats a slide's words like a paragraph's (under the table cap) and a sheet's cells as data.

## 28. Collapse

**Collapse** shows the article with every block collapsed to its **core**: what the block really says, in plain words, at a tenth to a third of its length, written by AI in the light of the whole article. The reader reads the cores in place of the blocks, in order, as the article, and opens any block whole with a click. The tool is the Collapse button at the top right of the article, beside Extract (in a split view, in the pane header); pressed, the article collapses and the button reads Collapsed; pressed again, the article shows whole. The choice is remembered per document in the browser. A transcript and the article card in the media pane do not collapse yet.

- **The cores.** One per collapsible block — a paragraph, a list, a figure with a caption, a table, an equation, code, a transcript line, a slide, a sheet (a table's, a slide's, or a sheet's text past 5,000 characters is data and shows as it is); never a heading, which is short already, a separator, a page marker, or the video block. Stored on the document (`Document.collapse`: `{v, cores: [{blockId, hash, text}]}`, `lib/collapse.ts`), each core with the hash of the block text it was written from (`blockHash`): a block whose text changed shows as it is until the next Collapse writes its core again, and a re-parse's new block with the same text keeps its core by the hash. The cores are the document's, written once for every reader, in the document's language.
- **The prompt** (`/lib/prompts/collapse.ts`, `COLLAPSE_MODEL` — Claude Opus 5.5, the best writer of the roster: the cores are read by every reader in place of the article, and a document collapses once — at `COLLAPSE_EFFORT`, high, usage under `collapse`). The model reads the whole document under the cached prefix and, for every block listed, asks three things in order: what the block serves in the whole document — the claim it makes, the evidence it gives, the step it takes, the example it shows, the ground it lays for what comes next; what it is really trying to say, the one thing the reader must take from it, said first, a number, a name, or a term kept only when the point rests on it and written as printed; and how to say it in plain, simple words, the way one explains it to a smart reader outside the field. Length: a tenth of a long block, a third at most, the ceiling given per block (`coreCeiling`: a third of the block's words, between 8 and 60; a block of 24 words or fewer keeps up to seven tenths, so a sentence stays one shorter sentence); a long paragraph becomes two or three sentences at most. A figure collapses to what it shows and why the document shows it; a table to what it says — the pattern, the comparison, the biggest number; an equation to what it states in words; a list to its point; code to what it does; a transcript line to what was said. A core past its ceiling is cut at the last sentence end under it (`fitCore`); a core is never longer than its block. Built one call per window of `COLLAPSE_WINDOW_CHARS` (30,000) of the missing blocks' text, the windows at once; a block the model skips stays without a core and shows as it is.
- **The route.** `POST /api/documents/[documentId]/collapse` (editor) writes the cores the document lacks and answers every current core with `complete`; a failed call is a 422 the reader shows as a toast, no model configured a 503. `GET` (viewer) answers the current cores; a viewer with none stored reads that an editor can collapse the article.
- **The reading** (`components/reader/core-block.tsx`, `reader.tsx` `collapse`). A collapsed block is a paragraph in the block's place, a sage rule at its left, and its kind before the core when it was a figure, a table, an equation, code, a slide, or a sheet; it carries the block's id, so a jump from the contents and a flash still find it. Every block that has a core has its own button at its right (`CoreToggle`): it collapses that block to its core, or reads it whole, whatever the rest of the article shows — in a collapsed article and in a whole one (`flippedBlocks`, cleared when Collapse is pressed). A block whose whole text has annotations shows a clay dot on its core. Edit mode shows no cores.
- **Two sets of annotations.** The collapsed view and the whole text keep their own annotations. A selection in a core opens the same tools as a selection in the text — highlight, comment, Explain, Simplify, Visualize, the assistant, Add to notes, a quote dragged to a note — except Link, which joins the texts themselves. Its anchor is in the core layer (`Source.layer` = "core", `lib/anchors/layer.ts`): the offsets and the quote are the core's words, the server resolves it against the cores the reader sees (`layerBlocks`, `coreBlocks`), and a tool reads the core words with the cores around them as context (the cached prefix stays the whole document). The core anchors heal against the current cores on every render (`resolveDocumentSources` with layer "core") and paint on the cores alone (`coreHighlights`); the whole text's healing and a block edit's remap never touch them. A core rewritten after its block changed keeps an anchor whose quote it still holds; else the anchor orphans, quote kept. In the reader a core anchor's block id is its core key (`core:<blockId>`, `lib/anchors/core-key.ts`), so marks, cards, and local marks work as on any block; the key turns back into the block id and the layer on the way to the server. The Annotations tab and the annotations full page list one set at a time under a Whole text | Collapsed switch (`layer-switch.tsx`), shown once a core annotation exists; in the reader the tab follows the article's view. A jump to a core annotation shows its block's core.

## 29. Blank documents: the page editor

A blank document (made with Blank document, §15) is written in Unitos the way a Google Doc is written: a title row, Google Docs' toolbar, and a white page on a gray canvas, one continuous rich text with nothing to click into. It is not a list of blocks. The Unitos layer sits on top of it: the selection toolbar and every tool on it, the cards, the marks of notes and annotations, and the notes tray work on its text as they work on an article's.

- **The document.** `Document.richText` is the rich text — Tiptap (ProseMirror) JSON, the source of truth for its words and formatting — and `Document.richTextRev` its revision; `Document.pageSetup` is its page (`{pageless, width, height, margins, color}` in points; null = Letter, 1 in margins, pages, white). A document with rich text is a blank document; every other document has none. The node and mark names, the request schema, and the sanitizer live in `lib/docs/schema.ts`: a save keeps only known nodes and marks, and every attribute that reaches a style, an href, or a src is checked by name (a color is a hex or an rgb, a face is a plain name in any script, a link is http(s), mailto, tel, or a path).
- **The paragraph index.** The document's Block rows are derived from its rich text on every save (`deriveBlocks`, `lib/docs/blocks.ts`, the same function in the editor and on the server): one row per node a reader can select — a paragraph, a heading, a list item's paragraph, a table cell's paragraph, a code block — plus an image (FIGURE) and a horizontal line (SEPARATOR). A row's id is the node's `blockId`, its text the node's words as if every suggestion were accepted (a line break is "\n"): the words a suggestion adds count, the words it removes and the zero-width spaces of a suggested break do not, and a block a suggestion removes has no row. Its html is the heading level and the layout tokens (`center`, `quote`), its styles and links the node's marks. Every node the index reads carries a `blockId`, drawn as `data-block-id`; a split, a paste, or a duplicate gets a fresh one, and pasted content never keeps the ids it came with. An equation on its own line is an EQUATION row holding its TeX. A smart chip's words are its label; an inline equation, a footnote's number, and a bookmark add no words; the footnotes' paragraphs are indexed. So an anchor captured in the editor (§5) resolves against the rows, and every AI tool, the digest, Stitch, search, and export read a blank document like any other. Accepting a suggestion leaves the rows as they are; rejecting one changes them, and the sync moves the anchors.
- **Saving** (`components/docs/use-docs-save.ts`), the way Google Docs saves: no Save button. A change marks the document unsaved; a pause of 700 ms, or 3 s of steady typing, sends the whole rich text with the revision it started from (`PUT /api/documents/[documentId]/rich-text {richText, rev}`, editor). One save runs at a time; steady typing saves once per 3 s, counted from the start of the last save. A save from an older revision answers 409 with the stored copy, and the editor lays its own changes since its last save over it (`mergeRichText`, `lib/docs/merge.ts`) and saves again 700 ms later, also right after a lost connection. Nodes pair by the first blockId inside them, at every level, compared as key-sorted JSON in the editor's form: two list items or two table cells are two changes, and two changes to one paragraph both stand when they touch different words; else the copy on screen wins that paragraph. A new node the stored copy already holds (a save whose answer was lost) is not added twice. A new node whose id another document holds answers 409 with the ids, and the editor gives those nodes fresh ids. A lost connection retries with a growing wait of up to 10 s, and at once when the browser is back online; typing in the meantime sends no more saves. Leaving with unsaved changes tries one last save, which fails quietly offline, and asks the browser to warn. A save whose text is the stored copy again (typed and taken back) sends nothing. The title row shows the state: Saving…, then Saved to Unitos for 3 s, the cloud with a check, Trying to connect…. One save runs at a time: every caller waits out the save that runs. A newer stored copy that arrives with the page (another person's save, a server-side edit; the sync refreshes the page while the caret is in the page editor, but not for the page's own save when it was the only change and lost or found no mark: the save's answer carries the project's new revision) replaces the one on screen when nothing waits to be saved; otherwise the next save merges. - **The sync** (`syncRichText`, `lib/docs/sync.ts`). One save runs under a lock on the document's row: it derives the index, creates, updates, and deletes rows, reorders them in one statement, clears the search vector of every paragraph whose words changed, and stores the rich text with its revision bumped. The anchors on paragraphs whose words changed or left move with their words. The paragraphs that changed or left between two unchanged ones form a run — each paragraph its own run when none split, joined, came, or went, so edits in two paragraphs never reach each other's marks; the run's words before and after the save differ in one stretch. A mark outside the stretch moves exactly: into the new paragraph after an Enter, into the joined one after a Backspace. Typing right before or after a mark stays outside it. A change inside a mark grows or shrinks it; an Enter inside it cuts it, it keeps the larger part, and it covers its quote again when the Enter is taken back. A change that takes one end of a mark and words outside it leaves the rest of the mark. A change of letter case alone (Capitalization) keeps every mark on its letters. A mark inside a longer stretch is found by its quote in the run, as quoted or in another case, else by its paragraph's word diff (§5), else by its quote across the document. It orphans only when its words are gone. An orphaned mark on a paragraph the save changed or brought back looks for its quote again, so Ctrl+Z after a deletion brings the mark back with the next save. Link ends (§13) move the same way. The history (§12) gets one row per paragraph added, removed, retyped, or restyled; typing merges into the account's last row for the paragraph while it is under 10 minutes old, and a paragraph added and removed inside that window leaves no row.

The editor runs no Tiptap input rule and only Link's paste rule: Docs' own rules are the only ones.
- **The paragraph mark.** A style pressed at a paragraph's end, or set on a selection that reaches the end, styles the paragraph mark (`markStyle`). An empty line types in it, and a list line's bullet or number is drawn in it.
- **Paste** keeps the formatting it can map and never turns Markdown into formatting; an address becomes a link. Plain text becomes one paragraph per line in the style at the caret. Ctrl+Shift+V pastes plain text. An image pasted or dropped is uploaded (Images). Pasted HTML keeps only what a save keeps (`pastedHtml`, `typing/paste.ts`): a color becomes #rrggbb (a see-through one drawn over white; Word's highlight too), a size whole or half points (px × 0.75; em, rem, and % against 11 pt; 1 to 400), and what cannot be read goes, so the page never shows what a save drops. A table cell keeps its background. Word's list paragraphs (`mso-list:lN levelM`) become list items at level M, without the marker Word writes before each: a marker in Symbol or Wingdings, "o", or one character that is not a letter or a digit means bullets, any other numbers. A Google Docs checklist line becomes a checklist line, ticked or not, without its checkbox picture; a ticked line's strikethrough is the checklist's, not the words'. Its pictures are copied into Unitos: a data: picture shows at once and uploads, a remote one uploads when the browser may read it and else keeps its address, and each image takes the stored address when its upload ends, not as an undo step of its own; a data: picture the server refuses goes, with the reason, and a picture no save keeps (file:, cid:) goes. A file dropped on the page editor never becomes a project document (`components/reader/use-page-file-drop.ts`): an image dropped on the text goes into the text only, and the rest of the page editor refuses files. With Markdown on, the right-click menu has Paste from Markdown and Copy as Markdown. A project document's address pasted on its own shows "Tab to replace" under it (`typing/url-chip.ts`): Tab puts the document's chip in its place, its own undo step; moving the caret or typing leaves the link.
- **Capitalization** (Format > Text; `setCase`, `typing/format.ts`): lowercase, UPPERCASE, and Title Case rewrite the selected letters. Each run keeps its marks, each changed letter is its own step so the Unitos marks keep their ends, and the change is one undo step. Title Case gives each word a capital first letter and small letters after it, by Docs' words (don't, well-known); a word the selection starts inside goes on.
- **Find** (Ctrl+F): a bar at the top right showing "N of M"; every result light green, the current one green; Esc, from anywhere in the bar, closes it and leaves the result selected. **Find and replace** (Ctrl+H, ⌘⇧H): Match case, regular expressions, Ignore diacritics; Replace all is one undo step; in Suggesting mode each match is a suggestion of its own. A straight quote finds curly ones, and ... finds ….
- **Also:** Keyboard shortcuts (Ctrl+/), voice typing (Ctrl+Shift+S, the browser's speech recognition), non-printing characters (Ctrl+Shift+P), and Spelling and grammar check (Ctrl+Alt+X or F7: the browser's underlines on or off, drawn in Docs' red and blue).
- **Spelling suggestions** (`typing/spelling.ts`). The browser draws the red underline, in the reader's languages. Unitos adds what the page cannot ask the browser for, in English: nspell with the English Hunspell dictionary (`public/spelling`), loaded on the first right-click on a word, Ctrl+', or Ctrl+; — never with the page. It reads words of two or more Latin letters, apostrophes inside ("don't"), and skips code and any stretch with a digit, "@", "/", "\", "_", or a dot between letters (an address, a number, a file name). The right-click menu's spelling suggestions come two neighboring letters swapped first, then those that keep the first letter. Ctrl+' and Ctrl+; select the next and the previous misspelled English word from the selection's start and scroll it into view; a toast says when there is none ("No next misspelling"). Dictionary (Tools, Ctrl+Shift+Y) opens Explain on the selected words, or on the word at the caret.
- **Navigation keys** (`typing/navigate.ts`), Google Docs' chords: hold Ctrl+Alt (Ctrl+⌘ on a Mac), press a key, then a second key within a second; any other key, or letting go of a held key, cancels the chord, and a character typed with AltGr is never taken. N (next) or P (previous), then H (a heading or the Title), 1–6 (a heading of that level), G (an image, selected), O (a list), I (an item of the list the caret is in), L (a link), B (a bookmark), F (a footnote number), T (a table; Google's Ctrl+Alt+Shift+N or P, then T, too), C (a comment), or U (a suggestion; its card opens) puts the caret at its start and scrolls it into view; a toast says when there is none ("No next heading"). U then A is Select none: the selection collapses to where the caret stands. Every command whose shortcut is a chord runs from the same reader: O then H or F (the header or the footer), O then U (Review suggested edits), Ctrl+Alt+Shift+W then M (Minimize comments). The keyboard shortcuts dialog lists the chords under Navigation, Comments, Tools, and Editing, written "Ctrl+Alt+N H" ("⌘+Ctrl+N H" on a Mac).

- **Links** (`components/docs/link-dialog.tsx`, `insert/links.ts`). Insert link (Ctrl+K) opens a box under the selection: "Search or paste a link" and Apply. A Text field shows above it when no words are selected or a link is edited. The field takes the keys at once; Escape or a press outside closes the box. Keyboard focus in the box is a 2 px blue ring, and Tab stays in the box. "example.com" gains https://, an email mailto:. The typed words, or the selected ones, list the project's documents and this document's headings that match. Headings and bookmarks lists every heading and bookmark. A link to a heading is `#heading=<blockId>`, to a bookmark `#bookmark=<id>`; opening one, or the document's address with one, scrolls to the heading or the bookmark and puts the caret there. A link to a project document opens it in this tab. A caret in a link shows its bubble: the title (the document's, the heading's, or the address), the site, Copy link, Edit link, Remove link. A pasted address of a project document offers Replace URL: Chip or Link. Alt+Enter and Ctrl+click open the link.
- **The "@" menu** (`components/docs/insert/at-menu.tsx`, `at-plugin.ts`), Google Docs' insert menu. "@" at a line's start, or after a space, a tab, "(" or "[", opens it under the "@"; the "@" and the words after it stay in the text as the query, and a gray (#80868b) "Search menu" shows until the first letter. An empty line with the caret reads "Type @ to insert" (#70757a); a press on it opens the menu. Sections: People (the project's collaborators; "@me"), Smart chips, Building blocks, Files, Lists, Media, Headings, Tables, Page components, More; Dates and Emojis join while searching. The words filter every section, in bold where they match; a section shows a few rows and › opens all of them. ↑/↓ move, Enter or Tab chooses, → and ← open and leave a section, Escape closes and keeps the words. A choice replaces the "@" and its words with the item, or opens its picker there: a date, a dropdown, the table grid, emoji, image sources, the table of contents' styles, a code block's language. ":" and letters open the menu with emoji only, unless Preferences turns off Insert emojis using the colon character.
- **Smart chips** (`insert/chips.ts`, `insert/chip-cards.tsx`): a date, a person, a project document, or a dropdown, each an atom that draws its label. A date is a calendar day in Google Docs' four formats, with or without a time, in the language of the person who inserted it. Resting on a chip or selecting it shows its card: a date's day, how far it is from today, and its formats; a person's badge; a document's title, which opens it. A press on a dropdown chip lists its options; the Dropdown options dialog saves onto every chip of that dropdown. Building blocks: Meeting notes, Email draft, Product roadmap, Review tracker, Task tracker.
- **The right-click menu** (`insert/context-menu.tsx`), drawn with the toolbar's menus: Cut, Copy, Paste, Paste without formatting, Delete (with Markdown on, Copy as Markdown and Paste from Markdown too); the object's items (an image's, a table of contents', a table's); Comment, Add to notes, Explain, Ask the assistant — the Unitos tools, which open on the selection as from the selection toolbar (the `docs:unitos-tool` event); Open, Edit, and Remove link, or Insert link; Copy heading link on a heading; Restart numbering and Continue previous numbering on a line of a numbered list; Format options ▸ (Select all matching text, Update 'style' to match), then Clear formatting. On a misspelled English word, while the spelling check is on, the menu starts with up to five spelling suggestions and a separator; a click puts the spelling suggestion in the word's place, with the word's marks, one undo step. Shift+right-click keeps the browser's menu, and with it the browser's spelling suggestions in the reader's languages. Shift+F10, Ctrl+Shift+X, and Ctrl+Shift+\ open this menu from the keys. When the browser keeps the clipboard, a dialog names Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X, and Ctrl+V.
- **Tables** (`insert/table.ts`, `insert/table-controls.tsx`): inserted from the grid, up to 20 × 20. A cell stores its background, each side's border ("1 solid #000000"), its vertical alignment, and its padding; a row its minimum height and whether it is a pinned header row; the table its alignment and indent. Hover pills drag, pin, sort, and insert rows and columns; a row's bottom line drags its height. The ▾ at the top right of the caret's cell (or the selected cells) picks which borders change — all, inner, outer, or one line — and draws them blue until the selection moves; its buttons and the toolbar's then change only those. Colors come from the toolbar's color menu, custom colors included. Split cell asks for columns and rows. Table options is the side panel. Its lengths are in inches (centimeters in Chinese), as in Image options. Sorting keeps pinned rows on top.
- **Images** (`insert/image.ts`, `insert/image-controls.tsx`): a new image keeps its ratio and fits the text's width. Selected, it shows the blue frame, eight handles, and the rotation handle; a double press crops. The toolbar under it: the five layouts (pageless takes In line only), the margin, Crop, border color, weight, and dash, Replace image, Reset image, More. The Image options panel: Size & rotation, Text wrapping, Recolor, Adjustments, Alt text (Ctrl+Alt+Y). Google Docs' keys resize and turn the image. An image pasted, dropped, chosen, or given by URL goes in through one path (`insertImage`, `insertImageFiles`, `typing/paste.ts`), on its own line after the paragraph that holds the caret (or the drop point), or in place of an empty line; one pasted over a selection replaces it.
- **More objects**: footnotes (Ctrl+Alt+F: the number is drawn as a plain superscript in the text's color, and a click beside it puts the caret there, never selecting it; the words stand at the foot of the number's page, or at the document's end in pageless), bookmarks (a blue ribbon), equations (KaTeX, typed in the equation box with the five symbol menus), a table of contents (plain, dotted, or links, with Update and an options panel for the style and the heading levels), special characters (the dialog stays open), emoji (a picker of nine groups), and Select all matching text (one selection of many ranges).

- **Word count** (Ctrl+Shift+C, `components/docs/word-count.tsx`): Pages, Words, Characters, and Characters excluding spaces, of the selection out of the whole when text is selected, with Cancel and OK. A word is a run of word characters; apostrophes, hyphens, and dashes never split one, punctuation does, and a CJK run counts as one. "Display word count while typing" keeps a counter at the bottom left of the page area ("N words", "S words out of T"). The toolbar's dialogs have Docs' dialog buttons, 36 px with 4 px corners, over a black scrim at 60%. A dialog of fields has Cancel and its primary button (OK, Apply, Split, Insert image); Enter in a field presses the primary, and does nothing while it is off.

- **The Unitos layer.** The reader interactions (reader-interactions.tsx) wrap the editor as they wrap an article, and every tool, card, and note works as on any document. A selection opens the text toolbar (§6): a mouse selection when the button comes up, a keyboard selection once Shift, Ctrl, or Cmd is let go. A caret moved with the keys closes it, typing closes it, and Escape closes it and keeps the selection. The passage is read from the editor's document (`components/docs/layer/anchor.ts`): one segment per paragraph, offsets as the paragraph index counts them; images and equations are left out, and the toolbar says so. Add comment (the toolbar's button, Ctrl+Alt+M) opens the Comment tool on the selection, or on the word the caret stands in; a comment is an annotation. When the comment field closes, posted or cancelled, the page takes the focus back with the caret after the commented words. The marks of notes, annotations, links, and extractions paint over the rich text as decorations (`components/docs/annotation-marks.tsx`), one per stretch of words under the same marks, as the reader draws them: a link wins, else the smallest anchor names the mark. Marks keep the kind colors (§6). A mark's chips sit outside the document (`data-anchor-skip`) and take no room in the line: right of the text column (in a table, at the cell's right edge), level with the line the mark ends on, so a new mark never moves the words. A click on a mark or a chip opens what it opens in the reader, also while the selection still holds its words: the caret goes to the click; a click on linked words opens the annotation or note under the link. A jump flashes the words as a decoration. The selection under the toolbar is the page's own (see The caret): an article's clay tint (§6) is not painted on the page, so opening the toolbar never repaints the page. A repaint happens when the highlights change in content, never for a re-render — a repaint puts the editor's selection back into the page — and only while the screen holds the stored copy the highlights' offsets were read from (nothing waits to be saved, the page's revision caught up); meanwhile the painted marks move with the typing. A save that lost a mark or found one again asks for the stored highlights at once. Typing saves before an anchor is stored, a plan runs, a dragged passage lands, or the voice command reads the document (`components/docs/layer/flush.ts`). A passage dragged onto a note in the notes tray lands in it as a quote (§6); dragged inside the page it moves. A blank document has no reading mode and no block edit mode, no Contents menu, no Collapse, and no translation bar, in a split pane too; Extract sits at the toolbar's right end. A viewer sees the page and the toolbar with the editing controls off.
- **The margin** (`components/docs/layer/margin.ts`), as in Google Docs. The toolbar sits 8 px right of the page's right edge, its first row level with the selection's first line, and fades in over 250 ms. With no room there it sits over the page's right margin, never over the text; with no room at all, under the words. The comment's field and the assistant's box widen it from 176 to 248 px: when the wider toolbar would cover the text, the page moves left for it as for a card; with no room even then, the toolbar keeps 176 px. Cards open in one column beside the page, each level with its words or below the card above: 282 px wide (260 at the least), 12 px corners, #F0F4F9 at rest, #E9EEF6 under the pointer, white with a shadow while in use; the border keeps the kind color. The page moves left to make room (`--docs-shift` on the pane) and moves back once no card and no toolbar is open and the document holds no suggestion, never under a held press. A log card never moves the page. With no room for a card even with the page at the canvas's left edge, and in a split pane, cards collapse to their chips and open under their words. The title row and the toolbar stay above the cards.
- **Comments** (`components/docs/layer/comment-card.tsx`), Google Docs' comment card. A click on a comment's words or its icon opens its card in the margin as the card in use: white and lifted, with Resolve (✓) and More options (⋮: Edit, Delete, Get link to this comment) shown, while the caret stays in the page. It shows the author's badge (32 px), name, and time, then the comment; without sign-in the author is the local reader, named "You" as the history names them. The comment's replies (§12) sit under it, with Reply. Resolve shows while a reply is open: it closes the open replies, which move under Resolved, and the card. Edit turns the comment into a field with Cancel and Save (Ctrl+Enter saves, Escape cancels). Get link to this comment copies the comment's address in the reader (§6); the address opens the page on the card. The card reads its author, time, and replies from the note's routes (`GET /api/notes/[noteId]/edits`, `GET /api/replies?noteId=`). On the focused card, Google's keys: R reply, J the next comment, K the previous one, E resolve, U back to the text; the keyboard shortcuts dialog lists them under Comments. A press anywhere else, or Escape, closes the card.
- **View > Comments** (Search the menus). Show all comments (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+A, or hold Ctrl+Alt+Shift and press W then E) opens the Annotations tab (the `dissect:show-annotations` event), the list of every comment with its replies. Minimize comments (hold Ctrl+Alt+Shift, press W then M) shows each comment as its icon at the end of its words, and a click opens its card. Hide comments (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+J) unpaints the comments and their icons until Minimize comments, Show all comments, or a new comment. Viewing mode hides the comments; Editing shows them again.
- **Suggesting mode** (`components/docs/ext/suggest.ts`, `components/docs/suggest/`), Google Docs' own, on `@handlewithcare/prosemirror-suggest-changes`. The mode menu has Editing, Suggesting ("Edits become suggestions"), and Viewing; Ctrl+Alt+Shift+X switches to Suggesting, and the pill turns blue. Suggesting is for editors: a viewer cannot suggest. In Suggesting mode every edit becomes a suggestion, and the whole toolbar stays: added words draw in the author's person color, underlined on a light tint of it; removed words stay, struck through in that color; a format change (a mark on words; a paragraph's style, alignment, spacing, or indent) takes effect and draws a dotted line; a suggested paragraph break shows a ¶. A suggestion is the `insertion`, `deletion`, and `modification` marks under one id, `<account id>.<ms>` (`suggestionAuthor`, `suggestionTime` in `lib/docs/schema.ts`, beside the mark names and the zero-width space that holds a suggested break), so it names its author and time with nothing more stored; words typed next to another person's suggestion get their own id. Delete strikes the letter after the caret and the caret moves past it, press after press, as Backspace strikes the letter before it. Backspace or Delete on a suggested paragraph break takes the break back: the lines join, and Enter then Backspace leaves nothing behind. A command that puts the caret in what it adds (a table's first cell, a footnote, a new row, a new list line) puts it there in Suggesting mode too. A change of letter case is one suggestion: the stretch it changes in each paragraph, replaced. A block's id is no formatting: its change is never a suggestion. The rich text saves the marks (`lib/docs/schema.ts` keeps a suggestion's id and checks a format change's values as the mark, the attribute, or the node type that rejecting it puts back). The paragraph index reads the text as if every suggestion were accepted (The paragraph index), so notes, Explain, search, and Stitch read "Bring the slides", never "Bring the reportslides"; the editor's anchors count the same way, a mark stops short of struck words at its ends, a selection only in struck words gives no quote, and a style from the assistant leaves removed words as they are. Clear formatting, Paint format, and Repeat leave the suggestion marks alone. Every suggestion has its card in the margin, the comment card's look, in one column beside the page (`suggest/layer.tsx`, placed with `layer/margin.ts`): each card level with its words or pushed clear of its neighbors, never over one. The card of the suggestion the caret stands in is whole, white and lifted, and level with its words; the cards above it move up and the cards below it move down: the author's badge, name, and time; Add: "…", Delete: "…", Replace: "…" with "…", Move: "…", or Format: …; and, for an editor, Accept suggestion (✓) and Reject suggestion (✕), each one undo step. Every other card is one line, the author and the change; a press on it selects the suggestion and opens its card. With no room beside the page, only the open card shows, under its words. A card reads a line break as ↵, a paragraph break as ¶, and a checklist tick as Format: Checked or Unchecked. A suggestion that takes whole blocks out and puts the same words back draws them once, the removed copy hidden: a list changed where its words stand (a list toggled, a bullet removed, bullets made numbers) reads Format: Bulleted list, Numbered list, Checklist, or remove bulleted list and draws as a format change; blocks moved (Ctrl+Shift+↑/↓) read Move: "…". Review suggested edits (Search the menus; hold Ctrl+Alt, press O then U) opens a box under the toolbar: the number of suggestions, the previous and the next one, Accept all, and Reject all; Accept all suggestions and Reject all suggestions are commands too. Viewing mode shows the document without its suggestions, and a copy made there (Ctrl+C) holds what it shows.
